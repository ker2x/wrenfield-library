# The Wrenfield Library — Licence of Circulation

### Made at the issue of the year 1504, in the Registry's own hand, and entered in no catalogue

- **The instrument.** It standeth in the Registry's own series, uncatalogued. It is no volume, nor
  holding, nor one of the nine-and-forty, and is not to be counted with them.
- **The giver.** The Wrenfield Library of the University of Alderwick, for the shelf as it standeth at
  this issue.
- **The taker.** Every reader, whether his name be entered in the Register of Readers or not. Nor is any
  fee to be asked of him for this instrument, nor any form of him required.
- **The term.** Perpetual, and not to be recalled; nor shall it be withdrawn from him that hath kept a
  volume past the quarter-day, though the fine for the same be standing against him.
- **The copies.** So many as thou wilt take. None is to be returned, and none is to be asked after.
- **Where it is set up.** In each of the three reading rooms. A fourth was struck in 1487 for a fourth
  room, and lieth at the desk, the room not having received it.

Forasmuch as divers persons have taken, and do daily take, copies of the volumes of this shelf, and no man
hath said upon what terms: be it known unto all such as take up a volume of this shelf — whether they read
it, or copy it, or cut it about, or set it to music, or lay it up in a cupboard — that the terms upon
which it is given are these and none other, and that they are drawn in the register of the old line,
wherein the Registry keepeth its instruments, and which the Registry hath been prayed to modernise four
times, to wit in 1431, in 1466, in 1491 and in 1503, and hath refused it four times, the meaning of the
words being the whole of the grant.

1. **Of copying.** Thou shalt copy, in whole or in part, by what hand and by what means soever, and shalt
   not be hindered in the copying; nor shall any fee be asked of thee for the taking of a copy, nor any
   form required of thee ere it be taken.

2. **Of the naming.** Thou shalt name the Library, and the hand that wrote the same, and the call number
   whereunder the volume standeth, wheresoever thou carriest it; and if thou hast altered the work, thou
   shalt say plainly that thou hast altered it, and shalt not father thy alterations upon the Library, nor
   upon the hand that wrote.

3. **Of further conditions.** Thou shalt not add terms of thine own, nor wrap the work about with any
   further condition, nor shut by a side door that which this instrument leaveth open by the front. The
   grant is given whole, or it is not given at all.

4. **Of that which thou passest on.** Thou shalt set thy copies, and whatsoever thou makest out of the
   work, under this same licence, to the end that what is given freely remain free in thy hands as in
   ours.

5. **Of the Library's name.** Thou shalt not say, nor suffer it to be supposed, that the Library hath
   allowed thee, thy use, or thy work, nor that thou speakest with the Registry's warrant. The Library
   hath allowed nothing since the year 1147, and is not minded to begin.

6. **Of honour.** The honour of the author is not the Registry's to give away. It abideth with the hand
   that wrote; this instrument neither taketh it nor granteth it.

7. **Of the words.** Thou shalt not shorten this instrument for a bill, nor paraphrase it for a colophon,
   nor keep the sense of it and let the words go. A paraphrase is not the licence: the Registry hath
   refused one in place of the instrument four times, and will refuse it a fifth.

8. **Of the dark hours.** A copy taken after the shutting of the reading rooms is a copy notwithstanding,
   and is not to be refused on that account, nor stamped, the Registry being gone home.

9. **Of the crate.** The crate behind the desk is not upon the shelf, and these terms reach it not until
   it be catalogued, which it hath not been since 1494.

*In witness whereof the Registry setteth its hand, twice, as is proper. The second stamp is the stamp
that standeth.*

*Endorsed upon the back, in a later hand:* Copying is permitted after the manner aforesaid; the Registry
is not to be prayed again. — *F. Oll, Under-Librarian*
