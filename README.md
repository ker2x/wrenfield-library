# book/ — the Wrenfield Library

A fictional scholar library: guides, manuals and theses from the **University of Alderwick**,
written as deadpan academic pastiche. Ridiculous subjects, entirely straight faces.

## Where to start

- **`CATALOG.md`** — the library's card catalogue: reading-room rules, borrowing terms, the full
  shelf-list with call numbers, and the outstanding matters (the missing Form 7B, the crate that
  falls due in 1505, the undergraduate still in transit since 1461).
- **`volumes/`** — all forty-nine holdings, one Markdown file each.
- **`_CANON.md`** — the world bible: cast, faculties, classes, house style, and the brief for every
  holding, including the two formats added in the 1504 extension.

## The holdings, by class

The shelf-list, with call numbers and condition notes, is in **`CATALOG.md`**. In summary:

**Full-length standard.** Volumes run **3,000–5,000 words of body** (memoranda and histories may run
longer), with the front matter, abstract, references and apparatus capped at a fifth of the file, and
every volume required to carry a case told in full, two tables of data, a document quoted at length,
an objection and a reply, a procedure, and a named failure with a fee attached. The first section of
every volume must state plainly **what the subject is** — what it is, what it is not, what it is for,
and what it feels like to do — with any private vocabulary glossed in a table at the front. The first
issue — 900–1300 words, mostly scaffolding — was withdrawn in 1504 and **all forty-nine holdings have
now been reissued** at this standard: the thirty-five faculty volumes, the seven manuscripts (**36–42**)
and the seven Foundation texts (**43–49**). See `_CANON.md` §4.2–4.4 and §8.

## The library's furniture

Each volume carries the same apparatus: a front-matter block (call number, author, degree, advisor,
imprint, condition), an **Abstract (as filed)**, four to six numbered sections, at least one table
of invented specifics, **References & Shelf-mates** (other volumes in this library, by call
number), and **Apparatus** — the violet Under-Librarian's stamp, a line of pencil marginalia, an
errata slip, an examiner's note. The marginalia is usually the best joke in the volume.

Recurring fixtures, for orientation:

- **Form 7B**, *Declaration of Non-Interference with Time* — required with every thesis, present in
  none, and obtainable only from a reading room that is not in the building.
- **Professor Emeritus Quillan Ashgrove-Vane**, who second-reads most of these works and whose
  entire written comment is *"Broadly."*
- **The Under-Librarian, Mr. Fenwick Oll**, who stamps everything, opens windows the pollen faculty
  needs closed, and has a crate behind his desk that has awaited cataloguing since 1494.
- **Percival Nott**, in the third-floor mirror since 1461, still in transit, still accruing fines.
- **The Harmless Jurisdiction Question** — whether a jinx that harms no one is a curse; the
  faculties of JM and HC have disputed it since 1402.
- **The fourth reading room** — in the plans since 1223, filed under R in the reordering of the
  Registry on the 6th of March 1487, and not in the building since. Its lamp is still trimmed, the
  draught in the corridor that replaced it is not to be stopped, and *Form 7B for Dummies* — the
  Library's only copy, and the reason nobody knows how to complete the form — went with it. See
  `book/CATALOG.md` under *Holdings Recorded and Not Held*, and `volumes/16_on-the-mis-filing-of-the-fourth-reading-room.md`.

## Adding to the library

`_CANON.md` is the world bible and house style — the cast, the faculties, the call-number scheme,
the required apparatus, and the brief for every volume. Read it before writing a new holding, then
add the file to `volumes/`, a row to the shelf-list in `CATALOG.md`, and a line to the accession
register. Keep the running jokes straight: the crate, the windows, the fines, and Form 7B.
