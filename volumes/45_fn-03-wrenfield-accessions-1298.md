# Wrenfield Accessions 1298–1302

### A fragment of the Accessions register, five leaves of a quire of sixteen, in two hands, with the chapter's table of accession fees and the receipt of the 27th of November 1302

**Call number:** FN 3.1 / — 1298
**Class:** FN — The Foundation Collection
**Text:** English and Latin, entries in the register hand of the Library; **the hand changes after the third entry**, without a note of any kind
**Date:** Entries of March 1298 to November 1302; the first leaf is an earlier fragment, and the Library cannot say of what
**Custody:** Kept in the Upper Crypt with the Chapter chests; not to be produced to any person named in it as a donor without the Deputy Under-Librarian's written leave
**Collation:** 5 leaves, the third cut down at the foot; two sewing shadows showing four leaves lost before the fragment
**Condition:** The cut at the foot of the third leaf has taken the lower half of one entry; water-staining along the fore-edge in the pattern of a thumb; the mark of a wet cup on the fifth leaf, made by someone reading

## The Library's Note

The text is the Library's own register of what it received, and of the fees it took for receiving it: sixteen entries from March 1298 to November 1302, entered in a ruled book of which five leaves survive, and closed by the receipt of the Wrenfield bequest in the fourth hundred of books. It is not a catalogue; it is the record of arrival, and it is the earliest document in the Library's own hand. The fragment was found in 1494 in the chest behind the counter, in the course of an inventory that was never completed, and was accessioned at once and shelved as a Foundation text on the strength of its date alone. The Library can verify five leaves, two hands, and the fact that the right-hand column was ruled before the entries were made and does not fit them. It cannot verify the fourth leaf, the one cut down, whose remaining text refers to a volume as being "at 44.9, where it is shorter", and it declines to investigate, the matter being one in which the Library is the party most nearly concerned. The Library further declines to explain the change of hand. The volume to which the entry of the 11th of March 1301 refers is elsewhere in this catalogue: it is catalogued under DP 44.9, and it is not held. No Form 7B is filed with this fragment; the form governs theses.

## The receipt of the 27th of November 1302

The fifth leaf carries the only document in the fragment, entered in the register's own hand and subscribed by the clerk:

> *Be it known that on the 27th day of November in the year 1302 the Library received of the estate of the Margrave Wrenfield, by the hand of the Bursar of the University, four hundred books, being the whole of the bequest and no part withheld, with one chest for their carriage and straw in the chest. Of these, the first hundred is canon law, the second is sermons, the third is medicine and the art, and the fourth is sermons also, to the number of two hundred quires. And the Library has counted the first hundred twice, and the second twice, and the third twice, and the fourth twice, and finds in each count the same number, whereof the fourth count disagrees with the third and is entered as the count that stands. And the fee of two marks was paid by the Bursar in the presence of the Chapter, and the straw was not paid for and is retained. Witnessed by W. de Aldre, clerk, who wrote this with his own hand, the day and year above written.*

## The accessions procedure, in the Library's own words

1. A thing brought to the Library is set on the counter and not on the press, the press being for books to be read.
2. The keeper of the counter compares the thing with the donor's word, and where the two disagree he enters both.
3. The keeper enters the date, the thing, the donor, and the mark, in that order, and does not enter his opinion.
4. The fee is stated from the Chapter's table and is paid before the mark is entered; where the fee is not paid, the mark is entered as *lapsed*.
5. The thing is chained, or not chained, at the Library's charge, and the chain is entered with the thing.
6. The keeper reports the accession to the Chapter at the next sitting, and reads the entry aloud.
7. The Chapter may strike an entry out; an entry not struck out stands, whether the thing is present or not.
8. A thing accessioned and thereafter absent is not struck out, but is reported, and the report is entered in the margin.

## The matter of the octavo, 1301

The volume accessioned on the 11th of March 1301 was an octavo, in a vellum wrapper, of self-help, given by one N. Nott, of this city, who paid the fee of six clipped bells and went away. It was catalogued on the 14th by the same hand that had accessioned it, under the mark DP 44.9. On the 19th the same hand reported it absent, and the report was entered in the margin and the entry was not struck out. The keeper of the counter, one A. Pen, was questioned at the Candlemas court of 1302, and deposed that he had seen the volume on the counter on the 11th, on the shelf on the 14th, and on neither thereafter; and that he had searched the press where shelf 44.9 then stood and found it shorter than its catalogue. He was fined one bell for a shelf untended, and paid it, and the fine stands at the foot of the leaf. The Library notes that the volume was accessioned, catalogued, reported absent, and never struck out, and that all four of these acts are in the register, in order, in the same hand.

## The depositor objected to, and the keeper's reply

Against the entry of the 14th of October 1299, in a hand that is not the register hand, though the Library does not say whose:

> *The bowl is mine and the Chapter's, and I gave it as the Chapter's, and the keeper has written it as mine, and has taken my fee for it. Let the entry be corrected, or let the fee be returned, and I will not ask twice.*

The reply is entered beneath, in the register hand, and is not signed:

> *The donor is the Chapter, and the Chapter is many, and the entry names the Chapter. The fee was paid by the Chapter's officer, who is the Chapter. Nothing is to be corrected, and the correction of a fee is not within the keeper's office.*

## Apparatus

*Under-Librarian's stamp, in violet:* Entered, and entered again. The Crypt is opened Tuesdays, ten to eleven. No person is to correct the fifth leaf with a sponge.

*Errata slip, pasted to the third leaf, printed at Alderwick in 1503:* **Correction.** The entry of the 11th of March 1301 is the fourth entry of the fragment and the nineteenth of the year. The Library regrets the ambiguity, and withdraws the phrase.

*Annotation, in a later hand, at the foot of the fourth leaf:* The wrapper of the volume entered on the 11th of March was of the same vellum as this quire, and the entry was therefore not made when the volume arrived, but when the leaf did.

*Annotation, in a later hand, at the head of the margin report of the 19th of March 1301, dated 1504:* The report is in the same hand and the same ink as the accession, and stands upon the same opening; it was therefore not written on the 19th, the ninth day, but at the making of the leaf. It is to be understood that the volume was never absent, but was entered as absent in the year of the bequest, so that shelf 44.9 should stand shorter than its catalogue by the act of the register and not by the act of any reader. The Library has accordingly stopped looking for it, and the fines of the reader who now holds it continue to accrue.

*Conservator's line:* Two sewing shadows before leaf one; the cut at leaf three is a knife-cut and not a tear; the cup-mark is to stay, being evidence of use.
