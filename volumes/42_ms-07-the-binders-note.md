# The Binder's Note
### The note of four lines left by Mr. Tobias Rell, binder, of the Aldermarket bindery, concerning *DO NOT READ*

**Call number:** MS 7.1 / R45 1487
**Hand:** Mr. Tobias Rell's own; the whole manuscript, apart from the Library's Note and the Apparatus, is four lines in his hand and is unsigned
**Class:** MS — Manuscripts & Personal Papers
**Dates of entries:** one entry, dated 9 April 1487
**Provenance:** Found in the waste of the bindery in 1488 and handed to the Library in 1504 by the binder's widow with a printed apology; she does not say whose it is
**Collation:** one leaf, folded once, written on the first page; watermark a bodkin
**Condition:** the fold is worn through at the head; pressed flat at some time, which has left the shadow of a clasp across the third line

## The Library's Note

This holding is a note of four lines, and the reader is owed the plain statement of what it is before the four lines are given. It is a note, not a letter and not a draft: one leaf, written on the first page by Mr. Tobias Rell, binder, of Aldermarket, dated the ninth of April 1487, unsigned, and complete as it stands. It is not the survivor of a longer sheet, and not the abstract of the apology of 1488; it is the whole of what he wrote at the time. What it records is an instruction given him by an author, his compliance with it, and his discovery that the instruction was two instructions which did not agree. The Library calls the holding short because it is short. The four lines occupy the upper third of the leaf and the remainder is blank, and the leaf is bound into boards of the Library's ordering with no other paper.

The note reads, entire:

> **9 April 1487.** The author instructed me to bind the book as written and to follow the printed contents.
> I did both.
> They are not the same thing.
> I have said so.

Four lines, of which the third is the note and the other three are the scaffold of it. The Library has twice been asked to expand this entry with the apology of 1488 and has declined, the apology being eleven pages long at TE 21.1, and its whole argument being visible here in nine seconds.

## Apparatus

*Under-Librarian's stamp, in violet:* The note is to be read at the counter and is not to be taken from the boards. The binder's firm is not to be written to again concerning the ordering of the held book, that question having been raised in 1488 and left where it lies.

*Conservator's note, 1504:* One leaf, sound. The fold is opened and closed at the head more often than below, which is where a thumb goes when a man does not intend to read the whole of it.

*Pencil note, in Mr. Rell's own hand, later:* I have seen the apology. It is not mine and it is longer than the book, which is the same thing said the way a printer would say it. The instruction was plain and I carried it out plainly. I was in the trade thirty-one years and had the one complaint, and the man who made it had not read the contents.
