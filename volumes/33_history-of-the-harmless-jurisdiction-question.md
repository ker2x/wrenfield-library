## Abstract (as filed)

This history sets out, from the record of the Faculty of Harmless & Decorative Curses, its dispute of one hundred and two years with the Faculty of Jinxcraft & Minor Maleficence: whether a jinx causing no harm is a curse, and which Faculty owns it. The chronology runs from the Instrument of Hiatus to the present year; the four findings are printed in full, of which two contradict each other and both remain in force. Form 7B, *Declaration of Non-Interference with Time*, is absent from the file, as from every file in this Library. The author, who chaired the Mowbray viva of 1496, claims no more for this volume than the record.

## 1. What the Harmless Jurisdiction Question Is

The **Harmless Jurisdiction Question** is a dispute between two Faculties of this University, now in its one hundred and third year, upon two connected points: whether a **jinx that causes no harm** is nevertheless a **curse**, and **which Faculty owns it** — Jinxcraft & Minor Maleficence, which licensed and taught the casting, or Harmless & Decorative Curses, which keeps the register of curses and the plates.

A **jinx** is a small deliberate maleficence: a named operation, cast in a settled form, whose intended effect is a minor and temporary inconvenience — a repeated yawn, a mislaid word, a shoe that finds every puddle in Bell Court. A **curse** is the *instrument* itself, made and named, whether or not it has discharged. A jinx **discharges** when the inconvenience occurs, and is **undischarged** when it does not.

The Question is not whether harmless magic exists; it plainly does. It is whether the *harmlessness* of a casting changes the *class* of the instrument for the licence, the fee and the discipline. Both Faculties agree the operations in dispute are the same operations, cast from the same register and taught from the same text; they disagree about the counter at which the paperwork is filed. Nothing turns on the spell; everything turns on the desk.

For a practitioner the Question decides three things. **Who may be licensed** — the register of jinx-workers is JM's and the licence to cast a decorative curse is HC's, and a practitioner holding one who operates under the other is unlicensed. **Who may be paid** — the fee schedules differ, and a fee taken under the wrong schedule is recoverable, as §7 shows. **Who may be disciplined** — and since both Faculties claim the power to discipline the same casting and neither the power to protect it, a practitioner caught by the Question may be disciplined twice for one act. What is **not** in dispute: that the jinx is the genus and the curse a species of it, agreed 1405; that either Faculty may hold a casting *sub spe*, agreed 1405; that the Question is jurisdictional and not moral, agreed 1403; and that Form 7B, *Declaration of Non-Interference with Time*, governs theses and not licences, and is missing from every thesis here in any case. Findings 1404 and 1462 contradict each other on the instrument, both remain in force, and both are printed in full below.

## 4. The 1404 Finding, in Full

The finding is entered in the HC register at HC 2.1 and in the EA register at 1404, Michaelmas term, on a case stated from this Faculty in the matter of a casting of the elder gate-curse form upon a gate in Cooper's Rents, the casting then forty-one days old and undischarged. The seal is appended and whole. It is printed entire, its spelling as entered.

> In the matter of a casting upon a gate, being of the harmless form, the Faculty of Harmless & Decorative Curses having stated a case and the Faculty of Jinxcraft & Minor Maleficence having answered, it is found: that jurisdiction in matters of maleficence follows the instrument and not the effect; that the instrument is the operation named, formed and entered in the register of the Faculty competent to receive it; that the effect, whether it come or not, is matter of fact and not of class; that a casting which does nothing is not therefore nothing, the register of this Faculty being full of such; that the question whether a jinx causing no harm is a curse is not answered here, no case upon a jinx having been stated, and no Faculty having asked it; and that the parties be at liberty to state a further case. Costs, four bells, to the clerk. Entered Michaelmas term. The Faculty having been asked to determine the class of the harmless casting declines for the present to do so, and will not entertain the like question again until the Faculties have agreed what it is they are asking.

The finding settles that a harmless *casting* may be an instrument, which was HC's whole case in 1404, and declines to class it. JM has relied on the final clause ever since.

## 5. The *Journal of Harmless Maleficence*, on Hiatus since 1402

Volume xi, the last issued, went to the binder in Fifthmonth 1402; in Sixthmonth the Instrument of Hiatus was sealed and the journal suspended *pending clarification*. It is preserved at **FN 7.1 / — 1402**, in the chest of the Concord in the Upper Crypt, indented at the foot, with two seals and a schedule of witnesses. Clause 6 is the sentence most often quoted, and the folds give way along it:

> The clarification in clause 3 shall be issued by the body issuing this hiatus, and by no other; and that the hiatus shall stand until the said clarification is issued.

The seal was the Journal's, and the body that issued the hiatus was therefore the Journal's editors, sitting as the Joint Standing Committee on Minor Maleficence. The Committee last sat on the fourteenth of June 1402 and was dissolved by the instrument that required it to meet, its dissolution being among the matters it was to have clarified. Volume xii is uncollated galley proofs in the Restricted Stack: the most requested item in the Stack and the second most returned, no reader having been permitted to fold it. The hiatus has been observed for one hundred and two years by a Faculty that has not once been able to read what it was asked to clarify.

## 6. The Casting of Aurelia Stape, 1499: the Question in Full Worked

The clearest instance of the Question working on a practitioner is the Stape file, kept by this Faculty since 1500 and never asked for. It shows the Question in three lines: a casting licensed by one Faculty, disciplined by the other, judged on a finding that names neither, and paid for by the practitioner.

**The practitioner.** Aurelia Stape, of Bell Court, licensed jinx-worker of the second grade since 1494, bond 12 marks, annual licence 1 mark 4 bells, insured at the Nine Cloisters office for 3 bells the quarter, and never the subject of a complaint.

**The casting and the licence.** Her client was a dyer of Bell Court, Werburgh Nunn, who wished her name kept out of a corn-factor's ledger. On the eleventh of Ninthmonth 1499 Stape cast *The Repeated Yawn* — the form printed at JM 6.1 / V35 1494, Appendix B — naming the corn-factor aloud, as the form requires, in the dyer's presence, and entering the casting in her day-book. The corn-factor did not yawn. The twenty-ninth syllable of the formula returned upon the speaker, and Stape yawned nine times in nine days, once while giving evidence, which the Registrar allowed her to finish. JM licensed the casting, and the licence is not in dispute: the register entry is in good order, witnessed, and stamped twice, Mr. Oll being in the habit of stamping anything presented at the counter twice, the second stamp being the one that counts.

**The discipline.** On the second of Fifthmonth 1500 the Faculty of Jinxcraft & Minor Maleficence found that the casting had not discharged, that a jinx producing no harm is not a curse, and that the casting fell within HC's remit. Stape was fined 4 marks for practising decorative maleficence without an HC licence and charged 2 marks 6 bells hearing costs.

**The appeal.** She appealed to the Registrary on the sixth of Fifthmonth, on Finding i of 1404: jurisdiction follows the instrument, and the instrument stood in the register of the Faculty competent to receive it, which on the register's face was JM. JM answered that Finding iii of 1462 sent the *undischarged* jinx to the Faculty that owns the instrument, and sent the file to HC, which holds the plates and returned it unopened, noting that the plates had come to HC under Finding iii, the transfer being what JM had disputed when it was made.

**The outcome.** On the fourth of Eleventhmonth 1500 the Registrary allowed the appeal in part: it found the casting a harmless casting within Finding i; declined to determine the class or the ownership of the instrument; reduced the fine by a jurisdictional discount of 2 marks, being the measure of the Faculty's doubt; and left the hearing costs of 2 marks 6 bells standing, a practitioner who reads both registers having been able to foresee the file. Stape paid 4 marks 6 bells, and her licence was re-entered with the adjective **unresolved**, which HC prints as the agreed form of words.

## 7. A Curse Struck Down on Jurisdictional Grounds: Corl, 1502

The named failure. **Dunstan Corl**, licensee of the first grade, Aldermarket, practised for eleven years a form of his own devising entered in the JM register as *the Aspirational*, a decorative curse cast upon a rival's work, intended to produce no effect until the rival prospers, and none then. He sold it as a harmless instrument at 8 marks the casting, schedule B, taken in advance, the practice being permitted by the second point of Finding iv. In Secondmonth 1502 he cast it upon the bell-foundry of one Silence Wren, a rival patron having purchased the casting, and certified to Wren that the instrument was harmless, harmless being the whole of what was promised. In Eleventhmonth Wren's foundry took the larger order, prospered, and suffered nothing at all. Wren, who had paid the 8 marks herself, brought the casting before the Registry and asked for her money.

The Auditor heard the matter on the twenty-ninth of Eleventhmonth 1502 and struck the curse down on jurisdictional grounds: the instrument, being incapable of effect, was a harmless casting within Finding i, its class and ownership were **not determinable**, and no fee could be assessed against it under either schedule; the 8 marks were recoverable as money had and received; and costs went to the client, 3 marks 4 bells, Corl's filing costs of 5 bells not recoverable, no schedule providing for them. Total loss 11 marks 9 bells, against a first-grade licence costing 2 marks 2 bells a year.

Corl was not disciplined, no Faculty having jurisdiction over an instrument of neither class. He did not renew his JM licence in 1503 and applied to HC in Fourthmonth 1503, which has no form for the application, the form having been withdrawn in 1462 with the plates. He is now a bell-founder, and has made a bell for the north range, where the draught is not to be stopped.

## 8. The Position of JM, Stated Fairly

The letter below stands in the deposited correspondence at Appendix D, fol. 218, answered at fol. 219. The position of that Faculty, stated by Dr. Corbin Vale at **JM 6.1 / V35 1494**, §4 and Appendix B, is best given in his own words.

> Dear Mistress Tarn. The Faculty of Harmless & Decorative Curses asks us to agree that the class of an instrument is fixed by the hand and the form, and not by what comes of it. We decline, and we decline with reasons. A jinx is defined by its aim: it is the maleficence of the small intention. Where nothing is intended, there is no jinx; where nothing follows, there is no harm; where there is no harm, there is a curse in name only, a decorative casting, which is your Faculty's business and not ours. This is not an evasion. It is the only reading that lets our practitioners work and your Faculty exist. We have four hundred and sixty-one entries in our own register recorded as *without result*. We do not call them maleficence; we call them registrations. Your Faculty calls them curses, and has already begun to issue licences for them; that is a claim, not a jurisdiction. If your position is doubt of ours, then let the clarification be sought from the body that put the Question, and let us all be bound by it as we have been bound for ninety-two years. Yours in the craft, Corbin Vale, for the Faculty of Jinxcraft & Minor Maleficence.

## 9. ...And HC's Reply

The reply, sent to **JM 6.1 / V35 1494** §4 and to the Faculty's counter on the eleventh of Ninthmonth 1494, is short and is in this volume's register at HC 2.1, fol. 61.

The Faculty's case fails at four points. First, it writes harm into the instrument and then measures the instrument by its absence, which is a disposal and not a definition, and one that empties JM's own register of 461 entries now offered to us as registrations. Second, it concedes the genus to keep the species: if the jinx is the genus and the curse a species of it that has discharged, the harmless curse is the completed instance of the art, and the genus belongs to whichever Faculty keeps the plates, which are ours under Finding iii of 1462. Third, latency is a duration; no Faculty here has ever claimed a thing for the length of time it fails to happen, and our claim is to the instrument and not to its patience. Fourth, the letter answers the Question by asking that it not be asked, which was the method of the Instrument of Hiatus of 1402. To the offer of a clarification we answer, as we have answered since 1411, that a clarification to be issued by the body that issued the hiatus, that body having been dissolved by the instrument requiring it to meet, is not merely outstanding but structurally so, as FN 7.1 makes plain.

What HC does *not* say, and what this volume records as a limit of its case, is that the Faculty has never established which Faculty owns the *instrument* — only which Faculty owns the plates, the register and the fee.

## 10. How a Practitioner Should Proceed while the Question Is Open

The procedure of Secondmonth 1502 was issued by the two Faculties jointly, HC/JM circular 4, and is reprinted here with the fees of 1504 and the warning that it is a holding measure and confers no immunity.

1. **Determine the class of the form, not of the result.** Take the form's name from the register in which it is entered: plate number, class letter, name. A casting is filed by its form, never by its outcome.
2. **Enter the casting in both registers before it is cast**, at 4 bells the entry, and keep both counterfoils. A casting entered after it discharges is a discharge, and the fee is doubled.
3. **Declare the licence on its face**, in the agreed words: *Provisional as to jurisdiction. The within licence is issued by the Faculty named and confers no immunity in the matter of a jinx causing no harm.* A licence not so endorsed is invalid against either Faculty.
4. **Enter on the licence, in the practitioner's own hand and not on a schedule:** the grade; the class letter of every form licensed; *harmless forms* against each form whose plate HC holds; and, after a hearing, the adjective **unresolved**.
5. **Take the fee into the hand of the Clerk of Appeals and not your own.** Counter II will hold a casting fee against a receipt, at no charge, while the Question is open. A fee taken before it is determined is taken at the practitioner's risk, per the second point of Finding iv.
6. **Insure the jurisdiction, not the harm.** The Nine Cloisters offices write a rider against disciplinary redundancy at 3 bells the quarter and one against double licensing at 5 bells the year. Neither has been tested; both have been bought.
7. **Do not read the papers of the *Journal of Harmless Maleficence*.** Clause 4 of FN 7.1 forbids it; the Upper Crypt is closed; the proofs of vol. xii are not to be folded.
8. **If a complaint is laid in the wrong Faculty, do not answer on the merits.** Answer on the instrument, cite Findings i and iii in the same paragraph, and put both to the Registrary.

## 12. What Would End the Question, and Why It Will Not Be Done

Three instruments would end it. A finding of the Registrary in the form of Finding iv, but naming the Faculty that owns the instrument: neither Faculty has asked for one, each having reason not to. An Act of the Concord of Nine Bells, which may be moved for once in seven years and has been moved for in every seventh year since 1454, the last three motions being item 4 on the paper and not reached. And a clarification under clause 6 of the Instrument of Hiatus, which succeeds the other two by being available at once, and cannot be obtained, the issuing body having been dissolved by the instrument that required it to meet.

A further course is open, and this volume does not recommend it. The Joint Standing Committee, being functum officio and not abolished — the word in the Instrument is *dissolved*, and it is the Concord's clerk who has read that as abolished — might be reconvened on its own seal, kept whole in the chest. This was proposed to the Clerk of Appeals in 1499 and to Dean Quill in 1501. The Clerk entered the proposal against the file and answered nothing.

## Appendix: The Findings, and Form HC/JM 11

Recited and not reconciled, as the Faculty's register has them; the spellings are the register's.

1. **Finding i (1404, EA).** Jurisdiction in matters of maleficence follows the instrument and not the effect.
2. **Finding ii (1419, JM).** Latency defines minor maleficence, and a jinx in good order need never discharge.
3. **Finding iii (1462, the Joint Standing Committee, last sitting).** Harmless casting belongs to HC; a jinx that discharges belongs to JM; the undischarged jinx belongs to the Faculty that owns the instrument.
4. **Finding iv (1491, the Registrary, on appeal).** The question is not determinable by either Faculty, and both are directed to proceed as they have been proceeding; and fees taken before the Question is determined are taken at the practitioner's risk.

The licence endorsement required by step 3 of the procedure of 1502, printed on the licence and not on a schedule, and written out by hand where the printed form is not available, which is the case at both counters:

*Provisional as to jurisdiction. The within licence is issued by the Faculty named and confers no immunity in the matter of a jinx causing no harm. The within licence does not determine the class of the instrument, nor the ownership of the instrument, nor the schedule under which a fee may be taken. This licence is entered twice.*
