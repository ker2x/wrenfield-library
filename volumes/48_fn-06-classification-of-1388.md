# The Classification of 1388

### The scheme of the presses, the series letters, and the numbering by position, in the fair copy of Mr. Ephraim Sallow, Under-Librarian, who was unwell

- **Call number:** FN 6.1 / S25 1388
- **Class:** FN — The Foundation Collection
- **Text:** English, fair copy in the hand of Mr. Ephraim Sallow, Under-Librarian 1381–1391, with the Library's later annotations in the margins
- **Date:** Michaelmas 1388; the Library doubts the last leaf, ruled but not written, and the final note, which is three words long
- **Custody:** Upper Crypt, with the Chapter chests; not to be produced to the Registry save by the Dean of Ethics, Appeals & Paperwork
- **Collation:** 14 leaves, one leaf ruled and unwritten, 1 folding table of series
- **Condition:** The iron-gall ink has burned through at the letter R on folio 7, so that the letter is legible by its hole; the final leaf smells of the room in which it was written

## The Library's Note

This is the scheme by which every book in this Library is placed, marked and found, drawn in the fair hand of Mr. Ephraim Sallow, Under-Librarian, at Michaelmas 1388, and it does two things and no others: it divides the Library into lettered series, one to a faculty, and it numbers each volume by its position upon its shelf, so that a book may be fetched by a person who has never seen it. It is not a catalogue, and is not a press-list; it names no title, and cannot be used to discover what the Library holds, only where a thing held would stand. The fair copy is Sallow's own, found in the Upper Crypt in 1494 during the inventory that was never finished; the Library has never found his working notes, and does not expect to, the man having been unwell. It can verify the hand and the date; it cannot verify the annotations, which are in four later hands and defend the scheme against objections raised only in the fifteenth century. The enlarged reissue prints the whole scheme, including the folding table and the two clauses the shorter issue passed over. Sallow's final note is three words long, and is not glossed here. The shelf-mates below were added in later hands, and are not Sallow's citations.

## The Scheme

1. **Of the series.** Be it known that the Library is divided into series, one to a faculty, and that each series is lettered with the letters of its faculty, and that no series is lettered otherwise. There are thirteen faculties, and therefore thirteen series; and to each series is assigned a press, and the press is numbered, and the number is the number of the faculty and not of the press's place in the room.

2. **Of R.** The letter R is also a series. It is assigned to the Registry. No press is assigned with it, the Registry keeping its property where it stands; but the property is classed, and is classed because it must be found, and is found by its letter.

3. **Of the shelf rule.** The number before the point is the shelf, counted from the door and not from the window, and counted on the left hand of one entering. A shelf not counted from the door is not counted.

4. **Of the numbering.** The number after the point is the position of the volume upon the shelf, and not a number of acquisition, and is not to be altered on accession, nor on the accession of a larger volume, nor on the binding of one.

5. **Of position.** A position is one hand broad of shelf, taken from the left. A volume of two hands occupies two positions and is entered *bis*. A volume too tall for the shelf is not a book of that shelf and goes to the tall presses, and the tall presses are classed by the same rule.

6. **Of the mark.** A volume is marked on the spine with series, shelf, position and the year of imprint; and a volume marked in the year of its imprint and rebound in another year keeps the first year, the mark being of the work and not of the boards.

7. **Of works of two faculties.** Where a work belongs to two faculties, it is placed in the series of the faculty that must rule upon it, and not in the series of the faculty that taught it, nor in that of the author; and where the faculty that must rule is the faculty that taught it, it is placed in the series of the faculty whose reader first must read it; and the cross-mark of the other faculty is written beneath, in pencil, and is not a mark.

8. **Of faults.** A volume mis-shelved is marked at the shelf with a slip, not with a knife; and the slip names the shelf, the series, and the day, and is kept with the volume until the shelf is read, and then destroyed.

9. **Of the making of a series.** A new series is made by the Chapter, on the motion of the Under-Librarian, and not by the Under-Librarian; and a series once made is not unmade, and a faculty once lettered is not unlettered, though it teach nothing.

10. **Of a room.** A room is not a faculty and has no series. A room is named and entered in the register of places, and its name is for finding and not for classing. Where a room is furnished with property that is classed, the property is entered under the series of its own faculty, and the room is entered in the register of places, and the two entries do not touch.

11. **Of the fourth reading room.** It is not a series. It is a room, and rooms are not classed.

12. **Of the annexes.** A class that is not a faculty and not a place, but a kind of thing, is annexed to the scheme by the Library, and is lettered with two letters, and is entered after the series; and the Library has annexed no such class, and does not intend to.

13. **Of the last leaf.** This leaf is ruled and is left unwritten. It is part of the scheme. It is not to be written upon.

## Named Failures and Their Penalties

**Michaelmas 1389, the return of shelves 3 and 4.** The annual return was signed by the Under-Librarian, Mr. Ephraim Sallow, without the shelves being read, three volumes of LT being on the tall presses of 6 and their positions vacant. For this he was fined two bells and required to read the shelf before the Deputy Under-Librarian and to enter the reading, which he did, in the register of shelf readings for that year, in his own hand; and the entry is the last in that book in his hand.

**The spring of 1391, the sixth position of the first series.** The sixth volume of EA, being the second part of a work of two hands, was entered *bis* by Sallow and the two positions marked on the spine as one; and the work was then bound in two volumes and marked with two positions, and for the whole of that year the second volume stood at the seventh position, and the seventh volume was therefore not found. The fault was entered at the shelf with a slip, by clause 8, and the slip is still with the shelf. Sallow was fined two bells and required to supply the scheme with a folded table of the *bis* entries; and he was forbidden to copy it again, the fair copy of 1388 being by then the only copy the Library would allow, which is why a document governing every shelf in this Library is in the hand of a man who was unwell.

**The tenth of June 1391, the fair copy.** A clerk of the Registry added at clause 4 the words *and not on the accession of a larger volume*; and the words are in the hand of the Library and not of Sallow, were written after Sallow had been forbidden to copy, and are not answered by him. The clerk was not fined, being of the Registry; the Registry was asked to enquire, and the enquiry is item nineteen of its book of enquiries for that year, and item nineteen has not been reached.

## The Library's Procedure in Classing and Reading

1. On the making of a series, the Under-Librarian rules a leaf, enters the series at the head, and enters the press against it.
2. The shelves are counted from the door, on entering, by the left hand, and the count is entered in the register of places as well as in the scheme.
3. Positions are counted from the left in hands; a volume of two hands is entered *bis*, and the *bis* entries are entered also in the folded table.
4. The shelf is read once in the year, in the week after Michaelmas, and the reading is signed; a reading not signed is not a reading.
5. A volume found out of its position is marked with a slip, not removed until the shelf is read again.
6. A series not read in the year is entered in the register of faults, against the shelf and not against the officer, officers being appointed at Michaelmas.
7. A new series is petitioned for on the motion of the Under-Librarian, is granted by the Chapter, and takes last place among the series, whatever its faculty's age.
8. The last leaf is left unwritten, is counted as a leaf of the scheme, and is not to be supplied by the Library.

## Apparatus

*Under-Librarian's stamp, in violet:* Consulted at the desk. The unwritten leaf is part of the work and is not to be written upon, including for the purpose of finishing it.

*Library's annotation, in four later hands, against clause 2:* The Registry is a series because its property is classed, and classed because it must be found. — *The letter is used as it is used.* — *If R is a series then the counter is a press, and the press is where it stands.* — *The clause says what it says.*

*Annotation, in a later hand, on the dorse, dated the eleventh day of March 1487:* The clause of the room is to be read with clause 2 and not with clause 8. Clause 8 provides for faults, and a room is not a fault. Clause 2 provides that a property classed under a letter is found by its letter, and a room's property is classed. On the sixth of this month a room was entered under R, with the form and the manual classed; and the entry is regular, the room being a place, a place being where a thing is put, and R being the letter of the Registry, whose reordering produced it. Sallow wrote his note at the foot of the last leaf, where the rules of the scheme end and the place of the scheme begins, and did not write it by accident.

*Annotation, in the Registry's hand, on the same dorse, dated 1491:* The room's clause is to be read with clause 8. A room entered under R is a room out of its place, and a thing out of its place is a fault, and faults are marked with a slip and kept until the shelf is read. No slip has been made. We are content.

*Marginalia, in pencil, hand unidentified, against clause 7:* Three faculties have ruled on the *Harmless Acts* since 1402, each ruling that the matter belongs to one of the other two; the book stands where it was placed, and the pencil cross beneath is a faculty's, and not a mark.

*Under-Librarian's note, in violet, pasted against clause 9:* The clause requires a motion, and a motion of mine, and I have made it. The particulars are four: *On Ventilation* and *The Care and Feeding of Library Poltergeists*, both mine and both at DE 22; the *Interim Report* of the Deputy Under-Librarian at DE 30.1; and the *Condition Survey* of the Conservator at DE 31.1. Every one of the four is a paper of this Library about this Library, and every one stands in Domestic Enchantment, which is the class of kettles and collars and the household poltergeist, and our papers stand there because there is nowhere else for them to stand. I put it no higher than this: clause 2 gives the letter R to the Registry, and the Registry is not a faculty; the Registry's property is classed because it must be found. Ours must be found also. The motion was made at the Michaelmas sitting of 1504 and was not reached; it was made again at the sitting following and was not reached again. I record that it was made, and that the record is here because this is the volume it would amend.

*Marginalia, in pencil, hand unidentified, beneath the note against clause 9:* The Registry's letter is the letter of a paper. The Library's would be the letter of the place the paper is kept in. Let them move the motion; the Chapter will have to say which.

*Marginalia, in pencil, in the same hand, at the foot of the note:* Four volumes, and no two of them by the same officer; the Library writes more than it classes.

*Library's annotation, in a later hand, against clause 9, dated 1504:* The four are read and are to keep their present letters. Where a series is not made, the mark stands, and a mark that stands is not a fault. The motion is entered as made and not reached, and is not to be entered as refused, a motion not reached not being a motion refused.

*Conservator's line:* Iron-gall burning at folio 7, R, stable but friable; interleave the unwritten leaf; the smell is not to be treated.
