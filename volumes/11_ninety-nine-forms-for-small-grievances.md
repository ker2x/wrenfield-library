## Abstract (as filed)

This handbook sets out the ninety-nine forms by which small grievances are raised: the fee in bells, the counter at which each is filed, the doctrine of the correct counter, and the six rungs of escalation. Of the 3,481 grievances lodged in 1492, 3,479 were accepted. The two refused were refused for want of a Form 7B, which is missing from this file and cannot be obtained, for the reason given at §9.

## 1. The Doctrine of the Correct Counter

A grievance filed at the wrong counter is not refused; it is *mislaid deliberately*, a kindness that spares the complainant a defective file. The counter follows the injury and not the injured party: settled since the Concord of Nine Bells, disputed eleven times a week. A reader who has not identified his injury files at Counter I in the hope that the counter will identify it; it will not. Counter I returns the grievance with a note naming the counter nearest in character, which the reader must then name. Novelty is not a counter; if an injury has no counter it has a Faculty, and if it has no Faculty it has this handbook.

## 2. The Fourteen Counters, Named

I, General Grievance, Small. II, Remedies, Worded. III, Thresholds. IV, Fare, Toll & Passage. V, Hours. VI, Beasts & Familiars. VII, Domestic. VIII, Insults, Written and Spoken. IX, Pollen & Produce. X, Water, Weather & Draught. XI, the Clerk's Counter. XII, Structures. XIII, Existence. XIV, Objections to Objections.

All close at four o'clock except Counter XI, which closes at three for its own reasons, historical and not entered in the schedule. Counter XI is where forms are obtained and fees paid, refunded or forfeited: the counter at which the hour matters most, and the one that closes first.

## 4. The Escalation Ladder

The ladder has six rungs; their order may not be varied, even where a lower rung is hopeless, and it is often hopeless, and it is climbed anyway. Two annexed motions may be entered at any rung.

## 5. How to File: The Procedure in Nine Steps

The steps are correct, complete, and should not be attempted in one morning.

1. Identify the injury, in one sentence, on a slip, and pocket the slip; a second sentence means two injuries and two files.
2. Identify the counter from the schedule, posted in the vestibule and at Counters I and XI. Do not ask the Clerk of Counter XI; the Clerk is not the schedule.
3. Obtain the form at Counter XI, or at the counter of filing where it is issued there. Form 7B is obtained at neither (§9).
4. Complete the form in ink. Pencil is accepted only on Form 8A, whose subject is smallness.
5. Present it at the counter of filing with the fee in bells or clipped bells. Marks are accepted and change is not given.
6. Require the form to be stamped twice, once by the receiving clerk and once by the clerk of the hour. A form stamped once is unstamped, and the second stamp is not supplied later.
7. Require a receipt; Form 47C is not a receipt. Check the date of the receipt against the date of presentation before leaving the counter.
8. If the hour has passed, require the file to be dated to the following day and that day's fee charged, and require the clerk to initial the receipt.
9. Keep the receipt. The receipt is the file; the file lies in the Registry and may rest there for a year, and the receipt is what the Tribunal reads.

A reader who omits step six has no complaint, the complaint being about step six, which was the reader's step.

## 6. The Rule of the Hour

The hour printed on the counter is the hour at which the counter closes — not when it begins to close, nor when the clerk looks up, nor when the reader's hand reaches the rail. A form presented at six minutes past the hour is dated to the following day, and that day's fee is charged, which is the fee prescribed for the form and not a penalty, the Faculty having no power to penalise at a counter. Since the first of January 1493 the rule has stood on the counter in paint; before that date it stood in the schedule, in the standing orders and in the Under-Librarian's stamp on the flyleaf, and nowhere at the counter — which the Tribunal in 1492 found a defect of the counter, not of the rule.

## 7. The Counter Matter of 1491, Told in Full

Mr. Wilfred Tye, a reader in Herbology of eleven years' standing, held a grievance against the Keeper of the North Greenhouse, who had labelled a bed of chervil as parsley and watered it as parsley for a season and a half. The injury was small and admitted. On the fourteenth of October 1491 Mr. Tye went to Counter XI for Form 12A, a provisional request for apology, and arrived at six minutes past three. Counter XI closes at three.

The junior clerk on duty, Mr. Jonas Petty, accepted the form, took the 4 bells, dated the file to the fifteenth of October, and charged that day's fee also, 4 bells again. He did this correctly.

Mr. Tye then made the error this handbook records without pleasure. The receipt described the fee of the fifteenth as "the fee prescribed", not as a second fee; the correct instrument was Form 9F, Objection to the Wording of an Objection (6 bells), at Counter XIV. On the seventeenth of October he presented it at Counter VIII, Insults, Written and Spoken, the receipt having insulted him. Counter VIII took the 6 bells and mislaid the file deliberately. He refiled at Counter XIV for 2 bells, and on the twenty-first of October the four-clipped-bell penalty under Standing Order 9 was entered against him.

The corrected file then climbed: rung one, the fourteenth of October 1491; rung two, Counter Schedule, the fourth of November; rung three, Counter Review, the second of December; rung four, Faculty Screening, the nineteenth of February 1492; rung five, the Tribunal, the eighth of August 1492; rung six, the Dean's Chamber, the fifteenth of September 1492, where the file rests.

The Tribunal found four things: that the file had been correctly dated to the fifteenth, the counter being closed at the hour of presentation; that the fee of the fifteenth had been charged to the wrong account, the counter having been open to receive the form and closed to charge the fee for it — hence the rule of the hour painted on the counter; that the four-clipped-bell penalty stood; and, as to costs, that the 4 bells be refunded, the 6-bell fee of Form 9F halved to 3, and 1 mark 2 bells awarded against the Registry, payable in form-credit, spendable only at Counter XI, which closes at three.

What the Faculty changed: the hour of closing was painted on every counter from the first of January 1493; the Clerk of Counter XI must ring the bell at the hour and at six minutes past; and Form 12C, Receipt of a Form Presented After the Hour (2 bells), was drafted and, not being a grievance, not numbered, so there are now one hundred forms.

## 8. The Named Failure: The Fee Forfeited at the Wrong Counter

The failure is Mr. Tye's filing of Form 9F at Counter VIII on the seventeenth of October 1491, recorded because it is complete: an adequate grievance, form, fee and counter. Standing Order 9 provides that a form presented at a counter not competent to receive it "shall be received, and mislaid, and the fee retained as the cost of the mislaying, and four clipped bells entered against the presenter as a witness that the counter was read."

The fee of 6 bells was forfeited. The refiling cost 2 bells. The four-clipped-bell penalty was entered on the twenty-first of October, payable within the month, not waived, and not waivable by the counter, the waiver lying with the Counter Officer — who had found against Mr. Tye at rung three. The wrong counter thus cost 8 bells and 4 clipped bells, against a disputed second fee of 4 bells. The Faculty's comment, entered at rung four, is that the reader came within 4 bells of being right and paid 8 bells 4 clipped bells to establish that the counter had also been wrong: a demonstration, the Faculty adds, that filing works. Mr. Tye does not agree.

## 9. On Form 7B, Reproduced as Printed

Form 7B is required for every thesis in this library and is missing from every thesis in this library. The reason is procedural and not a defect: the form is issued by one officer only, the Clerk of the Fourth Reading Room, at that room's counter, a hatch in the north wall. It is reproduced as printed.

> **FORM 7B — DECLARATION OF NON-INTERFERENCE WITH TIME.**
> *(Office of the Clerk of the Fourth Reading Room. Issued at that counter only.)*
>
> I, the undersigned, being of sound sequence, and not having been at any hour relevant to this declaration otherwise than where I say I was, do declare:
>
> 1. That I have not interfered with Time, nor caused Time to be interfered with, nor stood by while Time was interfered with by a person I decline to name, the naming being itself an interference.
> 2. That I have not entered, nor attempted to enter, the fourth reading room, whether by the door in the north wall or by any other means, the wall being two feet four inches thick, of which two feet four inches is wall.
> 3. That any interval unaccounted for in my day-book is unaccounted for because I was asleep.
> 4. That I understand this declaration to be required for every thesis, and the thesis of a candidate who has not filed it to be incapable of examination and of refusal.
>
> Filed at the counter of the fourth reading room, being hatch no. ................ in the north wall, on the ...... day of ......................., 14......, and received by me,
>
> .................................................................
> *Counter-Clerk of the Fourth Reading Room*

The signature line has never been filled. The only completed Form 7B known was filed at that counter in 1487 and is not held.

## 10. The Fourth Reading Room: The Plans of 1223, the Hours, and the Lamps

The room appears in the plans approved in 1223 (FN 2.1 / — 1223), which show four desks, a river window, a catalogue press and a hatch in the north wall. It appears in the schedule of hours as eight to four, the only reading room that opens before nine. It appears in the annual return of lamps, one lamp returned for it each year, trimmed, and drawn from stores.

The room does not appear in the building. Surveyors have measured the north wall nine times; the last measurement, in 1484, found it two feet four inches thick, of which two feet four inches is wall. A form issued from the room is nonetheless validly issued, validity attaching to the counter and not to the floor beneath it. A counter may stand at a wall; the wall need not open. That is the whole doctrine, and every thesis rests upon it.

## 11. On Dean Ambrose Quill, Who Approves Nothing

The Dean has approved nothing in twenty-nine years of office, which persons who have not read the Statutes construe as obstruction. A grant, a licence, a variation and a thesis all require his approval, and a thing he has not approved cannot be said to have been approved.

The procedure does not terminate at rung six. It rests there. A resting file retains its number, its fee and its dignity; its complainant retains standing; its subject retains a question over him. Approval would close the file, and a closed file can be forgotten, and a forgotten grievance is a grievance unhealed. It is submitted, respectfully, and against the weight of opinion in the Nine Cloisters, that a grievance properly filed is a grievance half-healed. The other half is the Dean's. He is not holding it. He is keeping it.

## 12. The Objection of the Rector's Office, and the Reply

On the twentieth of January 1493 the Rector's office wrote to the Faculty, over the signature of the Rector, Gervase Turl. The material paragraph is in the Rector's own voice.

> We are told a new form has been drafted, the hundredth, called a receipt. We are told also that eighty-seven of the ninety-nine are now supplied in a bundle, unstamped, for the applicant to stamp. A Faculty that drafts a form in answer to every inconvenience will in time discover that its forms are its government, and that its government is administered by clerks at fourteen counters, one of whom closes at three for reasons no one will state. We do not say the Faculty has exceeded its warrant; we say the Faculty has not yet shown us its warrant, and that the Faculty's answer to every question of warrant has been a form. Let the Faculty produce the warrant, or let it produce fewer forms, and let it begin by withdrawing the hundredth.

The Faculty's reply, entered on the second of February 1493 (folio 61), was this.

The warrant is the Concord of Nine Bells, which established the counters and required an injury to be answered at a counter and not in the cloister. The Faculty has never drafted a form that did not answer an injury already recorded, and the register of injuries is open to the Rector's office at any hour before four. Form 12C is a receipt for a fee, not a form of grievance, and the Faculty declines to number it among grievances: the number is the grievance's, and the receipt proves that the grievance was filed.

As to the eighty-seven, they are unused *this year*: the mean interval between a form's drafting and its first use is nineteen years, the longest eighty-one, Form 8A. The Rector's office has since written four further letters, the fourth a single line: *We have read folio 61.*

## 13. The Manual, *Form 7B for Dummies*, and What Is Given at the Desk Instead

The practitioner's manual for Form 7B is the Aldermarket piracy *Form 7B for Dummies*, octavo, boards rubbed, bought in 1483 for 4 bells, the Library's only copy known. It was shelved at the forms counter in the fourth reading room, and went with the room. It is catalogued and not held; it may be cited, and may not be consulted.

Readers who ask for the manual at the desk are given Form 41C (2 bells), whether they want it or not. The counter does not explain, and the reader who protests is at the wrong counter and will be mislaid deliberately.

## 14. The Duty to File

The forms are not an obstacle between the reader and the remedy. The form *is* the remedy, in its first and cheapest part. A complaint that has a counter, a number and a fee can be answered; a complaint made in a corridor cannot, and will be answered anyway, with a door. A grievance properly filed is a grievance half-healed. File early; file at the correct counter; require the second stamp; and where the correct counter cannot be found, because it is a hatch in a wall two feet four inches thick, of which two feet four inches is wall, file Form 55A anyway, before the hour, and keep the receipt.
