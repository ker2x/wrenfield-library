# The Instrument of Hiatus

### The document under seal of 1402 by which the *Journal of Harmless Maleficence* was put upon hiatus pending clarification, and was not taken off it

**Call number:** FN 7.1 / — 1402
**Class:** FN — The Foundation Collection
**Text:** English, in the hand of a notary of the Faculty of Ethics, Appeals & Paperwork, signing *J. Petyt*, with the seal appended on a tag
**Date:** Sealed the fourteenth of June 1402; the Library doubts the date on the tag, which is written in a second hand and differs from the body by one day
**Custody:** Upper Crypt, first press, in the chest of the Concord; not to be produced to the editors of the journal named in it, who are not to be admitted to the Crypt
**Collation:** 1 membrane, indented at the foot, with 2 seals and a schedule of witnesses appended
**Condition:** The seal is whole and the impression deep; the membrane has long been folded in four, and the folds give way along the sentence in clause 6, the sentence most often quoted

## The Library's Note

This is the instrument, under seal and indented, by which the *Journal of Harmless Maleficence* was put upon hiatus in 1402 pending a clarification that has not been issued, and it does one thing: it stops a journal, and it fixes the only body that could have restarted it. It is not a judgment and not a censure. It finds nothing against the editors, names no fault, and imposes no penalty; it forbids the reading of papers, and it makes the forbidding body the body that must read them. The instrument is authentic, indented, and sealed, and the Library has the counterpart, cut from the same membrane, and the cut edges agree. It can verify the notary, the witnesses, and the seal; it cannot verify the meeting at which the hiatus was resolved upon, for which no minute survives, and declines to investigate, the persons who might have kept one being the persons most nearly concerned. The enlarged reissue prints the schedule of witnesses and the table of clauses with their effects, both of which the shorter issue omitted. The instrument has never been repealed, and no instrument repealing it is expected. The absent Form 7B, *Declaration of Non-Interference with Time*, governs theses and not this document, and its absence from the journal's file is accordingly not a finding. The shelf-mates below were added by the Library in later hands.

## The Instrument

1. **Whereas** the *Journal of Harmless Maleficence* was established in this city in the year 1399 by the editors of the Faculty of Jinxcraft & Minor Maleficence and of the Faculty of Harmless & Decorative Curses together, and has published five numbers, in the fifth of which the doctrine of the harmless act has been carried beyond the doctrine of the faculties, and beyond the bounds of the question the two faculties put to one another.
2. **And whereas** it is doubted in the said two faculties whether a jinx causing no harm is a curse, and the doubt has not been resolved, and the Journal has twice declined to resolve it, saying in the fifth number that the doubt is its subject and not its finding.
3. **It is resolved** by the body here sealing that the said Journal be put upon hiatus, pending clarification of the doubt in clause 2, and that the hiatus take effect from the sealing and not from the printing of this instrument.
4. **And that** while the hiatus stands the Journal shall not receive, nor read, nor answer, nor return any paper offered to it; and that papers already received shall remain in the press of the Journal, and shall not be read by the editors nor by any other person, and that the press be sealed with the seal in clause 9 until the clarification is issued.
5. **And that** the seal of the Journal shall not be used upon any number, nor offprint, nor answer to a letter, nor receipt, and that the seal here appended shall be the only seal used in this matter.
6. **And that** the clarification in clause 3 shall be issued by the body issuing this hiatus, and by no other body, and in the form of a resolution of that body entered in its own book; and that the hiatus shall stand until the said clarification is issued and entered.
7. **And that** a meeting of the body issuing this hiatus shall be held in every year, upon the fourteenth day of June, for the purpose of considering whether the clarification can be issued; and that the meeting be entered in the book whether or not any member attends, and that no business be done at it other than the entry.
8. **And that** the fees of the notary, six bells, be paid from the contingency of the Faculty of Ethics, Appeals & Paperwork, and that the payment be acknowledged in the margin by the Bursar's clerk, and it is so acknowledged.
9. **The seal** is the seal of the *Journal of Harmless Maleficence*: a press broken, with three threads whole across the break.
10. **Witnesses.** Master Hildebrand Ost, of the Faculty of Divination; Master R. Sallow, mason, of Alderwick; Mr. A. Wyke, clerk of the Chapter; and the notary, who signs in his own matter as the rule requires.

## The Clauses and Their Effects

| Clause | What it provides | What it has done |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recites the Journal's establishment and its fifth number | Read in 1402 only; the fifth number was never reprinted |
| 2 | Recites the doubt: whether a jinx causing no harm is a curse | Still recited, still unresolved, in use as a citation by both faculties |
| 3 | Puts the Journal on hiatus pending clarification | The operative clause; the Journal has published nothing since 1402 |
| 4 | Forbids the reading of papers, and seals the press | Prevents the editors from reading the papers on which they must rule |
| 5 | Restricts the use of the Journal's seal | Not applied since 1402; the instrument's seal is the seal of the matter |
| 6 | Confines the clarification to the body issuing the hiatus | Bars every other body, including the Chapter and the two faculties |
| 7 | Requires a meeting of that body on the fourteenth of June each year | Entered in the book for 1402; not entered for any year since |
| 8 | Charges the notary's fee to the contingency | Paid; the acknowledgment is the only signature of the Bursar's clerk on the membrane |
| 9 | Identifies the seal, and so the sealing body | The press broken, three threads whole: the Journal's own mark, and no faculty's |
| 10 | Names the witnesses | Two of the four have left no other trace in the record |

## How the Body Is to Meet, and What It Must Read

1. The body meets at the third hour in the room of the Journal, on the fourteenth day of June.
2. The senior editor present calls the meeting, and if he is not present the next senior present calls it, and if none is present the meeting is not held and the day is entered by the clerk of the Journal as a day on which none was present.
3. The clerk reads the entry of the previous year, and no other paper is read.
4. The body considers whether the clarification of the doubt in clause 2 can be issued.
5. To consider whether it can be issued, the body must be satisfied as to the state of the doubt, and to be satisfied it must read the papers in the press.
6. The press is opened only by the seal of the Journal, which is the seal of the body, and by clause 4 the papers are not to be read.
7. The body resolves, or does not resolve, and the resolution is entered in the book, and the entry is the clarification if it is a clarification.
8. The meeting is then closed, and the year's stipend of two marks falls due to the clerk of the Journal, and is paid, the entry of the year having been made.

## Findings, and Where the Procedure Has Failed

**The finding of 1402.** The Editors of the Journal, having published five numbers and having twice declined to resolve the doubt in clause 2, are put upon hiatus pending clarification. No fault is found; no penalty is attached; the schedule of forty-one unread papers is appended and is not to be opened.

**The failure of the notary, 1403.** The date on the tag was written in a second hand and differs from the body of the instrument by one day, and was written by the clerk of the notary after sealing. The notary was fined four bells and required to enter the correction in his register, which he did, in a hand the Library takes to be his, and which the Library prints in the shorter issue without comment.

**The failure of the Faculty of Harmless & Decorative Curses, 1411.** The Faculty's reader, one Master Odo Rennet, opened the Journal's press in the fourth week of Lent, read eleven of the forty-one papers, and returned them in the order in which he found them. For this he was fined sixty bells, of which the Faculty paid thirty and the reader thirty, and the press was resealed on the twenty-ninth of March with a second seal, which is not to be relied upon. The papers read are not to be cited, and have twice been cited.

**The failure that is not entered, 1402–1504.** The body required by clause 7 to meet on the fourteenth of June has not met since the fourteenth of June 1402; the book of the Journal contains no entry of a meeting for any year after that, and no clerk's entry of a day on which none was present, the clerk's post itself having fallen into the contingency with the stipend. No penalty is provided for a body that does not meet, the instrument having provided only the meeting and not the consequence, and the two marks of the annual stipend have accordingly been paid to the Library as a lapsed charge since 1403, in the form of forty-one marks in arrear, entered in the Library's books and not to be written off.

## Apparatus

*Under-Librarian's stamp, in violet:* Entered twice. Not to be consulted on a Wednesday, when the readers of the two faculties are both in the room.

*Annotation, in a later hand, in the margin against clause 9, dated 1504:* The seal is not the Faculty's, nor the Chancellor's, nor the Concord's. It is the seal of the *Journal of Harmless Maleficence*: a press broken, with three threads whole. The body issuing the hiatus under clause 3 is therefore the body named in the seal, and the body named in the seal is the editors of the Journal; and the body that must issue the clarification under clause 6 is therefore likewise the editors; and the editors are forbidden by clause 4 to read the papers on which they must resolve, and no other body may resolve in their place. The instrument that required the editors to meet every fourteenth of June is the same instrument that forbade them to read what they were to meet about; and it has been read as dissolving the board, since a body that may not read its own press cannot be a board of editors. The last entry of a meeting is the fourteenth of June 1402, the day of the sealing; the hiatus since that day has not been pending a clarification that has not come. It has been pending a clarification that clause 4 prevents, and clause 6 forbids anyone else to issue.

*Protocol of the meeting of the fourteenth of June 1402, entered on the dorse by the clerk of the Journal, in a hand that appears nowhere else:* Present, five of the seven editors; the two absent are in the Tenterfields. The instrument was read aloud by the notary. It was asked whether the press should be sealed before or after the papers were listed; it was resolved that the listing was before and the counting after. Forty-one papers were counted. It was asked by Master Ost whether a board that cannot read its papers is a board; no answer was entered. The meeting closed at the fourth hour. The next meeting is the fourteenth of June next.

*Marginalia, in pencil, hand unidentified, against clause 6:* They sealed the door and kept the key, and then disposed of the key by the same seal.

*Objection, entered on the dorse in the hand of the senior editor, dated the same day, and crossed through in the same ink:* The instrument forbids the reading of papers already received; it does not forbid the reading of papers not yet received, and the doubt in clause 2 may be clarified from the doubt itself, which is written in our own fifth number and is ours to open. — *Reply, beneath, in the notary's hand:* The doubt is a paper of the Journal and lies in the press with the rest, and the press is sealed in clause 4, and the fifth number is a number within clause 5.

*Conservator's line:* Membrane sound; folds giving at clause 6, to be supported from behind; the tag is a second hand and is not to be cut away; do not humidify the seal.
