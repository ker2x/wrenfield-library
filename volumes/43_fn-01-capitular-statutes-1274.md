# The Capitular Statutes of 1274

### Statutes of the Chapter, in force 1274–1504, and last confirmed in the present year, with the roll of confirmations and the Chapter's table of fines

**Call number:** FN 1.1 / — 1274
**Class:** FN — The Foundation Collection
**Text:** Latin, with clauses 13–18 in Norman French; the fair engrossment in the hand of a Chapter scribe signing *W. de Aldre*, the numbering in a second hand throughout
**Date:** Enacted at the Michaelmas Chapter of 1274; the Library doubts the eighth and ninth leaves, which are in a later hand and interrupt the numbering
**Custody:** Kept in the Upper Crypt, in the second press, in the Chapter's own chest; not to be produced to any officer of the Registry without the Dean of the Faculty of Ethics, Appeals & Paperwork present
**Collation:** 11 leaves; one roll of confirmations 1275–1401 appended; one half-leaf of fines in a hand of about 1300
**Condition:** The lower edge of the fifth leaf is eaten by damp to the loss of four words; the seal-tags have been cut away, and the cutting is tidy, which the Library finds worse than the loss

## The Library's Note

The text is the Chapter's own book of statutes: the clauses of 1274, saying who the Chapter is, what rents it takes, how long a deposit may lie unclaimed, what its officers are fined, how the oath is sworn, and how the whole is amended. It is not University law but the Chapter's private law, and the Faculty of Ethics, Appeals & Paperwork treats it as the root of the University's practice in property and in lapse. The statutes came to the Library in 1501 with the Chapter's chest; the roll of confirmations runs to 1401 and stops, for reasons the Chapter has never offered and the Library has never asked. The Library cannot verify the eighth and ninth leaves, which are in a different hand, contradict the numbering they interrupt, and are the two clauses the Registry has relied upon most heavily; it declines to investigate, a question of that kind once opened having to be answered by the Chapter. A fourth reading room is not mentioned in the text, and clauses 9 and 10, which the Registry reads as having anticipated it, are the wording the Library has always used for the third. No Form 7B is filed here; the form governs theses, of which this is not one.

## The Statutes

### Of the Chapter and its sittings

1. That the Chapter shall sit at Michaelmas and at Candlemas, and at no other time, save when the bells disagree.
2. That the ninth is the bell-warden, who shall not speak where the nine are divided four and four.
3. That no clause shall be pleaded in a court of the University until read at two sittings.
4. That the Chapter keep a chest of three locks, the keys held by the bell-warden, the rent-warden, and the keeper of the seal, and that no one key shall open it.
5. That the clerk shall enter the acts of each sitting within eight days; an act not entered is charged to the clerk at twopence the day.
6. That the first week of a sitting is given to the reading of the roll and the audit of the rents, and no cause between scholars shall be heard in it.
7. That a member late shall pay one bell, absent two, and late at Candlemas three, the winter being none of his excuse.

### Of rents, tenancies, and the rooms

8. That no beast of the field, the yard, or the water shall be distrained upon for the debts of a scholar, if the beast be the whole of the scholar's substance.
9. **[later hand]** That the room next the river, of four desks, be reserved.
10. **[later hand]** That the same room be accounted a room of the Library.
11. **Of geese.** No goose shall be raised from the dead within the precincts. And this is not for any cause touching the art, which the Chapter leaves to the schools, but upon the law: for a goose is a tenant, holding of the Chapter at a rent of one egg, and the Chapter does not disturb its tenants.
12. That the same is not to be said of hens, which hold nothing.
13. That every tenancy shall be held by a rent certain, and a rent of one egg is certain, and the Chapter shall not compound a rent of one egg for money, having done so in 1231 and lost the tenancy.
14. That a room named in the rent-roll three years shall be named in the statutes, and where it is not so named the officers shall say *the room beyond*.
15. That a tenant may not dig, build, or plant against the Chapter's wall without the leave of two sittings; leave given at one sitting is a favour and not a leave.
16. That the rent-warden shall walk the tenancies once in the year, between Easter and Whitsun, with the rent-roll and no other man's opinion.

### Of deposits and things left

17. **Of deposits.** What is left with the Library and not claimed, and not entered in the register, and not demanded at three Candlemas courts, shall lapse to the finder in the eleventh year, and the finder shall render a tenth to the Chapter.
18. That the eleventh year is counted from the Feast of the Epiphany following the deposit, and not from the deposit.
19. That no officer shall break a seal to discover what has lapsed, and that what is lapsed shall be opened only by the finder, and privately.
20. That the lapse shall not run against the Chapter, nor against a person in the Chapter's service, nor against a person whose death the Chapter has no notice of.
21. That where a thing is left and neglected and its owner is dead, the lapse shall run as if the owner lived, the dead being patient.

### Of fines, windows, and the seal

22. That a scholar who strikes a bell out of order pays twopence, and a scholar who strikes it twice pays nothing, the second being the offence.
23. That each faculty shall have its window, and shall open it at its own charge.
24. That fines shall be paid in bells, and in clipped bells only where the sum does not divide by twenty, and that no fine shall be paid in kind save the fine of a goose, which is an egg.
25. That a fine unpaid within three days shall double, and a fine doubled twice shall stand as a debt of the tenancy and not of the man.
26. That every officer shall be sworn on the chest and not on the book, the book being the University's and the chest the Chapter's.
27. That the oath shall be these words and no more: *I will keep the rents, I will not open what has lapsed, I will say the room's name as written.*
28. That an officer sworn at the wrong sitting shall be sworn again at Michaelmas, and that the first oath be burned with the taper.
29. That a member who will not swear shall be excused the sitting and charged as present.
30. That the Chapter's mark be a bell, and the seal of two faces, the bell and the wall; sealed with the wall alone the instrument is the Chapter's, and with both the Chancellor's.
31. That no clause shall be sealed in the absence of the keeper of the seal, who shall be a member of the Chapter and not a clerk.

### Of amendment and appeal

32. That these statutes shall be amended only at Michaelmas, only by seven of the nine, and only after the clause proposed has stood a year on the roll unamended.
33. That a clause once proposed and withdrawn may not return within the year, and a clause twice withdrawn not until the ninth year.
34. That an officer fined may appeal to the Chapter, and shall be heard at the same sitting, and shall pay the fine first.
35. That an appeal not entered in writing within three days is not an appeal but a complaint.
36. That the Chapter shall answer an appeal in writing, an answer not in writing being no answer, and that the Chapter's practice is accordingly to answer nothing.
37. That the roll of confirmations shall be read aloud once in seven years, and that a failure to read is no lapse, and that the roll stops where it stops.
38. That these statutes are the whole law of the Chapter, save the customs of the yard, which are older and are not written.

## Clause 11 objected to, and the Chapter's answer

On the dorse of the eighth leaf, in a small undated hand:

> *The clause is a confusion. If a goose is a tenant at one egg then it is a beast, and the Chapter may distrain it as it may distrain any other beast; and this statute, which forbids the raising of a goose, is a statute against the art and not for the tenancy. Let the clause be struck, or the rent raised, and the argument is at an end.*

Beneath it, in the Chapter's own hand:

> *The goose holds of us. The rent is one egg and it has been paid. We have not raised a beast from the dead, and we have not raised the rent, and we do not intend to do both in one year. The clause stands.*

The objection was never withdrawn; the answer is in the hand of clauses 9 and 10.

## The procedure of a rent-day dispute

1. The rent-warden calls the tenancy at the Michaelmas sitting, three times.
2. A tenant who disputes the rent pays it and disputes it; a tenant who withholds it is not heard.
3. The warden states the rent due and the tenant the rent paid; neither may produce a writing not in the chest.
4. The bell-warden counts the rents aloud and does not speak to the merits.
5. Where warden and tenant agree, the clerk enters the agreement and the matter ends.
6. Where they do not, the Chapter takes the evidence of the yard, the memory of the oldest tenant present.
7. The Chapter answers at the following sitting; where it does not, the rent stands as paid, which has happened twice.
8. No appeal lies from a hearing at which the warden was absent, and there is no remedy for this.

## The cause of the goose at one egg, 1279

In the second year of these statutes one Aldith, a widow of the Tenterfields, held a garth on the north side of the precinct, and with it a goose; the rent of the garth was one egg, and the rent of the goose stood in the roll as a second egg. In the spring of 1279 the rent-warden, G. de Wren, distrained the goose for arrears of that second egg, being then of opinion that a goose which lays is not a goose which pays. The goose was taken on the 4th of April and penned in the yard. On the 6th the tenant pleaded the clause of geese. The warden answered that the second egg was a rent of the bird and not of the tenancy, and that the bird had laid it and eaten it, which he could not prove and did not withdraw. The Chapter found for the tenant on the 19th, restored the goose, and fined the warden four bells. The second egg was struck from the roll, and the Chapter has held since that a goose's rent is one egg. The cause stands first in the half-leaf of fines, as *pro ansere, iiij bell*.

*Annotation, in a later hand, at the head of clause 10:* Upon this clause it is to be understood that the room next the river is not a room but the reservation of a room, and that what the Chapter reserved in 1274 was the right to name such a room when it should be built; the room being built, the reservation is spent, and the fourth reading room is held under no clause of these statutes but by the general law of rooms.
