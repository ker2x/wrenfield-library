## Abstract (as filed)

The complainant is a cultivar of the common bean, *Tenterfield Quiet*, which will not germinate within earshot of laughter, having been certified so by the Faculty of Herbology & Sentient Botany under HS 8.2 / W75 1500. It filed on Form HS/9, paid the fee of 3 bells, and asks the Faculty to find the laughter at its bed an insult and the cultivar defamed. The Faculty records the insults, holds the licence void, declines the defamation, and orders no remedy; no party is content, and Form 7B is absent from the docket.

## 1. The form by which a plant files

A plant does not file as a person; it has no hand to sign and no dwelling to be served at. It files under the Apiary Schedule of 1389, which gave standing first to a hive, which may claim through its keeper, and then to a single bee on its own account. Two bees have litigated, and one succeeded, the apiary being required to hear the settlement read aloud. Rooted parties the Schedule left to whoever had leisure, and the Faculty of Herbology has had the leisure since the founding of the north range.

Form HS/9 — *Statement of Rooted Grievance* — was struck for the purpose in 1398 by a committee that then dissolved. It has four leaves: two for the history of the planting, one for the matter complained of and the tender, and a fourth ruled wide for the cultivator's mark. *Tenterfield Quiet* made its mark in the pressed impression of one leaf of its own habit, taken warm, in iron-gall ink, at the fourth node. The fee is 3 bells; it was paid in coin on the second of September 1500. The bean declined a receipt, having nowhere to keep it; the refusal of a receipt does not affect the fee.

## 3. The pleading in full

The Clerk of Appeals took the statement down at the bed, before the keeper and one bee. It is set out as filed on leaf three, without correction, the Clerk having entered his doubts in the left margin where the form provides for them.

> I am the cultivar *Tenterfield Quiet*, sown the eleventh of May 1500 in bed nineteen of the north range, at a depth of two fingers, and catalogued in the North Greenhouse Day-Book under the mark Q as an insult trial. I am one of one hundred and four of my cultivar in that bed and the only one named. I do not refuse to germinate; refusal is a thing done by a party who could have done otherwise, and I am told that I could not. What I do is this: I do not germinate within earshot of laughter. That is my condition, certified of me by the Faculty of Herbology & Sentient Botany under its own seal in 1500, and the certificate names my bed and my mark. On the third of June, the eleventh of June, the nineteenth of July and the twentieth of August of that year, laughter was administered at my bed under one licence and directed at my habit, at my parentage, and at the company I keep. My germination on those occasions was nil, and in the fifty-one days before them also nil, which I record against my own case. I ask the Faculty to find the laughter an insult and that I have been defamed among the only body that could hold my name. If no remedy is fit, I ask for an apology, read aloud at the bed, at an hour when the range is empty. I am at the Faculty's disposal, in the ground.

The cultivar's evidence, appended at exhibit Q/2, is three impressions in ink on the reverse of leaf three: the fourth node in June, habit entire; the same node in September, one tendril turned from the north; and the ground about the stem in October, radicle unbroken, testa unruptured, the soil in place exactly as sown. The Clerk's marginal entry against the third reads: *The soil is indeed in place. It is in place in all one hundred and four.*

## 4. The night of the insult at the bed in the north range

The principal occasion is the fourth, of the twentieth of August 1500, compiled from the keeper's complaint, the return of laughter, the Day-Book, and the inspection of the fourteenth of April 1501.

Licence HS/L-1500/41 was granted to Marigold Thistlewick, M.M.M., of Agricultural Curses & Pollen Pathology, on the ninth of May 1500, authorising laughter in the chamber standing fourth in the north range, upon a fee of 12 bells. No chamber stands fourth in the north range. The range holds three glazed chambers, a potting shed and a passage; the plans show a fourth, and the survey of 1484 found the north wall two feet four inches thick, of which two feet four inches is wall. Miss Thistlewick did not inspect the chamber. On her evidence she engaged one, paid a porter 4 clipped bells to unlock it, and laughed where the porter led her, in the open bed-ground.

At about eight in the evening she came into the bed-ground with two of Pollen Pathology, a lantern, a stool, and a fair copy of a jest in her own hand. The bed-keeper, Mr. Alban Kipper, was in the potting shed nine yards off, trimming a lamp, and did not attend, the hour being after his hours and the party being licensed. Miss Thistlewick read the jest aloud at the head of bed nineteen; it concerned the complainant's parentage, and the Faculty has read it and does not reproduce it, a jest reproduced in a judgment being a jest administered again. The two of Pollen Pathology laughed immoderately at the close, one having been supplied with the jest in advance and the other not. Miss Thistlewick then read it a second time, which the licence did not authorise, and all three laughed again. The complaint puts the duration at eleven minutes and the return at four; the Faculty, weighing the party who administered the laughter against the party who heard it, finds between four and eleven minutes and declines to fix it.

The bed was inspected on the following morning by Mr. Kipper, and again on the twenty-second and twenty-ninth, and no radicle had broken the testa of the named bean, nor of any other bean in bed nineteen. The Day-Book for the twenty-first of August records: *Bed 19 inspected at six. Nil. All 104 sound. Soil undisturbed. Mark Q unchanged. Laughter heard last night, three voices, in the bed-ground, not in the chamber, which is not there.* The last clause is struck through, the strike legible, and the Faculty prints the entry with the strike.

Mr. Kipper's complaint was laid on the twenty-eighth of August, not on the complainant's behalf: the keeper complained of the licence and the porter, of the open bed-ground used as a chamber, and of the loss of a week's work while the Faculty's clerks attended. The bean's statement followed six days later, and the bean would not have filed, in the Faculty's view, but for the keeper.

The inspection was executed under warrant EA/19 by the Clerk of Appeals, with the Conservator, Mr. Casper Nunn, and Mr. Kipper for the range, upon the bed, the bed-ground, the chambers, the potting shed and the passage. The party found the bed in good order, one hundred and four plants above ground; no fourth chamber, the wall being solid and the plans wrong in the Registry's own keeping; a porter's hook on the wall where the plans show a door; and a stool and a fair copy of a jest in Miss Thistlewick's hand. The porter was not called, having left the University's service.

The Faculty draws three things and declines a fourth. First, the control reached ninety-one of one hundred and four, the range's ordinary figure, and the laughter is the only difference between the beds that the range did not intend. Second, the named bean was nil at every count. Third, five beans of bed nineteen germinated, from the sixth week, and none is the complainant and none is named; the Day-Book records the first as *not Q*, which the Faculty reads as evidence that a bean which is not the named bean cannot complain.

The fourth thing, offered by the keeper, is that the bed germinated better in the weeks when the laughter was at second hand than in those when it was not at the bed at all, the numbers being twelve and eleven. The Faculty has not been persuaded, having regard to the weather, the watering, and the fact that laughter reported is laughter at a remove.

## 6. Whether a bean can be defamed: the objection of the Faculty's counsel

The Faculty's counsel was nominated by the Dean on the twenty-first of November 1500, upon the copying fee of 4 clipped bells, and filed an opinion of nine leaves; the objection on standing is printed in the counsel's own words.

> It is objected that the complainant has no standing to be defamed, and the objection is not technical. Defamation requires a reputation, and a reputation requires a body among whom it is held and by whom it may be lowered. The complainant names its body and the body is the bed: one hundred and four plants of a single cultivar, none named but the complainant, none able to give evidence, and none, on the record, germinated. A body that has produced no one able to speak cannot hold a good name, and cannot lower one. It is objected further that the complainant's own case is that its condition, and not the respondent's laughter, is the cause of its non-germination: a laughter that occasions a condition is not an injury to the party but a discovery of it. It is objected thirdly that no remedy can reach the complainant: an apology read at the bed is addressed to a party that hears and cannot answer, and the Faculty has held that an apology read outdoors reaches one hundred and four. The Faculty is invited to dismiss the pleading on the first objection, and to record the second and third as reasons why it should not be asked to do so. There is no fourth objection.

## 7. The keeper's reply, from the Apiary Schedule and the doctrine of consent

Mr. Alban Kipper replied for the range on the nineteenth of December, on plain paper, with no form and no fee, the Faculty having waived the form, the fee of one bell being paid as a loss of the trial.

> To the objection that a bean has no standing to be defamed I answer with the Schedule of 1389, which stands in the Foundation Collection and is engrossed and is not a thesis. The Schedule gave a hive a claim through its keeper, and gave a single bee a claim on its own account, and the bee brought it, and the bee had it. If a bee may have a name among bees, a bean may have a name among beans, and the Faculty is not entitled to say that the bee's fellows could speak and the bean's cannot, because it is not the fellows who hold the name; it is the name that is held, and a body of one hundred and four is a body. To the second objection I answer that the licence is the answer, and that the licence is the Faculty's own instrument. A licence is a species of consent, and consent is bounded by its terms. The licence named a chamber. There is no chamber. The laughter was therefore administered outside the consent, and the defence of the condition falls away. To the third objection I answer that the remedy I want is not the apology but the finding: I ask the Faculty to say in writing that the licence was bad, because I have a trial to run in the spring and no Faculty will license me a chamber that is not there.

The Clerk of Appeals has noted in the margin that the keeper asked for the finding and not the fee, and that the Faculty gave him the fee and not the finding.

## 8. How the laughter came to be licensed, and the defect found

The licence was struck on the fourth counter on the ninth of May 1500 by a Junior Clerk, Third Class, Unconfirmed, who entered the chamber as the chamber standing fourth in the north range, that being the description in the printed schedule then in use, uncorrected since the survey of 1484. The clerk did not open the plans, a plan being a record and a counter not a reading room. The defect was found on the eighteenth of November 1500 by the Clerk of Appeals of his own motion, on reading the return of laughter; the licence was held void by notice EA/3, and the fee of 12 bells was not refunded.

Two of the three errors in this matter were the Faculty's own: the schedule at the counter, and the licence executed in the open. The third was the Faculty's also, in permitting a licence to be administered by a party who had administered it before. The clerk was not disciplined; being unconfirmed, no charge lay against him, the Registry's remedy where no charge lies being promotion. He was promoted in the Hilary following.

## 9. Procedure: how a plant files, numbered

The Faculty sets out the course in full; a party wishing to attempt it should read all nine steps, and should then not begin.

1. **Certification.** Obtain from the Faculty of Herbology & Sentient Botany a certificate of condition, under seal, naming the cultivator, the bed, the mark and the condition, and pay 2 bells.
2. **Mark.** Take the cultivator's mark, in iron-gall ink, on leaf four of Form HS/9, at the node of the cultivator's own choosing, before a clerk. A party with no node makes its mark at the counter, in the clerk's hand.
3. **Statement.** Complete leaves one to three: the history of the planting, the matter complained of, and the tender. Describe the matter in the terms of the instrument complained of, not the party's feelings; feelings are not a form.
4. **Fee.** Pay 3 bells at the forms counter, second position, and take a receipt. The receipt is a separate instrument; declining the receipt does not decline the fee.
5. **Numbering.** The clerk enters the matter in the register of rooted grievances and assigns a number. The number is the matter's and not the party's; a party that files twice has two numbers and one habit.
6. **Service.** Serve the respondent, who in a rooted matter is the keeper of the ground, not the person complained of — unless the person complained of is the keeper, in which case the party is advised to reconsider, the Faculty having one keeper and no spare.
7. **Answer.** The respondent answers within twenty-eight days on Form EA/6, or, where the respondent is a Faculty, within such longer period as its own teaching permits.
8. **Hearing.** The Faculty sits, ordinarily in the third week of term and ordinarily without the Dean. The Dean is consulted and approves nothing, and non-approval is not to be read as refusal.
9. **Order.** The Faculty enters an order, or declines to. A fee is not a warranty. The order is filed with the docket and does not travel with the party.

Step 6 and step 9 are the ones on which rooted matters fail: the keeper is usually the respondent, and parties read the order as the beginning of the matter while the Faculty files it as the end. Form 7B, the Declaration of Non-Interference with Time, is required with step 3, is not attached to this one, and has not been attached to any filing in this jurisdiction. The registry requested it twice, under EA/11 and EA/11a, at the counter for the fourth reading room, and no counter was found and no form issued. The Faculty's view, held since 1487, is that Form 7B and the chamber standing fourth in the north range are kept in the same place, and that the place is not in the building.

## 10. Finding

The Faculty, sitting in the third week of Hilary term 1501, finds: that the complainant is a cultivar of the common bean, sown the eleventh of May 1500 in bed nineteen of the north range and mark Q in the Day-Book; that its condition, of not germinating within earshot of laughter, was certified in 1500 under the seal of the Faculty of Herbology & Sentient Botany, and is a condition and not a refusal; that laughter was administered at the bed on the occasions and in the words of the table at section 11; that three of those occasions were administered without a valid licence, the licensed chamber being non-existent; that the licence HS/L-1500/41 is void, and the fee of 12 bells is not refunded, a fee not being a warranty; that the complainant was insulted, insult requiring only a hearer, and a hearer being present; that the complainant was not defamed, defamation requiring a body capable of holding a name, and the Faculty being unable to say what a bean's good name is worth where no bean will say; that no remedy lies, the injury being to a condition and not to a right; that the clerk who struck the licence is not charged, being unconfirmed, and is promoted; that the cultivar is not to be moved; and that the costs are taxed as at section 12. The Faculty further finds that the matter is not before the Dean, who was consulted and has not approved the finding.

## 13. Parties who cannot file

Under DE 5.5 / P48 1497, a familiar may be released only by a ritual, a fourteen-day notice, and the return of a small collar; where the familiar returns of its own accord, that work does not provide for it, and the Faculty has kept the file open since 1499. A bean that will not germinate has the same shape: it cannot be served, cannot be heard, cannot be dismissed, and continues to occupy the ground. The Faculty's practice in such files is to leave them open. The practice has been described as a policy. It is not a policy. It is a shelf. The shelf also holds EA 1.1 / G74 1493, whose ninety-ninth form is left blank for the case that has no form; this matter has no form and has been entered on HS/9.
