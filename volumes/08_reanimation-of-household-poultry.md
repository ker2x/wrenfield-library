## Abstract (as filed)

This proposal sets out a licensed method for the reanimation of household poultry, with a species table, a welfare schedule and an account of the first licensed case, for submission to the Faculty of Applied Necromancy (Provisional) and to the Dean of Ethics, Appeals & Paperwork, whose non-approval is noted at §10 and not required. The method rests on the Capitular Statute of 1274 and is observed as the goose at §3 requires. The file lacks a completed Form 7B, *Declaration of Non-Interference with Time*, as does every thesis in this Library.

## 1. The Problem, Stated Plainly and Then Not

The smallholder who keeps eleven hens and one cock is subject to three griefs: the winter cessation of laying, the fox, and the ordinary mortality of birds. The first is daylight, the second fences, the third everything. This proposal addresses the third, and only in respect of birds already dead, already owned, already named; a bird so taken is not stock but evidence.

A hen is worth 3 bells at Michaelmas and 3 bells 6 clipped in the hungry gap, and dies of a stoppage, a cough, the dog, or the frost on a bad perch. The yard then owes her feed for the nine days it takes her owner to accept the fact, which interval is this work's subject.

## 2. Standing, and the Statute That Does the Work

The Faculty holds its warrant under clause 19 of the Capitular Statute of 1274 [FN 1.1 / — 1274], and under no later instrument. Where the Statute speaks, the Faculty's ordinances do not; where it is silent, the Faculty may regulate, and has, at length, in a booklet from the porter for 2 bells.

A licence is required for every bird reanimated: not for one reanimated before 1274 and lawfully maintained since, nor for a goose. The exemption is not in the Faculty's gift, as the Faculty has said in writing, twice, at 2 bells a copy.

The author holds her D.Th. of 1497 and has taught the subject to eleven students, nine of whom passed and two are in husbandry. The word *Provisional* stands in the Faculty's charter, calendar, signage and stationery, and has stood there since 1458; it describes neither the Faculty's competence, examined annually, nor its curriculum, but the fact that the Faculty of Ethics, Appeals & Paperwork has never brought the charter to a vote.

## 3. The Goose: an Exemption in Law and Not in Magic

The goose is exempt under clause 14 of the Capitular Statutes of 1274 [FN 1.1 / — 1274]: a goose kept upon a tenement is a lawful witness in boundary and trespass, and its keeper has standing to speak for it. A witness cannot be maintained in a state of death at the suit of the party who keeps him. **The ground of the exemption is therefore tenancy**: the bird has standing in a court of law, and the Faculty may not hold a witness in its own cause. It is a legal exemption and not a magical one, and no rite, fee, indemnity or premium will obtain it. A goose that came back perfectly would be exempt, and one that came back not at all is exempt.

## 5. The First Licensed Reanimation, in Full: Hesta Bell and the Hen Called Marrow

The only bird reanimated under licence in this Faculty's history was a common hen of Hesta Bell's yard at Long Coombe, licensed on the 4th of November 1497 by Dr. Emeric Tallow, Reader in Minor Rites. The bird's name was **Marrow**. She died in the hen-house in the night of the 1st of November 1497, of a stoppage, aged four years and eleven days, and was named before she died and not for the purpose, as the annex at §10 requires.

The licence was read aloud in the yard on the morning of the 4th, before Hesta Bell, her son Willem, the neighbour Will Cobb, and the Inspector of Standing Stock, Mr. Thomas Beddoes. The reading took six minutes, in full and not in summary, Hesta Bell arguing that a summary is not a reading. Will Cobb said he had no objection to the bird and an objection to the smell, and asked that it be entered in the register; the Inspector entered it as *a note of expectation*.

The rite was performed in the byre at the eleventh hour, the hen-house being out of the question. It took nine minutes: spatula only, a second person taking the bird's weight and not holding the bird down, the Ordinary Word at the third minute in the reduced form for a bird under six pounds. The bird stood at the seventh minute and took the mash at the ninth.

The smell was applied on the day according to the Faculty's schedule. Days 1–3, warm iron: Will Cobb reported the stove that evening, and was told the byre was not the house. Days 4–11, straw over warm iron: the neighbour checked the chimney on the 8th and complained again on the 10th. Day 12, stable; Hesta Bell entered that the yard smelled as it did in the week Marrow died. From day 41, a third complaint the Inspector did not enter.

She was credited from the 5th of November 1497 to the 23rd of April 1498 with **one hundred and twenty-two useful working days**, eighty-one in the winter quarter; she laid forty-one eggs and sat nine days on a clutch that did not hatch, the Faculty having ruled in 1463 that a reanimated bird's futile sitting is still labour. On the 23rd of April she came to the doorway, stopped, and did not resume, dying the next morning, and Mr. Beddoes took the carcase to the lime pit. One hundred and twenty-two days were delivered against a fee of 6 bells and an inspection at 4 bells: the case succeeded at the ledger and failed at the door.

## 6. The Rite, in Nine Steps, Which the Reader Should Not Attempt

1. **The exemption check.** Where the bird is a goose, stop: it is a witness under clause 14 of the Statute of 1274. The check is the Inspector's; every failure of this Faculty has failed here, in the first minute.
2. **The statement of hatch.** Receive the owner's signed statement of the bird's hatch, name and death, in the owner's own hand.
3. **The three days.** Keep the dead bird in a clean hutch three days on its customary feed, with a warm mash on the third morning.
4. **The licence.** Read the licence aloud in full in the owner's yard, before the owner, the neighbour and the Inspector.
5. **The exclusion.** Clear the byre of living birds of the same species, and of children under the age of reason.
6. **The word.** Speak the Ordinary Word at the third minute, in the reduced form for a bird of six pounds and under, spatula in the right hand, left hand flat and empty.
7. **The wait.** Wait, without speech, to the ninth minute. Where the bird has not stood, discontinue; the fee is not refunded.
8. **The mash.** Offer the customary feed at the ninth minute; do not hold the bird.
9. **The rest.** Leave the bird an hour in which it is required to do nothing, with a perch within reach. The Faculty calls this *the rest*; the author prefers *the permission to stop*. A bird not permitted to stop will do so anyway, usually in the doorway.

The rite is not to be repeated on the same bird; the Faculty's reason is that the register holds no case.

## 7. The Licence AN/4, Reproduced in Full

Form AN/4 is unobtainable, as §12 records. The copy below was issued to Hesta Bell and kept in the forms press.

> ALDERWICK, in the County of the Nine Cloisters, in the Faculty of Applied Necromancy (Provisional), being the Faculty of Record in this matter. LICENCE AN/4, No. 1, issued under clause 19 of the Capitular Statutes of 1274, upon the application of the keeper, in triplicate, the first part retained, the second posted to the Inspector of Standing Stock, and the third kept with the bird: THIS LICENCE grants to Hesta Bell, of Long Coombe in the parish of Nine Meadows, smallholder, leave to maintain ONE BIRD, a common hen now named Marrow, in a state of reanimation, from the fourth day of November 1497 until the anniversary of the death of the said bird, and thereafter for one year upon each renewal. The fee is 6 bells per bird per annum, payable in silver, and is not abated by the bird's idleness, illness, sale, flight or second death. Renewal falls due on the anniversary of the death of the bird and not upon the anniversary of this rite, the difference being the whole of the Faculty's difficulty with the public. A licence lapses upon the second death of the bird, upon the sale of the bird, upon unexplained absence of the bird for seven days together, and upon the refusal of entry to the Inspector twice in one year. A licence is not transferable between birds and is not transferable between owners. The bird shall not be pledged, lent, or shown for money. The bird shall be permitted to stop. The keeper accepts liability for damage to person of 2 marks, to property of 3 marks, and to the feelings of a neighbour of 1 mark, and shall admit the Inspector at any reasonable hour. Given under the hand of Dr. Emeric Tallow, Reader in Minor Rites, this fourth day of November, in the year 1497.

## 9. The Objection of the Faculty of Divination, and the Reply

That Faculty has objected since 1496 and prints its objection at cost in its calendar; it is printed here in its own voice:

> The soul of the bird is elsewhere. It was measured elsewhere in 1474 by the Nine Meadows method and again in 1481 by the Rook equations, and it has not moved. What the rite recovers is therefore not the bird. It is a working likeness of the bird, kept in motion by the practitioner, which does the work of the bird while being nothing of the kind. The Faculty of Applied Necromancy calls this a bird because it is paid for a bird. We call it a puppet, we call the licence a fee for puppetry, and we decline to be reassured by the table at §4, which measures the puppet and not the hen.

The first part of the reply is arithmetic. The Faculty of Divination holds one hundred and eleven observations of the soul of the bird and none of the bird's labour, though it has published ninety-four papers upon the labour of the soul, which it cannot weigh. This Faculty holds one hundred and twenty-two documented days of work by Marrow. If the bird is a puppet, the puppet laid forty-one eggs, and the Faculty of Divination may take it up with the eggs.

The second part is the difference. A puppet is the practitioner's instrument and answers to the practitioner's hand. Marrow was required to stop, and stopped; took the mash when offered it; worked six days in seven and rested on the seventh without instruction; and came to the doorway on the 23rd of April and did not resume, though nothing in the rite required her to cease. The Faculty of Divination must therefore hold that the rite compels the bird to disoblige its owner — an odd account of a puppet.

## 10. The Ethics Annex, Which Is Longer Than the Method

**Before.** The bird is dead of natural causes or of the axe and of nothing else. The owner holds a signed statement of hatch in the owner's own hand. The bird has a name given before its death and not for the purpose, and is kept three days on its customary feed. No bird is taken from a neighbour's yard, taken in settlement of a debt, or reanimated for evidence; a bird so taken is forfeit to the Chapter as evidence and not stock.

**During.** The rite occupies nine minutes and requires two persons, one of whom takes the bird's weight; none holds the bird down. It is discontinued where the bird resists, the fee not being refunded, and is performed out of the hearing of a living bird of the same species. The bird is not taped, bound, drugged, flattered, or addressed as a person: the rule of 1489 is that the reanimated bird is a bird, and that compliments are a species of fraud.

**After.** The bird requires shelter from direct sun, a perch within reach, and an hour in which it is required to do nothing. The keeper shall not work it more than nine hours, nor more than six days in seven, shall not pledge or lend it, and shall permit the bird to stop, neither requiring nor requesting its return. The consent forms of the Faculty of Draconic Dentistry [DD 7.2 / O56 1502] are followed in clause order but not length, that Faculty holding sixteen leaves necessary for a dragon and eight lines a hen.

**The Dean's part.** Dean Ambrose Quill was consulted on the annex in Hilary term. His office replied that the Dean does not approve proposals of this kind, not that he disapproves of them. A non-approval that is not a disapproval is an absence of a bar, and the annex stands; the non-approval is noted here and is **not required**.

## 11. The Goose Reanimated Anyway, 1498: a Failure with a Fee

Clause 14 was first broken at the Michaelmas of 1498, at Long Coombe, by a hedge-necromancer of the Hanging Field, one Wolferan Skell, who held no warrant, kept no register, and charged four bells for a rite this Faculty has never licensed. The bird was a goose, unrecorded in any lawful register and recalled as Bellweather, kept by the widow Margery Pring, who had asked for a hen and was told that a goose was a hen of a larger kind — the species of statement the check at §6, step 1, exists to prevent. Skell performed the rite on the 29th of September and did not perform the check, on his own evidence, because he understood the exemption to be *a form*.

The bird answered; the rate at §4 is 12 per cent, and this bird was in the twelve. It stood and walked the yard from the Michaelmas of 1498 to the Michaelmas of 1502, the annual inspection having found it each year under the heading *stock of the yard, ordinary*: the Inspector inspects the byre and not the bird. It did not lay and did not work, and the widow told the Chapter in 1502 that she had grown fond of it. The consequence is entered in the ledger under the heading *the second bird, unlicensed*, in the Under-Librarian's hand: *not ours*.

The penalty for maintaining a witness in a state of death at the suit of its keeper is 9 marks under clause 14, to which the Faculty added the unpaid licence at 6 bells for each of the four years: **10 marks 4 bells** in all. Damages to the neighbour Ailwin Rudd, who had claimed the bird's presence in a boundary dispute and lost, were 2 marks 8 clipped bells. Skell was fined 7 marks, the widow Pring 3 marks 4 bells.

The bird's status afterwards is the part of the case the Faculty cites. Being unowned it could not be licensed; being unlicensed it could not lawfully be kept; being a witness it could not be taken by the Faculty; and it was taken nonetheless, on the Chapter's warrant, on the sixteenth day. The widow paid her share in grain; Skell's 7 marks were never paid, he having left the parish, and the debt was written off against the contingency, the only account in this University never audited.

No licence has issued, and the Faculty remains Provisional.
