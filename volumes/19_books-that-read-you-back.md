## Abstract (as filed)

This report concerns the self-removing class of the Restricted Stack: volumes which, read, read the reader. It records nineteen dated incidents; four categories of reader response, being Relief, Indifference, Injury and Recognition; and the finding that the class is accurate, which is the difficulty. It raises, and does not settle, the consent question where the reader is the one read, and recommends the class be left alone. Form 7B, required of every thesis and missing from every thesis here, is not required of a report.

## 1. This Reissue, and Why There Is One

The first issue was entered at the desk on 6 vi 1503 in sixty copies and withdrawn on 14 ii 1504, by its author, on the ground that it printed only the recommendation, which cannot be assessed without the incidents and the counts. This reissue restores what was omitted. The Under-Librarian has stamped it, which is not approving it, and has asked that this be minuted.

## 2. Terms of Reference

The report was commissioned in the ninth month of 1502, after the fourth incident of that year, and filed in the fifth month of 1503, for reasons at §5. Its terms, as minuted by the Deputy Under-Librarian, are to identify the class, to count the incidents, and to say nothing the Stack cannot support. It does not concern the sentient class, which argues and can be answered; a self-removing volume agrees, and cannot.

## 3. The Class, Defined, with the Holdings Surveyed

A volume is of the self-removing class if, opened and read, it removes something from the reader and keeps it. The Classification of 1388 has no letter for it. Nine volumes are held, the shelf being presses two, three, seven, nine and eleven of the Stack; seven are given below, the remaining two being unread and unclassified. Four are confirmed, two suspected, one disputed: the disputed because the volume holds that it read the reader and the reader the reverse.

The Conservator surveyed the shelf in 1503 and 1504: leather sound in eight volumes, the ninth recased; the shelf elevation at plate 1 drawn in 1502 from a measurement taken at the door; and one shelf in that plate drawn empty. I have written to the Registry. It has not answered.*

## 4. The Four Classes of Report, and Their Counts

The desk classifies every reading by what the reader did with it, not by what the volume said. The classification is a form with four boxes and one instruction.

| Class | Count | One example | Did the reader consent? | Was the reading accurate? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Relief | 9 | 2 vi 1503, the doubled grievance | one of nine; eight read unasked | eight confirmed; one false |
| Indifference | 5 | 28 iv 1503, the undeclared jinx | two of five | five of five confirmed |
| Injury | 4 | 11 v 1503, the reader's own grievance | four of four | four of four confirmed |

Recognition is the smallest class and the most expensive: Injury is settled in marks and bells, while Recognition costs the reader the ground he was standing on. Of the nineteen readings, eighteen have been confirmed against the register, the readers, or the volumes, which is the finding of this report, and the difficulty.

## 5. The Recognition Incident, in Full

The reader was the Deputy Under-Librarian. The reading was on 19 ix 1502, at desk two, between the first and the third bell, in the presence of the reading-room clerk, who was asked to remain and not to take a note. The volume was the desk copy of this report's own shelf-mate: supplied by the binder as a courtesy, unaccessioned, and left there by the Deputy Under-Librarian, who had written it.

The register enters the reading in the volume's voice and not the reader's: that the reader had written a report on a class he had not consulted; that he had counted the incidents by reading the register and not the volumes; that he had put Recognition last and counted it at one, a second case obliging him to declare it; and that he would agree with every word of this.

The reading was verified three ways. The register bore out the volume's four claims, including the date on which the count of Recognition was last altered, the afternoon of 18 ix 1502. The count: the reading placed a second Recognition in the twelfth month of 1502, and the desk could not find it and could not find that it was absent, and has since altered the count twice without entering the alteration. The plate: the Conservator established from the watermark that the shelf elevation was drawn before the reading.

Two colleagues were told, the desk's practice being that a reading of this class is reported to two and minuted by one. Mrs. Judith Cray, Conservator, attended within the hour and returned the volume with the note at §3, which does not mention the reading; the reading-room clerk on duty was entered as a mark of a cross and a number, the practice for a witness not confirmed in post. Neither was told the content; the Conservator has since been told, and asks to be told nothing further.

The Deputy Under-Librarian's own entry in the register reads, in full: *Nineteenth reading. Reader: myself. Volume in class. Agreed with it. Count unchanged. Reissue ordered.* The entry was made the same evening, in the register and not in his private book.

## 6. The Volume's Report on the Reader

The document is the reading itself, entered in the class register on 19 ix 1502 and first printed here. The register is kept at desk two and is not to be taken into the Stack.

> That the reader has written a report on a class he has not consulted, and has called it interim, which is a word that costs nothing and has lasted two years.
> That he has counted the incidents by reading the register rather than the volumes, and that a man who counts his own slips and calls the total a finding has performed a division and not an enquiry.
> That he has put Recognition last and counted it at one for a reason that is not arithmetic: a second case would have obliged him to declare it in the text and not in the margin.
> That he has drafted a declaration of willingness to be read and has not signed it himself, having nothing to declare.
> That he will agree with every word of this, and will not amend the count, and will reissue the report with the table complete. The reissue is the agreement.

The Conservator's note, on the guard at the register's front: *Leaves foxed at the gutter; the entry legible; the ink identical in the first clause and the last; the hand not the Deputy's. I have authenticated the paper. I make no finding on the reading, and I do not read at this desk.*

## 8. The Committee's Objection, and the Reply

The Library Committee considered the class in the eleventh month of 1504, on the Conservator's condition report and not on this report, which it had not read. The objection is printed unaltered, the reply in the same type.

> The Committee does not accept the desk's recommendation. The Committee has before it a class of volumes which, on the desk's own showing, remove something from every reader who opens them; which have injured four members of this Library and one member of the public; which cannot be cross-examined; and against which no form lies and no licence is issued. The Committee notes that the desk recommends these volumes be left alone, that it has left them alone since 1502, and that since 1502 there have been nineteen readings and four injuries. The Committee notes further that it has not read the class and that two of its members do not propose to. The Committee directs that the class be destroyed at once, by fire, in the yard, in the presence of the Conservator; that the class register be closed and deposited with the Registry; and that a form be drafted for the future, in case the class recurs.

The reply was minuted on 20 xi 1504.

The direction has two limbs. First: the volumes are the evidence. The desk's account of what a volume read in a reader exists in three places — the register, the reader's recollection, and the volume. The register is the desk's own hand; the recollection is the least reliable instrument in the building; and if the volumes are burned, the only record left will be a report by the man they read. That is a filing decision, and this Library made one in 1487, with a room.

To the second: the Committee's objection is not that the volumes are dangerous but that they are accurate, and the direction is a direction to stop being told. It had the opportunity to dispute the accuracy of a single reading and disputed none. If it holds them true and not to be said, the remedy is not fire but a change to what the desk does next, set out at §11. The desk will not carry out the direction, the ninth it has not carried out.

## 9. Injuries, and What They Cost

Four readers have been injured. The scale was set in 1503 with the Bursar, who holds a scale revised to be admitted. Marlow Quist's case is the desk's named failure, and the case from which the scale was set; the Conservator has entered it as the precedent, and the desk has no form for striking a precedent.

Quist, Assistant Conservator, at press two on 3 xi 1502, recasing the third edition of *Grooming Your Centaur* under the Conservator's standing order that a volume be read before it is recased, so that its errors may be noted on the guard. He read it in the course of the work, and it read in him that he had known the answer at a first reading in the previous month: that he had groomed a centaur at the Nine Meadows fair in the ninth month, in the fourth hour, without waiting for it, and had entered the matter nowhere. That was accurate, and the desk has verified the fair, the hour and the animal against the fair's gate-book, where Quist's name stands.

The injury was not the fact but the naming. Quist could describe the animal, the fair, the hour and the weather, and for three days could not say the word *hoof*, the account stopping at the syllable. The Conservator treated him in the reading chair at the south window, on the practice for handlers, and he named the subject on the fourth day, taken through the alphabet backwards, which she records as the only method that worked. He returned to the press on the fifth day and recased the volume, after the reading and not before.

The compensation was assessed at 9 marks 4 bells: 6 marks for the lost work, 2 marks 4 bells for the treatment, and 1 mark entered by the Bursar as the fee for the assessment itself.

## 10. Procedure, from the Desk to the Stack

The following procedure is in force, and the desk asks the reader to attempt it in order.

1. The request is taken at desk two, by the Deputy Under-Librarian, or in his absence by no one. The desk does not accept a request by a junior, by letter, or by card.
2. The register is consulted before the shelf. If the title is in the class, the volume is not issued; it is read at the desk.
3. The reader is given the notice, read aloud by the desk and signed by the desk and not by the reader, the reader's signature being the consent the desk cannot obtain.
4. The window of the Stack is closed and remains closed. If it is found open, the reading is postponed a day and the Under-Librarian informed in writing.
5. The volume is brought from the press by the Deputy Under-Librarian, carried flat, spine down, and placed with the fore-edge to the reader's left.
6. The reading proceeds. The desk does not prompt, does not time, and does not close the volume. If the reader stops, the volume is left open for a hundred counted breaths and closed from the spine.
7. The reading is entered in the class register that day, in the volume's voice and in the reader's, distinguished by a rule in the margin and not by a difference of hand.
8. If the reader shows Injury, the reading stops and the reader is attended before the volume is touched — the person, then the register, then the volume.
9. The volume is returned to the Stack by hand, on foot, by the Deputy Under-Librarian, not by a junior, and not after the third bell. The press is locked; the key is the desk's.
10. No part of this procedure is to be printed in the reissue — this clause printed here at the Conservator's request and against the desk's order.

## 11. Recommendation, and What It Costs to Leave Alone

The class be left alone, in the press, closed, no window opened in the Stack, and the desk to record and not to investigate. The recommendation was made in the twelfth month of 1502, accepted, and has not been carried out, the volumes being read; it is re-made here with the arithmetic the first issue omitted: nineteen readings, four injuries, seven consents of nineteen, eighteen confirmations of nineteen, two refusals of the Herbology merger, one unanswered memorandum to the Faculty of Ethics, and not one reading the class has withdrawn.

The ground is this. A volume of this class may be corrected if inaccurate and prosecuted if malicious; these are not inaccurate and have shown no malice. They are accurate, and accuracy at this Library is not a charge. The alternative is the Committee's: it would cost the evidence, the count and the eighteen confirmations, and leave the desk with a report and a mark, which is the state of the Registry's clock. The desk asks that this be read with §§5 and 8, where its own interest is declared, counted, and not deducted.

## Appendix A: Consent, Where the Reader Is the One Being Read

The reader consents to read. It is not established that the reader consents to be read: he is not told before, the volumes do not ask, and the desk has no form for the asking, the Library's forms for consent being licences, issued to a person for an act, none of which fits an act done by the object read. The Deputy Under-Librarian drafted one in 1502, a *Declaration of Willingness to Be Read*, and took it to the Faculty of Ethics, which declined to register it on two grounds. First, a declaration made before the reading cannot bind the reader after it, the reading's content being unknown at signature. Second, the volumes cannot be bound by a declaration they have not signed. Both grounds are good. The desk has since proceeded on a notice instead of a declaration, which is worse in law, better in practice, and is quoted at §10, clause 3. The Faculty of Ethics has had before it since 1502 the question whether a true statement may constitute an injury; the Dean has approved nothing. Form 7B, the *Declaration of Non-Interference with Time*, is missing from every thesis here and would not help, being a declaration against time and not against persons, and being in any case unavailable, its only completed copy having been filed with the fourth reading room in 1487.
