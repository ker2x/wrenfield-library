## Abstract (as filed)

This report sets out the findings of the Visiting Committee appointed in Michaelmas 1497 to examine the alphabetical shelving of the Bramblegate Lending Library, with a table of casualties, the reply of the Librarian, and the argument that an alphabetical shelf destroys the shelf-mate and with it the citation. The file lacks a completed Form 7B, *Declaration of Non-Interference with Time*. The recommendation is not recorded in this report; the Committee records that it had one.

## 1. The Committee, Its Warrant, and Its Fee

The Visiting Committee was appointed by the Faculty of Ethics, Appeals & Paperwork on the 3rd of October 1497, upon a motion of the Committee on Standing Matters, to visit the Bramblegate Lending Library and report. It consists of the author, who signs; one clerk in holy orders, who did not attend; and one member of the Committee on Standing Matters, who attended and would not sign.

The warrant authorises the Committee to examine the shelves, count the volumes, read the visitors' book and question readers, and forbids it to move or reshelve any volume. It was read aloud at the start of each of the fourteen visits. The fee is 2 bells a day each for the two members attending, and 4 clipped bells for the ferry.

The report was filed as a thesis and lacks a Form 7B, *Declaration of Non-Interference with Time*; the Committee did not interfere with any time, having had no occasion to, which is not the same as having declared it.

## 2. What the Alphabetical Shelf Is

An alphabetical shelf is a library in which a work is found by the first letter of its title and by nothing else: *How to Lose a Familiar Politely* stands in the H's beside *A Study on Harmless Curses*.

The consequence is single and severe. Wrenfield is arranged by Faculty and shelf, so that a work is found by what it is about and its neighbours are its colleagues [JM 6.1 / V35 1494; HC 1.1 / M89 1496; AC 118.7 / T45 1487]. Bramblegate is arranged by first letter, so that the neighbours of a work are whatever else begins the same way. A reader who wants a subject, or half a name, or the work next to the one he has finished, has only the desk, the hand-list holding about 1,050 titles, its last entry of 1461.

Three things the alphabetical shelf is not. It is *not an index*, for an index points to the shelf from outside it, and here the alphabet points only to itself. It is *not a catalogue*, for Bramblegate's hand-list describes a third of the collection and the rest is described in conversation. It is *not a classification*, which places a work by what it is and so claims that these two belong together. Sallow's Classification of 1388 places every work by its Faculty; the alphabet places every work by its spelling. Only one is an argument.

## 3. The Bramblegate Shelves, Described

The Library occupies one room, 41 feet by 19, with a river window at the east, and holds some 3,100 volumes, all shelved by the first letter of the title. It is clean and warm, which the Committee records with regret. The system has two rules upon a card at the desk:

1. A work is shelved under the first letter of its title, the articles *A*, *An* and *The* being disregarded.
2. Where two works share a letter, they are arranged by the second letter, and so onward.

The card is undated; rule 2 was added in 1489, after a complaint. The Committee asked to see the complaint and was shown a shelf. The alphabet produces nineteen sections: seventeen letters in use, of which Q and X are empty and kept swept, and one anomalous section holding about sixty volumes whose titles begin with a numeral, a foreign article, or a mark, and which stand in a cupboard beside the desk.

## 4. The Shelf-Mate, the Citation, and the Discipline

A citation is not a title but a position: *Vale, Advanced Guide to Jinxes, 4th ed., 1494, p. 88* tells where a work stands in the argument of a discipline and where it stands upon a shelf [FN 6.1 / S25 1388]. At Wrenfield the shelf-mate of a work is its neighbour in the Classification: the jinx beside the harmless curse, the curse beside the pollen pathology, the severed familiar beside the familiar not yet severed [JM 6.1 / V35 1494; HC 1.1 / M89 1496; AC 118.7 / T45 1487; DE 5.5 / P48 1497]. A reader who pulls one down pulls down its argument.

Under the alphabet the shelf-mate is a matter of a first letter. The *Advanced Guide to Jinxes* is set beside the *Advanced Guide to Knitting*, and *A Study on Harmless Curses* stands eleven shelves away. The citation still resolves, the work being where the desk says it is; but the shelf has ceased to be a claim about the discipline and become a claim about spelling. A shelf with no argument in it cannot be appealed, and a discipline whose citations cannot be appealed is a correspondence.

## 6. The Case of Aldith Rennick, Told in Full

*The reader.* Aldith Rennick is a midwife of Low Town, licensed by the Guild of Familiar Keepers, and a Bramblegate borrower since 1491.

*What she needed, and why.* The cat had belonged to a household in the Tenterfields which had gone into mourning and put it out; it came to Mrs. Rennick and stayed. By the last week of October it had installed itself in the room where she attended a lying-in and could not be moved. She required the book about how to let a familiar go — the notice, the fourteen days, the words, the small collar [DE 5.5 / P48 1497].


*What she did first.* On the 2nd of November she crossed the ferry and asked the desk for the Pell book by title. The desk told her it was upon the H shelf. She found *Horses*. She asked again; the desk explained rule 2 and the second letter. The work was in fact there, at the fourth shelf-end, under *How*; she did not find it, and did not ask again.

*What she did instead.* She crossed to Alderwick and applied at the Wrenfield desk, where the Pell is held at DE 5.5, and consulted it forty minutes, taking an extract of the notice and the words; told to return for the rest, she returned on the 4th. In that fortnight the cat crossed the river five times.

*What it cost her.* On the 24th of November she performed the release without the remainder of the text. The Guild of Familiar Keepers heard the matter on the 4th of December and found it malformed: the notice had been given in eleven days, not fourteen, and the collar returned to the household rather than the animal, which its schedule of 1389 treats as a disposal. She was fined 1 mark 4 bells and paid it on the 9th of January 1498, having also paid 6 clipped bells in ferry crossings and lost twelve days.

*The visitors' book.* On the 9th of November the desk entered her formal reproach:

> *A. Rennick, midwife, Low Town. Wishes it recorded that she did not lose her familiar, that she was sent to the Hors shelf, that the book was upon the said shelf and not found by her, and that she has paid the ferry twice. Says the shelves are clean.*

The word *Hors* is the desk's spelling, not Mrs. Rennick's. The final sentence the Committee takes as the whole of Bramblegate's defence, and the reason this report has no recommendation.

## 7. The Evidence of Readers, Quoted

Thirty-one readers were questioned. Two quotations are set down.

> "I asked for the *Pollen Curse*. I was given *Politics*. I have the curse. I did not want the politics." — a reader of the second class, the 19th of October 1497.

> "Under the alphabet the neighbour of the *Guide to Jinxes* is the *Guide to Knitting*. I have read both. I recommend the knitting." — a reader of the third class, the 21st of October 1497.

No reader complained of the alphabet itself; every complaint was of a journey to a shelf that did not contain what had been promised.

## 8. The Objection: Miss Coote's Case for the Alphabet

The Committee put its findings to Miss Verity Coote, Librarian of Bramblegate, on the 22nd of November 1497. Her case is that a lending library is not a scholar library; that her three hundred and forty readers ask for a book by name, a title being the only part of a book that travels; and that she has no Faculties, one room and one desk, the alphabet being the one arrangement nobody must be taught.

The reply, of the 21st of January 1498, is quoted in full.

> Dear Mr. Lint, — Thank you for your report, which I have read twice, and for the second copy, which I have sent to the branch at Lower Bramble, where it has been read with interest, the branch having shelves of its own and the same two rules. Our readers find the books they ask for by asking for them, which is why the desk is staffed from the first bell to the sixth bell and has been since 1481, and why I have never found it necessary to teach a reader the second letter. I am glad the Committee found the shelves clean; I clean them myself on the fourth day. I have shown your table to my assistant, who says the table is fair, and who asks whether the midwife of Low Town was offered the Hors shelf twice, which she was. The Committee is welcome at any time, and the ferry is not our affair. I have considered your question about the citation and I find that a lending library's citations are its readers, who come back. — Yours sincerely, V. Coote, Librarian.

It mentions neither the alphabet, the shelf-mate nor the eleven shelves, nor the loan request, which she had refused twice, politely.

## 9. The Reply to the Objection: The Shelf-Mate, the Citation, and the Discipline

Miss Coote's case ought to be granted at three points, and is. Bramblegate is a lending library, and the Committee's mission was to a lending library, not a Faculty. No reader complained of the system, and the system is teachable in one sentence, where Wrenfield's is not.

*On the shelf-mate.* The Committee does not ask Bramblegate to move a volume. No reader is harmed by the jinxes standing away from the curses; he is harmed when the desk names a shelf that does not deliver, and that harm is curable in place, by carrying the hand-list forward and printing the second letter on the card. What can be restored is not the shelf-mate but the desk's promise.

*On the citation.* Miss Coote is right that a lending library's citations are its readers, and wrong that this settles the matter, a lending library lending to scholars as well. Eleven of the thirty-one readers questioned were University members, and seven of the nine casualties were suffered on University business.

*On the discipline.* If a shelf carries no argument, a scholar trained upon such shelves learns to cite works and not positions, and a literature so cited ceases to be cumulative. *Advanced Guide to Jinxes* has three readers a year at Bramblegate; here it is cited in eleven theses [JM 6.1 / V35 1494].

The Committee did not recommend the abandonment of the alphabet; it recommended four clauses, to be moved under item 5, which were not moved.

## 10. Procedure: How a Library Is Inspected Without Rearranging It

The method of the visiting committee, numbered, so that the same inspection may be performed at any library.

1. Obtain the warrant in writing and read it aloud at the threshold of every visit. A committee that has not read its warrant is a party, not a committee.
2. Copy the card at the desk before speaking.
3. Count the shelf-ends, a shelf-end being the unit of search. Bramblegate has 176.
4. Count the volumes without taking them down: 2,050 were unlisted.
5. Sample the shelf-ends for order: at each, verify the first and last title.
6. For each casualty, ask the reader to lead the Committee to the shelf and to say what he asked for and was given.
7. Take the shelf-end in writing, in the reader's words and in the desk's, and do not adjudicate upon the day he speaks.
8. Read the visitors' book entire, and copy every entry touching a request, a failure or a fine.
9. Interview staff at the desk, in the hour of work: a closed room produces an account of the system, never its practice.
10. Time the searches by the library's own clock.
11. Put no volume anywhere. If a volume must be moved to be read, mark the shelf-end as disturbed and record it.
12. Write the recommendation before the last visit.

The Committee performed all twelve steps. It recommends step 12 to the Committee on Standing Matters, which did not perform it.

## 11. The Two Systems Compared

A sample of twenty named titles was drawn from Bramblegate's own hand-list in the order in which they stood upon it, and for each the Committee recorded the shelf, the time required for a reader who did not know it to find the volume, and the nearest volume there and here. No party was sent to a shelf on which the volume did not stand, 214 of Bramblegate's volumes being out.

*Table II. Twenty paired searches, Wrenfield against Bramblegate, October 1497.*

| Test | Wrenfield | Bramblegate | Wrenfield: time taken | Bramblegate: time taken | Works found |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Named title, has call number | JM 6.1 / V35 1494 | *Advanced Guide to Jinxes* | 40 sec | 3 min 10 sec | 20 / 20 both |
| Named title, has title only | JM 6.1 / V35 1494 | *Advanced Guide to Jinxes* | 3 min 40 sec | 3 min 10 sec | 18 / 20; 20 / 20 |
| Title half-remembered | by Faculty | by first letter | 6 min 10 sec | 12 min 30 sec | 12 / 20; 4 / 20 |
| Subject, not a title | shelf-mate | none | 4 min 10 sec | not tried | 11 / 20; 0 / 20 |
| Work arguing with a known one | 2 shelf-ends | 19 sections | 5 min 20 sec | 16 min 40 sec | 10 / 20; 1 / 20 |
| Shelf-mate consulted on purpose | 3 within 20 ft | 3 within 19 shelves | 1 min 30 sec | 21 min 10 sec | 20 / 20; 2 / 20 |
| Reader's first journey | 3 wrongly found | 9 wrongly found | — | — | 17 / 20; 11 / 20 |
| Return visit required | 2 of 20 | 8 of 20 | 3 days | 6 days | 18 / 20; 12 / 20 |
| Mean of all searches | — | — | 4 min 23 sec | 10 min 46 sec | 116; 70 of 180 |
| Search abandoned | 0 of 20 | 6 of 20 | — | — | — |

The Committee draws three conclusions. The alphabet is not slower for the reader who knows the title; it is faster by 30 seconds, which is recorded against the Committee's own argument. It is slower by 2.5 times for the reader who knows what he wants but not what it is called, which is the reader the University exists to produce. And the search is abandoned six times in twenty there, never here.

## 12. The Cost of the Visit, and the Item Not Reached

The recommendation stood at item 5 of the standing orders. The agenda of the 14th of December 1497 stood: 1, the minutes; 2, the ferry; 3, the casualties; 4, the motion to consult the Committee on Standing Matters; 5, the recommendation. Item 4 was carried and required a reference to a body meeting in Hilary; the hour being late, the meeting adjourned, and item 5 was not reached. It has not been reached since. The recommendation is therefore not recorded here; the Committee records that it had one, in four clauses, at Appendix B.

## 13. Note on Sources, and What This Report Does Not Know

The Committee's sources are the warrant of the 3rd of October 1497; the card at the desk; the hand-list; the visitors' book of the Bramblegate Lending Library, 1481–1497; the day-book; the Guild of Familiar Keepers' finding of the 4th of December 1497. It does not know how many readers the alphabet has cost since 1481, there being no register of a search that fails. The ten casualties in Table I are the ten that somebody was angry enough to report, and the Committee thinks them a minority.

## Appendix B. The Recommendation of the Committee, as Drafted for Item 5, and Not Moved

*In the author's hand, dated the 29th of November 1497.*

1. That the Bramblegate Lending Library print rule 2 upon the card in the same size of letter as rule 1, and add a third: that a reader sent to a shelf who does not find the work there be told the second letter.
2. That the hand-list be carried forward from 1461 to the present year, at the expense of the Wrenfield Library.
3. That the said library keep a column in the visitors' book for the outcome of a search.
4. That this Committee be discharged, and that no further committee be appointed to Bramblegate for seven years, the Library having been inspected and the shelves being clean.

## Apparatus
