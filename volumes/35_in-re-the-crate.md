# In re the Crate
### A Memorandum upon the Deposit of 1494, the Claim filed upon Form AN/4, and the Question Whether the Matter Is Before Me (It Is Not)

- **Call number:** EA 6.1 / Q55 1504
- **Author:** Ambrose Quill, Dean of the Faculty of Ethics, Appeals & Paperwork
- **Faculty:** Ethics, Appeals & Paperwork
- **Degree sought:** none. A memorandum, bound as a volume at the Dean's instruction, which he has declined to confirm
- **Advisor:** none · **Second reader:** Professor Emeritus Q. Ashgrove-Vane, who returned it unread
- **Imprint:** Alderwick: Wrenfield Press for the University, 1504
- **Edition:** Reissue, corrected and extended; the Dean has authorised no impression, and not forbidden this one
- **Accession:** Wrenfield Accessions 1504.23
- **Collation:** 57 numbered paragraphs, no plates
- **Condition:** Unmarked, unread, and the only volume in this Library without a pencil note, a fact the Library has verified twice
- **Form 7B:** *Declaration of Non-Interference with Time*. Not filed; the Dean has never required one, and does not propose to begin.

## Abstract (as filed)

This memorandum concerns a crate deposited in 1494, a manifest describing one octavo volume of a self-help character, the lapse of unclaimed deposits under the Capitular Statutes of 1274, and a claim upon Form AN/4 in the name of a person the Registry cannot establish. It sets out the instrument clause by clause, the deposit and its four countings, the claim particular by particular, the custody, three precedents, and the objection. The Dean's conclusion is that the matter is not before him. No Declaration of Non-Interference with Time (Form 7B) is filed; the Dean has never required one, and does not propose to begin.

## Part I — The Matter Referred

**§1.** On the ninth of July, 1504, the Registry referred to me a claim against a deposit. The claim is filed upon Form AN/4; the deposit is a crate. I have considered the papers, of which there are thirty-one.

**§2.** The question is not who owns the crate, which nobody has asked. The question is whether the matter is before me. It is not. My reasons follow, and are the first reasons I have written. I decide nothing upon the crate, the claim, or the ownership of either.

**§3.** I am told that in thirty-one years I have approved nothing, and that this memorandum is therefore an event, the Registry having entered it under the word *event*. It is not an approval but a refusal to act, at length, filed in a different press.

**§4.** The papers comprise the claim; the manifest of 1494; four countings in the Under-Librarian's hand; the Registry's covering note; a pencil reminder of 1491, which I reach at §40; and twenty-four sheets of correspondence, twenty-two of them the Registry's.

## Part II — The Instrument: the Capitular Statutes of 1274, Clause by Clause

**§5.** The instrument is the Capitular Statutes of 1274, held in this Library at **FN 1.1**, in force from their making and not since revised. I set out the clauses bearing upon this matter, 17 to 24, with the condition each imposes and my finding whether it is satisfied.

| clause | the clause, in substance | the condition it imposes | satisfied |
|---|---|---|---|
| 17 | a deposit taken into the Library's custody is entered in the register on the day of receipt | entry on the day, by the receiving officer | yes; entered 3 March 1494, in the Under-Librarian's hand |
| 18 | a deposit is described in the register by its manifest and nothing else | that the register go no further than the manifest | yes; it repeats the manifest and adds "closed, not sealed; ring present; no device" |
| 19 | a deposit unclaimed for eleven years lapses to the finder | eleven years unclaimed, and a finder | not yet; the eleventh year is 1505, and the finder is not in dispute |
| 20 | the finder is the officer in custody at the lapse | custody at the moment of lapse | yes; the Under-Librarian, unless he dies or retires |
| 21 | a claim upon a lapsed deposit is made upon Form AN/4, under the claimant's hand, with 5 bells | a person, a hand, a form, and 5 bells | no; hand and form given, fee promised for 1506, person not established |
| 22 | the lapse is certified by the officer to whom the deposit does not belong | an officer, not the finder, who certifies | the clause is a solecism, and is treated at §6 to §8 |
| 23 | no deposit is opened before the lapse, save by the owner on proof | that the crate remain closed until 1505 | yes; closed for ten years and five months |
| 24 | the register is produced to the Chapter once in seven years | production in 1491, 1498, 1505 | yes; produced in 1498; the 1505 production is entered, though not yet written |

**§6.** Clause 17 gives me no difficulty; the register was kept. Clause 18 is the clause on which the Registry relies for its contention that the crate may not be described as a manual. It directs what the register shall say, not what the thing is; a register forbidden to go beyond the manifest is under a discipline, not a deposit under a description.

**§7.** Clause 19 is the operative clause and is not in dispute, the only proposition on which the Registry and the Under-Librarian agree. Clause 20 makes the finder the officer in custody. The Registry argues the finder is the Faculty; I rejected that in 1495 and reject it here, a finder being a person and a Faculty a body, and a body cannot find what it cannot lift.

**§8.** Clause 22 is the solecism. It requires the lapse to be certified by the officer to whom the deposit does not belong, which on clauses 19 and 20 is every officer but the Under-Librarian. It does not name me, describe my office, or provide how the officer is chosen. The Faculty has read it for a hundred and thirty years as requiring a second officer; I have read it as requiring nothing. I do not say the clause is void. I say it names me for no purpose it states.

**§9.** The Registry construes the clause by practice and counts thirty-one instances. I have had the register counted again: the count is twenty-nine, four of deposits that never existed, one of the same deposit twice, in 1411 and 1412, the deposit being a goose. A statute requiring a thing to be done by a person it does not identify, in a manner it does not prescribe, at a time it does not fix, binds nobody; and a construction resting on twenty-nine instances, one of which is a goose, is not a constitution but a custom.

## Part III — The Deposit

**§10.** The crate was received on the third of March, 1494, at eleven in the forenoon, at the north door, by the Under-Librarian, alone because the Deputy was at the counter.

**§11.** The carrier declined to give a name, and is described in the register in six words: "a man, middle years, wet to the knee." He wore no badge and named no sender. He said the fee was paid and he would not be back. He was not back, and the porters do not remember him.

**§12.** The manifest was opened at the door, and is the only part ever opened. It is a single sheet in a clerk's hand, describing the contents in nine words: "one volume, octavo, boards, self-help, complete, no plates." The claim agrees with it except in the word *complete*.

**§13.** Nobody signed for the crate. The manifest bears no signature of the carrier or the Under-Librarian, but only the Under-Librarian's name at the foot in his own hand, written, he tells me, so that the register would have something to say.

**§14.** The crate is deal, banded with iron, and measures one foot four inches by eleven inches by nine. It is closed and not locked, having no hasp and no mark of one removed. The nails are not the maker's, being later and of a different head.

**§15.** The register adds four words to the manifest: "closed, not sealed; ring present; no device." The Under-Librarian wrote them at the door, in the wet, and will not explain them to me or the Registry.

**§16.** The crate has been counted four times by the Under-Librarian, who counts it by lifting one corner and reading the register, that being the only description of it that may lawfully be made.

| counting | date | how made | the Under-Librarian's record | witnesses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the first | 6 March 1494 | corner lifted; register read | "one crate, closed; one volume by the manifest; weight of a volume and a half" | none |
| the second | 2 January 1498 | corner lifted; register read; nails examined | "one crate, closed; nails not the maker's; weight of a volume and a half" | the Deputy Under-Librarian |
| the third | 14 August 1501 | corner lifted; register read; weighed on the meal-scales | "one crate, closed; 14 pounds 6 ounces; the manifest says one volume" | the Conservator and the porter |
| the fourth | 1 April 1504 | corner lifted; register read; not weighed | "one crate, closed; the hour not recorded; I did not weigh it; I will not weigh it again" | the Deputy Under-Librarian and a reader, unnamed in the register |

**§17.** The first counting was made three days after receipt, in obedience to a standing instruction of 1374 that a deposit be counted within the week. The second was made on the first day of the year in which the Chapter was to produce the register under clause 24, which the Under-Librarian says is the reason, not a coincidence.

**§18.** The third counting is the only one at which the crate was weighed, and it was weighed because the Faculty of Agricultural Curses complained that it obstructed the draught at the door. The weight is 14 pounds 6 ounces; an octavo of the character described weighs between one and two pounds, according to the Conservator, who weighed six of them in 1502.

**§19.** The fourth counting was made on the first of April, 1504, the afternoon on which the fourth reading room was booked twice, and the Under-Librarian has never explained why he counted it that day rather than another.

**§20.** The one thing about this deposit never explained is the ring. There is a ring on the lid: a clean circle two inches across, in the wood, of the kind a seal makes when the wax is lifted off and kept. The Under-Librarian saw it and wrote it down, and has been asked about it five times, twice by the Registry and three times by me, answering always in the same words: "It was there. I wrote it there. I have nothing to add, and will not remove it."

**§21.** The Conservator examined the ring in 1502, and reports that it is older than the nails; that no wax, resin, or gum survives in it; that the wood inside the ring is unstained and that outside it darkened, from which she infers that something covered the circle for some years and was removed before the crate reached this Library; and that she declines to infer anything further, reporting it in the flat voice of one who has seen the hoof-prints.

**§22.** My own view is that a ring is not a fact in this matter until there is a claimant who is a person, at which point it will be the only fact in it. I decline to certify what is under the ring, what made it, or whether the wax of it is in this University.

**§23.** The carrier's fingers were stained. The Under-Librarian noted it in the margin of the day-book as "fingers black," did not enter it in the register, and mentions it whenever the crate is mentioned, the sign of a man who has decided which of his observations is evidence. Lamp-black, or soot, or ink, is upon the manifest in two places: where a thumb rested, and across the word *self-help*, the only word touched.

**§24.** The manifest is dated the second of March, 1494, the day before receipt, in the clerk's hand and not the carrier's, and the year is written in the Arabic manner, the practice of the Aldermarket bindery. I do not draw the inference. I record the manifest, the date, the thumb, the ring, the nails, the weight, and the four countings, because a memorandum that decides nothing must leave the next officer all he will need, and he will not be sent the register.

## Part IV — The Claimant, and the Difficulty of a Person Not Yet Born

**§25.** The claim was filed on Form AN/4 on the ninth of July, 1504, at the forms counter, by a person who gave no name, described by the clerk as "a young man, or the sound of one." The clerk is the same who received the crate's papers in 1494; his explanation of the phrase is that the counter was empty and the form was on it.

**§26.** The form was supplied by the counter and is reproduced at Appendix A. I take its fields in turn: what the form requires, what the claim gives, whether the defect is curable, and the paragraph at which I dispose of it.

| the particular | what the form requires | what the claim gives | curable | disposed of at |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the claimant's name | the name in full, in the claimant's hand | "Ambrose Quill, called the second" | no; a name is given, a person is not | §32 |
| the claimant's standing | that he be a person capable of holding a deposit, and the ground | "I am the person named, and I am not yet" | no; capacity asserted, not shown | §31 |
| the date of birth | the day, month, and year of the claimant's birth | "not yet fixed; the Registry will know it" | no | §33 |
| the identity of the deposit | a description sufficient to identify it in the register | "the crate at the north door of the Library, one volume, octavo, boards" | yes; it identifies the crate and only the crate | §34 |
| the ground of the claim | that the deposit is the claimant's, and how | "the volume is mine, and I have not written it" | no; the ground is the difficulty | §29 |
| the date of the lapse | the date on which he says the deposit lapsed | "the eleventh year, in 1505" | yes; and the date is right | §27 |
| the fee | 5 bells, paid at filing, in coin, receipted | 5 bells, promised for 1506 | no; a promise is not payment, and 1506 follows the lapse | §30, §53 |
| the hand | the whole claim in the claimant's own hand | a hand the clerk cannot match, and no witness | no; the clerk has looked | §35 |
| the address | an address at which he may be served | "the fourth reading room, when it is open" | no; the Registry will not serve it there | §34 |
| the signature | his signature, with the date | a signature dated 9 July 1504, and a second, "1506, again" | no; two dates are not a date | §35 |

**§27.** The claim is right about the lapse. I say so at once, because a memorandum that finds against a claimant on ten particulars and does not record the one on which he is right is not a memorandum but a temper. The lapse is in 1505, and the claim says so, and the Registry says so, and the Under-Librarian says so.

**§28.** I now reproduce the claim in full. It is written on the face of the form, in a small hand, without erasure, and runs to two hundred and eleven words in a space ruled for forty.

> To the Registry of the University of Alderwick, and to the officer to whom this deposit does not belong. I make my claim upon the crate which lies at the north door of the Library, deposited the third of March 1494, described in the register as one volume, octavo, boards, self-help. The volume is mine. I say that plainly, since the form asks for the ground and the ground is the whole of it: the volume is mine, and it is mine because no one else will write it, and I have not written it yet. My name is Ambrose Quill, called the second. I do not give a date of birth, because I have none yet, and any date I gave would be wrong. I do not give an address, having none yet either; I may be served at the fourth reading room when it is open, which it is not. I undertake to pay the fee of 5 bells in the year 1506, when I shall be in funds, and I ask that the fee be entered as promised and not as unpaid, promising being the most I can do before I am. I ask that the lapse be certified in 1505 as the statute directs, and that the volume be kept for me till I call for it, which I shall do. I have read the form. I am aware that it requires a person. I am a person, in the sense that I shall be one, and I ask the Registry to hold the form till the sense is complete.

**§29.** The ground is the difficulty, and I take it first because it is not a defect of form. The claimant says the volume is his and that he has not written it. Those two are not in tension; they are the claim. The claim is not defective in one particular but in its subject: a form may be corrected, but a claimant cannot be entered on the register in advance of himself. A manual not yet written belongs to the person who will write it, and the law has no difficulty with him until he asks it to certify that it is his.

**§30.** The fee is not a defect of form either, though the Registry has entered it as one. The form requires 5 bells at filing; the claim offers 5 bells in 1506. The difference is a year and a discretion, which is not mine. The counter may accept a promise, having accepted promises since 1494, when the Under-Librarian took the carrier's word that the fee was paid, without a receipt.

**§31.** As to standing, the claim asserts capacity and does not show it. The Registry has no form for a person not yet born and has declined to make one, the form requiring a date of birth, and any date entered being wrong. It is right, and the only sentence in its twenty-four sheets that I have adopted.

**§32.** As to the name, the claimant gives one. It is not a name unknown to this Faculty, and the Registry has asked me three times whether I wish to say anything about it. I do not. A name is not a person.

**§33.** I have considered whether I might certify the capacity myself. I decline. A certificate of capacity is a certificate that a person exists, and I know a person exists only by his standing before me, and the claimant has not stood before me.

**§34.** As to the deposit and the address, the particulars are sufficient and curable. The claim identifies the crate as well as the register, and not what is inside it. The claimant asks to be served at the fourth reading room, and the Registry has declined, the room not being in the building; I observe only that it is in the plans, is procedurally extant, is bookable, and that Form 7B issues at its counter.

**§35.** As to the hand and the signature, the particular is not curable and is not therefore fatal. The clerk cannot match the hand to any on the register of readers, and he has looked: in the register of borrowers, the register of fines, which is short, and the register of the fourth reading room, a ruled book with one entry, for a lamp.

## Part V — The Custody, and Why the Under-Librarian Is Right

**§36.** The custody of the crate is the Under-Librarian's. It has been his since the third of March 1494, when the carrier put it into his hands, and will be his in 1505, when clause 19 makes him the finder, unless something happens to him first, and nothing is before me on that.

**§37.** The Registry argues that the custody is irregular, the crate having been never catalogued and never shelved. I do not accept it: a deposit is not a holding. Clause 18 forbids the register to describe it, the Classification cannot assign it a shelf, and the only place here for a thing with no shelf and no entry is behind the desk, which is where it is.

**§38.** The Under-Librarian has informed me in writing that he will not open the crate. He does not say that he cannot. He says it is not his to open, the crate having been deposited and not given, and the officer who opens it being the officer then in custody, who will not be he, he having retired. It is not mine to open either. That is not a difficulty. It is the whole of the arrangement. I asked whether he means to retire. He replied that he means to be right, and that the two are the same question asked by different offices.

**§39.** His position is correct. I say so without qualification, which is the second and last sentence in this memorandum that gives anyone anything. He is right that a deposit is not a gift, that a closed crate is closed by the statute and not by his obstinacy, and that the officer who certifies the lapse and the officer who opens the crate will be two officers, neither of them he. He is right also to keep it behind the desk, the only furniture here in use since 1388.

**§40.** Mr. Casper Nunn, Deputy Under-Librarian, has deposed in writing that he would open the crate tomorrow if instructed. I record the deposition, that it is not before me, and that the Under-Librarian read it and said, in my hearing, that the Deputy will learn.

## Part VI — The Four Grounds on Which I Do Not Decide

**§41.** I do not decide the matter for four grounds, each sufficient, and any one sufficient alone. I set them out because the Registry has asked me to, and because a ground not written down is one the Registry will discover on its own, in a form, in 1511.

**§42.** **The first ground: the claimant is not a person within the meaning of clause 21.** The claimant gives a name and a hand and a promise, and does not give a person, and cannot until he is one. I have searched the register of claims from 1374, four hundred and nine leaves: no claim by a person not yet born, and one, of 1409, in which the claimant's existence was in issue and lost.

**§43.** **The second ground: the claim precedes the lapse.** A claim upon a deposit cannot be made before it has lapsed; clause 21 begins from a lapsed deposit, and there is no other beginning in the statute. I am asked to certify in 1504 a lapse that will occur in 1505, for a claimant who will exist at a time the Registry will not specify. I decline to certify the future. I have declined it before, and I am consistent. The Registry says that certifying a lapse which will certainly occur is a formality; a formality is a thing a form requires, and no form requires this.

**§44.** **The third ground: the custody is not mine and the certificate would approve it.** To certify the lapse is to approve of the custody, or of the Under-Librarian's refusal to open the crate, or of the weight of 14 pounds 6 ounces, or of the nails, or of something. I do not approve. It will be said that a certificate is not an approval. In this Faculty a certificate is an approval, and the Faculty has said so since 1402, in a case in which it was wrong and has not retracted.

**§45.** **The fourth ground: the officer is not identified.** Clause 22 does not identify the certifying officer, and I will not identify myself by construing a clause that thirty Deans have declined to construe. A jurisdiction assumed by practice is not a jurisdiction but a habit, and the habit is why the crate has stood behind the desk for ten years while four officers have certified nothing about it in four hands, none of them mine.

**§46.** I am asked what will happen in 1505 if no officer certifies. The statute does not say, and it is silent not by accident: the Chapter could not agree, the motion to amend clause 22 having failed in 1371, 1374, and 1402, by margins of eleven, nine, and four. The lapse will occur whether or not it is certified. It is not an act but an event, and events are not certified but recorded, which is the Registry's office.

**§47.** I therefore decide nothing upon the crate, the claim, the custody, or the lapse. I decide that the matter is not before me, upon each of the four grounds, and I do not choose among them; the Registry asked again on the second of August, and this paragraph is the answer.

## Part VII — Precedent

**§48.** I have not decided this matter before. I have not decided three matters before that are the same as this one, and I set them out because the Registry maintains that this matter is unlike them, which is the ground of its objection at §52, the likeness being the whole of my jurisdiction.

**§49.** **The matter of the borrowed *Field Guide*, 1484.** In 1484 I borrowed from this Library the volume at **CR 1.1** / — 1483, *DO NOT READ: A Field Guide to Timecrafting*, whose author is not yet born. In 1485 the Library asked me to certify, for the shelf, that the volume had been written. I declined, the volume being in my hands and the author not, a book in a reader's hands being evidence of the Library's holdings and not of its author's existence. The Library entered the refusal in the shelf-list under the word *annotated*. The likeness is exact: in 1484 a work by a person not yet born lay here and I was asked to certify the person; in 1504 such a person's deposit lies behind a desk, and I am asked to certify the lapse that makes it his.

**§50.** **The matter of the count of the fourth reading room, 1487.** On the sixth of March 1487 the fourth reading room, its counter, its lamp, its copy of the manual recorded at **EA 2.4** / P54 1504, and the only completed Form 7B were filed under R by Mr. Anselm Pike, Junior Clerk (Third Class, Unconfirmed), and on the eleventh day the annual return of lamps discovered the loss. The Registry asked me to certify the loss, and the day on which it had occurred. I declined both, the room being extant, and a thing extant not being capable of being certified lost but only mislaid, and the clock having stopped at forty-one minutes past two. The likeness is in the clock: in 1504 I am asked to certify a date not yet arrived, in 1487 an hour that had happened and could not be shown.

**§51.** **The matter of the Tenterfields covenant, 1494.** By the deed at **EA 4.3** / W55 1501 the donor covenanted that the fourth reading room be kept in good order. The room having been filed under R in 1487, the covenant became unsatisfiable in 1494, and the Bursar asked me to certify that the Library was in breach. I declined, a covenant being breached by a party and not by a filing, and the filing having been made by a clerk who, being unconfirmed, was not a party to anything, and against whom no charge could lie, and whose remedy was promotion. The likeness is the officer: in 1494 I refused to certify a breach that no one could be charged with; in 1504 the lapse will benefit a claimant who cannot be charged either. The Registry distinguishes these precedents as documents, this being a crate; but the crate has never been opened, so all I have of it is a document, the manifest of nine words.

## Part VIII — The Registry's Objection, and My Reply

**§52.** The Registry's covering note of the ninth of July, 1504, is a considered argument; I set out so much of it as is argument, in its own voice.

> The Registry has the honour to refer the within claim to the Dean of the Faculty of Ethics, Appeals & Paperwork, and respectfully submits that the Dean is the officer to whom the within deposit does not belong, and that the certificate required by clause 22 of the Capitular Statutes of 1274 is therefore the Dean's to give, and that he is under a duty to give it, the lapse being certain, its hour fixed by the statute, and the claimant having filed upon the proper form and undertaken the fee. The Registry does not accept that the clause is a solecism. It has been construed by practice in every case since 1374, in each of which the certificate was given by the Dean of the day, and the register so records in thirty-one instances, counted twice. A clause construed by practice for a hundred and thirty years is not a solecism; it is a constitution. Nor does the Registry accept that the claimant's non-existence is an answer. The lapse is an event in the calendar and not a transaction with a person; it occurs whether or not anyone is there to take the benefit, as a bell rings whether or not anyone is in the court to hear it. The Registry further submits that if the Dean declines, the deposit will lapse in 1505 to an officer who has informed the University in writing that he will not open the crate, so that the Dean's refusal leaves the within volume closed for ever behind a desk, and asks whether that is a consequence he is content to have occasioned. The Registry notes that the volume is described as a manual, and that this Library lost its only manual in 1487, with the room and lamp. The Registry asks for the certificate, and for the Dean's reasons in writing, filed in the press of 1475, which is open.

**§53.** First, as to the construction and the count, I have answered at §9. Second, as to the fee, which the Registry does not mention and I raise because it will raise it in 1506. The fee of 5 bells promised for 1506 is not paid, 1506 is after the lapse of 1505, and a fee promised for a year in which the claimant hopes to be in funds is promised upon a condition he has not established he can satisfy. Form 7B would not assist him: it is required of theses, it is missing from every thesis here, and I have never required one and do not propose to begin. I record that this is the named failure here, whose name is *the fee of 5 bells, promised for 1506, never paid*, and that it will be entered in the ledger of unpaid fees against a year in which no person can be charged.

**§54.** Third, as to the bell. The Registry's simile is that a lapse occurs whether or not anyone is there to take the benefit, as a bell rings whether or not anyone is in the court to hear it. The simile is good and it is against the Registry, for in this University a bell is rung by a ringer, and the ringer is a person, and the Registry has been unable to name him. I have had the bells looked at. The second bell has not rung since the sixth of March 1487, and the Registry will not wind the clock that rings it, on the ground that it is not to be wound, recording that ground in the note in which it asks me to certify that time is passing.

**§55.** Fourth, as to the consequence. The Registry asks whether I am content that the volume remain closed for ever behind a desk. I am content. The crate may be opened lawfully in 1505 without any certificate of mine, by the following procedure, which I set out as a courtesy to the officer who follows me, and which I decline to perform.

1. The lapse occurs on the third of March 1505, the eleventh anniversary, at eleven in the forenoon.
2. The Registry records the lapse in the day-book that day, upon the Under-Librarian's report and not a certificate, none being required.
3. The officer then in custody enters the lapse and the finding under clause 20, and signs it: the crate's first signature in eleven years.
4. That officer gives the Chapter notice in writing, under clause 24, that the finder's title has accrued; the Chapter acknowledges it, as it has since 1374.
5. The University offers the crate to the owner at the north door, once, at the original hour, having advertised the offer by the porter's bell and a notice at the counter.
6. If no owner appears within forty days, and none will, the finder's title is complete: the finder being the officer then in custody, who will not be the Dean.
7. That officer applies to the Conservator for a report, which will include the ring; she will decline to infer anything from it, as in 1502.
8. That officer opens the crate, the Deputy witnessing, in the north corridor, the draught there being the only air fit for opening boxes.
9. He removes the volume, if there is one; the manifest is read aloud; the reading is entered with the volume's weight, against that of the third counting of 1501.
10. The volume is catalogued, the lapse having ended clause 18, and assigned a class by the Classification of 1388, which has none for a manual; the officer will choose AN, Provisionally.
11. The volume is shelved; the ring is recorded in the shelf-list in the Conservator's words; and the crate is kept, empty, behind the desk, where the Library will count it a fifth time.

I decline to be the officer who performs step 8, or step 9, or step 10, which is the step requiring a choice, and which is the whole reason the matter has been brought to me, and the whole reason I will not take it.

**§56.** Fifth, as to the Registry's observation, put in the alternative, that a man who approves nothing cannot be said to refuse anything. A refusal is an act of a will; this memorandum is the first act of mine the Registry has had in writing. I do not claim that my refusal alters the lapse; it does not. What it alters is the record, and the record will show that no Dean certified, that the crate was opened anyway, that the procedure at §55 was followed, and that the volume was catalogued by a person, and not by me. Sixth, as to the Registry's request that my reasons be filed in the press of 1475. The press is open; it was opened in 1475, in the matter of a claim upon a deposit of 1469, in which the claimant was a person who existed and could not prove it, and in which I declined for a fourth time, my refusal being the first entry. I direct that this memorandum be filed with the pencil reminder of 1491 at Appendix B, which is kept with the papers and not the file.

## Part IX — Conclusion

**§57.** The matter is not before me. I certify nothing. The deposit falls due in 1505 and will accrue to the officer in whose custody it lies, who will then be obliged by clause 19 to open it, and who has informed me in writing that he will not. That is a matter between him and the statute, and is therefore also not before me. The matter is not mine to decide, which is the only question that was ever before me, and which I have now answered in writing for the first time in thirty-one years. The Registry asks whether this is an authority; it is an authority upon nothing, a record of a refusal, and a refusal is no precedent.

*Signed:* A. Quill, Dean. *Dated* the fourteenth of July, 1504.

*Postscript.* The claimant describes the volume as a manual, octavo, boards. I am aware of this Library's interest in a manual of that description, which is treated at **EA 2.4** / P54 1504. I have not opened the crate and I do not propose to. I record only that I am aware.

## Appendix A — Form AN/4, as Printed

**Form AN/4** · *Claim upon a Lapsed Deposit* · Wrenfield Press, for the Registry. *In the claimant's own hand, in ink, without erasure; the counter will not receive one completed by an agent or a clerk.*

1. **Name of the claimant**, in full.
2. **Standing of the claimant**, and its ground.
3. **Day, month, and year of birth.**
4. **The deposit claimed**, as described in the register, and no further.
5. **The ground of the claim**, that is, in what manner the deposit is the claimant's.
6. **The date on which he says the deposit lapsed.**
7. **The fee**, being 5 bells, paid at filing, in coin, receipted at the counter.
8. **Address at which the claimant may be served.**
9. **Signature and date.** *A witness may be added, not being a person for the purposes of field 2. A false statement in field 4 is a matter for the Chapter; one in field 2 is not, the field being unenforceable.*

## Appendix B — The Registry's Reminder of 1491, and the Ruling upon It

*In pencil, in the margin of the register, against the entry of the third of March 1494, in the Under-Librarian's hand:* "Counted. Ask the Dean in the eleventh year. Ask him in good time. He will be busy." *Entered 1491, against an entry not made until 1494.*

**My ruling.** Pencil is not ink, and a register is a record in ink. The reminder of 1491 may not be filed. It was entered before the entry it annotates, which the Registry has raised and I do not resolve; it stands in a register the Chapter then produced, which the Chapter does not wish resolved; and it is in the Under-Librarian's hand, and he will not say whether the hand is his. It is kept with the papers and not the file: not to be erased, not pencilled over, not counted. *So ruled,* 14 July 1504.
