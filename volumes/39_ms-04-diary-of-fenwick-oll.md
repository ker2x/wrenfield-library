# The Diary of Fenwick Oll
### The diary of the Under-Librarian of the Wrenfield Library, kept from January to the second of August 1504; reissued at the enlarged length

**Call number:** MS 4.1 / O55 1504
**Hand:** Mr. Fenwick Oll's own, small, upright and unusually even; the entries after the second of August are shorter by one line each week
**Class:** MS — Manuscripts & Personal Papers
**Dates of entries:** 4 January – 2 August 1504; four months unentered, and the ruled leaves continue to the end of the year
**Provenance:** Found on the Under-Librarian's desk in the week of his death; Mr. Casper Nunn writes on the first leaf that it is "a day-book and not a record"
**Collation:** 40 leaves, 32 written; watermark a wren, poorly cut
**Condition:** the plainest boards, re-sewn once by an amateur, with white thread

## The Library's Note

This is the day-book of Mr. Fenwick Oll, Under-Librarian of the Wrenfield Library, kept at the desk in his own hand through 1504: what came to the counter, what he counted, what he trimmed, whom he answered and on what day. It is not a defence and not a treatise; it is the book of a man working out his duties in the order he found them, and the Library asks readers not to look in it for anything else. The first issue printed the crate, the lamp and the two bookings and left out the enquiries, the letters and the four countings of the crate, which made the diarist look like a man with three habits and no work; the omitted matter was on the leaves all along. The Library declines to fill the four unentered months from the office papers.

## The entries, kept from the 4th of January to the 2nd of August 1504

**the 4th of January.** Counted the crate for the fourth time: one volume, octavo, self-help, and I have measured it twice and am satisfied that it is octavo. A reader asked whether a drawer would hold it. A drawer would be deeper, and it would also be a drawer, and the crate has awaited cataloguing since 1494 and is not to be re-housed in a manner that would make the waiting look like progress.

**the 6th of January.** Trimmed the lamp to a point. The wick wanted a quarter inch off and I took a quarter inch and no more, the reading room being occupied and the smell of a long wick being a complaint I have answered twice.

**the 9th of January.** The Faculty of Agricultural Curses writes again about the windows of Reading Room 2. Their letter is short and I have copied it here, because the same letter has come every year since 1487 and I keep the year's copy at the desk:

> From the Faculty of Agricultural Curses to the Under-Librarian: the indult of the ninth of May 1487 being spent, and the air of the second reading room being as it is, the Faculty renews its request that the windows of the north range be kept shut in the pollen season, which the Faculty defines as the middle of March to the middle of May, the only exception being the window that has been shut since the indult, which is shut. The Faculty does not ask for a ruling. It asks for a practice.

**the 11th of January.** Answered the Faculty's letter. The air of a room is not a practice and not a policy; it is a window and a hand on it. I have kept the windows shut in the pollen season at the desk's discretion since 1487 and have written the hour of each opening in the day-book, which the Faculty has never asked to see. I have offered to show it. If the Faculty wants the windows shut in law it must be shut by the Committee and not by me, and the Committee has not met since 1499.

**the 13th of January.** The fourth reading room was booked twice for the same induction, at the fourth bell and at the fifth, by two clerks each of whom had the room before the other. I have entered both bookings and have not preferred one.

**the 15th of January.** Both inductions were held. Complaints: two, both of draught. I have recorded the complaints and have not stopped the draught, which is a matter for the joiner. The lamp was trimmed on the sixth, to a point, and will be trimmed again on the twenty-first.

**the 21st of January.** Trimmed the lamp to a point. The crate was measured again by a reader with a rule of his own, and I stopped him, not because he was wrong but because the third counting of 1499 was entered as a return of furniture and the crate was not furniture, and any further measurement will be a fifth counting and must be entered as one.

**the 2nd of February.** Answered the enquiries of the first of the month. I answer on the second and not on the first, the first being given to the month's business. Of the eleven, nine were answerable. Two were not, and I have entered them as unanswered, which is what they are.

**the 9th of February.** Wrote to Miss Coote, Librarian of the Bramblegate Lending Library, on her enquiry about the loan of 1495, which is a Wrenfield loan and not hers, and which I have traced to the borrower and not to her shelf. I have sent her the date and the call number, and I have not sent her the borrower's name, the borrower being dead.

**the 2nd of March.** Answered the enquiries. Ten. One was from a student of the Faculty of Ethics who asked what the fourth reading room is for, and I have answered that it is bookable, which is what it is.

**the 2nd of April.** Answered the enquiries. Ten. One was from the estate of Mr. Tarry, of Bramblegate, which asks whether a fine waived in 1497 may be reinstated. It cannot, the waiver being recorded, and I have sent the answer twice.

**the 12th of April.** The joiner attended for the north corridor and charged two bells and three clipped, and did nothing to the door, the draught in the corridor not being for a joiner. I have paid the charge out of the contingency and have entered it as a charge that bought a wedges-and-nothing.

**the 2nd of May.** Answered the enquiries. Eight. One concerns the account of Mr. Nott, which I look at once a year and not twice. The fines are waived, as for forty-three years.

**the 2nd of June.** Answered the enquiries. Seven. Two concern the form issued at the counter of the room in question. I have explained where the counter is, and nothing further.

**the 2nd of July.** Answered the enquiries. Five. Three concern the crate, which is not to be counted, moved, opened or measured again, and I have said so in the same words three times, and the words are the answer.

**the 2nd of August.** Answered the enquiries. One. It was a good question and it was put to me civilly, and I have entered it here in full and have not answered it. Nothing further is entered.

### The way I answer an enquiry at the desk, which I keep to

1. Read the enquiry twice, and if the second reading does not agree with the first, read it a third time and enter that it took three.
2. Answer on the second of the month and not on the first, the first being given to the month's business.
3. Where the enquiry concerns a form, name the counter at which the form is issued, and do not describe the room.
4. Where the enquiry concerns a fine, look at the account once and not twice, and cite the year of the waiver.
5. Where the enquiry cannot be answered, enter it as unanswered, in the enquirer's own words, and leave the line blank beneath.
6. Where the enquiry is a good question, enter it in full whether or not it can be answered, and date it, so that the desk is not said to have lost it.

### The enquiry of the second of August, entered in full

> To the Under-Librarian of the Wrenfield Library. Sir, — I am a candidate of the Faculty of Locomotion and I am told that the form required of my work, which is the Declaration of Non-Interference with Time, has never been completed by any hand in this Library, and that the counter at which it is issued stands in the fourth reading room. I do not ask you to issue it, the room being as it is. I ask only whether it has ever been completed, and if it has, where it now lies, so that I may say in my own preface that I have asked. — Your servant, H. Beddoes, M.L.S.

I have not answered it. It has been completed once, and it lies with the room, and the counter that issued it is the counter of the room, and I have written this line and no other, and I have entered the hour, which was the eighth bell.

### The failure of the third counting, and what it cost

In 1499 I entered the crate on the return of furniture, the crate standing against the wainscot and the form of the return having no line for a crate. The return went to the Bursar's office and the crate went on it, and the Bursar's office returned the crate as not furniture and struck the line, and the clerk of the return asked me twice whether the crate was a box, and I said it was a crate, and he said a box is furniture and a crate is a box, and the Registry upheld him in 1500. What the error cost the Library is the fifth counting and a joiner's charge of two bells and three clipped for a wedges-and-nothing in the north corridor, which was entered against the crate's line by the Bursar's office and has never been removed; and the manifest of 1494, which disagreed with the crate in the year, still does. The crate is not to be counted again. A fifth counting will not be entered, because a fifth counting is not a counting, it is a habit, and I have kept four.

## Apparatus

*Under-Librarian's stamp, in violet:* The crate is not to be opened, counted, moved or measured again. The north corridor is not to be closed. Enquiries are answered on the second of the month.

*Conservator's note, 1504:* The leaves after the second of August are blank and ruled, and are handled as the written leaves are, so that the book was opened daily to the end of the year. The lamp oil has marked the foot of the leaf for the twenty-first of January, which is the leaf the diarist trimmed the lamp by; the mark is not to be cleaned, and the recipe has been entered at the desk and refused.

*Pencil note, in Mr. Oll's own hand, later and pressed hard:* The enquiry of the second of August was whether the form had ever been completed. It has, and it is with the room. If the crate is ever catalogued it must be as an octavo. I have answered every enquiry that was put to me. The Faculty defines the pollen season as the middle of March to the middle of May, and I keep the windows shut in that season to the day, and I have written the hour of every opening in the day-book, and the candidate with the plates asked in the wrong month.
