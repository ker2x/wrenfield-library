# The Diary of Anselm Pike
### The diary of a Junior Clerk (Third Class, Unconfirmed) of the Registry, kept 1–20 March 1487, with the quire ruled and unused after it; reissued at the enlarged length

**Call number:** MS 2.1 / P54 1487
**Hand:** Mr. Anselm Pike's own, small and upright; the last three entries are set wider, in the same ink
**Class:** MS — Manuscripts & Personal Papers
**Dates of entries:** 1–20 March 1487, twelve days of which are one line each; fourteen leaves ruled and unused after the 20th
**Provenance:** Found in the Registry cupboard in 1492 among the counterfoils of 1487; the note of transfer misdates it 1486 and is not to be corrected
**Collation:** 24 leaves in one quire, the last 14 blank and ruled; watermark a bell without its clapper
**Condition:** the corner of the eleventh leaf is worn, and the leaf is blank on both sides

## The Library's Note

This is the working diary of Mr. Anselm Pike, a Junior Clerk of the Registry of the third class and unconfirmed, kept in his own hand through the reordering of the Registry in March 1487; it records what came to his desk, what he cut, what he entered, and what he filed, together with the weather, his hours and his bread. It is not a defence, not a memorandum, and not a submission: it was kept for the clerk's own use, and the Registry never asked for it. The first issue printed the twelve one-line days and the last three entries and stopped there, which made the reordering look like a single afternoon's work; the earlier days were in the quire all along and are here given in full. The diary stops on the twentieth of March and never resumes, the remaining leaves being ruled and never written on; the Library has had the quire rebound twice, declines to insert the missing days from any other source, and directs readers who wish to know what became of the room to the thesis of the diarist's grandson, which answers the question correctly.

### The reordering, 1–20 March 1487

**the 1st.** Called to the reordering of the Registry. Mr. Sallow's letters stand, only the marks have been rubbed and are to be cut again. I am given the R series. Reported at the seventh bell; the fire was not yet laid and I laid it, the porter being at the lamps.

**the 2nd.** Mr. Nunn read the order of the reordering at the counter and had it copied into the day-book, and I copied it into this book because it governs me and because a clerk should carry his instructions with him. It is as follows:

> **REGISTRY. Order for the Reordering of the Marks, entered this second day of March 1487.**
> 1. The letters of the Classification of 1388 stand as they are and are not to be improved, Mr. Sallow's scheme being in force.
> 2. Every mark in every series is to be rubbed out and cut again, the rubbing to be done dry, the cut to be deep enough to be read and not deep enough to be argued over.
> 3. An item is filed with its series. An item bearing a series mark is of that series, and its nature is not to be looked into by the clerk, the looking into of natures being a matter for the Faculty.
> 4. Where an item has been entered with fittings, the fittings go with the item, and the phrase *with the room* on an entry is to be read as binding on the fittings and on nothing else.
> 5. Nothing is to be disposed of in a reordering. The reordering is not a disposal and the two have been confused before, in 1402.
> 6. Objections are to be entered in the day-book and answered by the Deputy Under-Librarian and not by the clerk, who has no jurisdiction over a mark.

**the 3rd.** R series begun. Forty-one items, of which nine are boxes, four are ledgers, and the rest are cards and counterfoils. Mr. Fisk has the S series and says S is the worst in the building.

**the 4th.** The fourth reading room stands in the plan and takes bookings, and its mark is R/4. It came up in the reordering and I have put it in the R box with the rest. It is an R item; it has an R mark; I do not see the difficulty.

**the 5th.** Mr. Fisk said the room is not a room. I said it is in the plans and is bookable, and that a key was cut for it in 1423 and charged to the building account. He said the key was never collected, and that a key in a key-box is not a room, and that a clerk who files a plan files a plan. I said the room is not the plan; the room is the room, and it came up under R like the rest, and the order of the second of March says an item bearing a series mark is of that series. He said that proves nothing except that the mark came up. He is a good clerk and I have entered his words as he spoke them, and I do not agree with them.

**the 6th.** Finished the R series. Behind the room went its counter, its lamp, and the two things that stood on the counter, which are a completed form and a small book about the form. They have the room with them. That is what "with the room" means on the entry.

**the 7th.** Entry made at forty-one minutes past two by the Registry clock. The clock stopped while I was writing, at that minute, and has not been wound. This is not to be read as anything. Clocks are wound by the porter, and the porter says he wound it on the fifth and will wind it again when the joiner has seen the case.

**the 8th to the 16th.** Nothing from the desk. The north corridor is cold early; the porter says the door has swelled and will not shut, and that the draught in it is not to be stopped until the joiner comes. Bread and small beer at the desk, one bell. Mr. Fisk did not come in after the tenth, his sister being ill at Low Town.

**the 17th.** The lamps came in for the annual return. Every lamp in the building is returned and counted, and the return is complete to the last lamp. There is a difficulty about the return which I have entered in the day-book and shall not enter here. The porter says the lamp of the fourth reading room was found in the room and not in the corridor where it is kept, and came in for the return like the rest, and its wick was new.

**the 18th.** Mr. Nunn sent for the R box and took the day-book away and brought it back at the ninth bell without the leaf. Nothing was said to me. I have entered the hour.

**the 19th.** Mr. Nunn says the promotion has gone forward. I did not ask for it, and a promotion for a clerk unconfirmed is an oddity, and I have said so. I said I would rather be confirmed. He says that is not before him.

**the 20th.** Nothing from the desk. I shall not write in this book again. The ruled leaves may serve for the countersignatures when the room is found, which it will be.

### The clerk's way of cutting a mark and making an entry, which I keep to

1. Rub the old mark dry with the flat of the thumb, and brush the dust into the tray and not on the floor, the floor being the porter's.
2. Cut the letter first and the figure after, and cut the letter at the width of the quire's rule, so that a mark made by one clerk can be read by the next.
3. Read the mark back against the series box before the item goes in, and read it aloud if the room is empty.
4. Enter the item in the day-book in the order it was cut, and not in the order it was taken from the box, the box being a box and the day-book being a record.
5. Where an entry carries fittings, write *with the room* and write out the fittings under it, listing them, and do not trust the phrase alone.
6. Where there is an objection, enter it in the day-book in the objector's own words and put your own under it, and go on with the next item.

### The eleven days and what the loss cost

The room could not be booked from the sixth to the seventeenth, and two bookings stood in the ledger and were honoured nowhere; the clerk who took the second booking was Mr. Fisk, who had the S series and no room in it, and he has said plainly that a room filed is worse than a room shut, a shut room at least being in the building. There was the joiner, sent for on the eighth for the corridor and again on the fourteenth, who attended once and charged two bells and three clipped for the attendance and for the wedges, and the wedges were of no use, the draught in the corridor not being for a joiner. There was the annual return of lamps, which found the room's lamp in it and could not have found anything else. And there was one completed form and the only copy of a small book about the form, both of which went with the room, and neither of which can be issued anywhere else, the form being issued at that counter and the counter being filed. I have not been charged, being unconfirmed, and the Registry's remedy where no charge lies is promotion. I would rather have been confirmed, and I have said so twice.

## Apparatus

*Under-Librarian's stamp, in violet:* The Registry's clock is not to be wound, and the hour it shows is not to be described as late. The north corridor is not to be closed. Clerks are reminded that the reordering of 1487 is closed.

*Conservator's note, 1504:* Quire sound; both rebindings are the Library's own. The ruled leaves smell faintly of lamp oil. The eleventh leaf is worn at the corner though it is blank on both sides, and the wear is consistent with a thumb, which the Library has not explained and does not intend to.

*Pencil note, in Mr. Pike's own hand, much later:* There is nothing in the entry. The room is an R item, the mark is an R mark, and a clerk does what the series says. If a charge had lain I would have answered it. Being unconfirmed, none did. Confirmed 1494, retired 1500, and never once mis-filed anything at the desk. The order of the second of March is still in my own copy at the third leaf, and I have read it again, and it says what it says.
