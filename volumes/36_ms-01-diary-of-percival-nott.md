# The Diary of Percival Nott
### The diary of a third-year undergraduate, kept 1459–1461, with eight leaves cut out and further entries in the same hand; reissued at the enlarged length

**Call number:** MS 1.1 / N68 1461
**Hand:** Percival Nott's own; fair at first, and fairer afterwards, though the later entries are written more slowly
**Class:** MS — Manuscripts & Personal Papers
**Dates of entries:** 9 September 1459 – 1461; a gap of eight leaves cut out after 6 February 1461, and entries continued in the same hand after it
**Provenance:** Transferred from the mirror-room desk to the Registry in 1462; the accession note says "found", which the Library doubts
**Collation:** 31 leaves, of which 8 are stubs; watermark a hand-and-star
**Condition:** the cut edges are brown, and the text resumes without a break in the sentence

## The Library's Note

This is the day-book of Percival Nott, an undergraduate of the third year, kept by his own hand from his matriculation in September 1459 until some point after the demonstration of the sixth of February 1461, at which he was the hand at the mirror's frame; after eight leaves cut from the quire he continues in the same hand, in a room he describes and does not name, setting down his money, his weather, his essay and his bell. It is a diary and not a record. It is a diary, not a deposition: this enlarged reissue restores the months the first issue left out.

### The first book, 1459–1461

**the 9th of September, 1459.** Matriculated. The fee was two bells and I paid one and a half and was let pass. My board is four bells the quarter, and I have taken the room with the small window, which is nine feet by seven.

**the 14th.** Went to the bindery in the Aldermarket for the *Cantor Minor*, which is prescribed. Mr. Rell's man asked three bells and would not take less, and I had two and eleven clipped, and so I owe three bells and have the book. His name is in the credit book and not in the book, which is the way there.

**the 19th of October.** Mr. Rell's man looked at me across the market and I looked at the pumps. I shall pay in the new year. There is one bell owing to the College for the chair, which the porter carried up and I did not ask him to.

**the 4th of November.** Read in the third-floor reading room, where it is warm. The windows are twenty-two inches across the inner frame and thirteen at the glazing, which is not what is written anywhere. Frost on the inside of the glazing and none on the outside, which I have entered twice.

**the 20th of November.** Paid the College one bell for the chair. Mr. Rell's man was at the corner of the market and said nothing, which is his way of keeping the account in front of me.

**the 8th of December.** The *Cantor* read at the eighth bell by a cousin of the Ashgrove-Vane family, who reads the intervals as if they owed him something. I have begun my own book against his at the back of the *Cantor*.

**the 11th of January, 1460.** Hollis, who has the room under mine, cannot make his board this quarter and has asked me for half a bell. I have not got half a bell, and have told him so.

**the 3rd of February.** Settled with Mr. Rell's man: two bells and eleven clipped paid, and the balance entered in the credit book, so that the book and the man agree. He struck the first entry and wrote the second under it in the same line, which I think is how they keep a man's character there.

**the 6th of May.** Lent Hollis three clipped bells, which I had by me for ink. He says he will repay at the quarter.

**the 2nd of June.** Rain on the north range for eleven days and the river up over the lower step at Bell Court. The reading room shut at the fourth bell on account of the damp, and I read in my own room, where the window weeps.

**the 19th of September, 1460.** The yearly fee, two bells, paid in full and on the day, which is one thing at least. Hollis repaid two clipped bells of the three and had the third forgiven him by me, before he asked.

**the 2nd of January, 1461.** Dr. Ferrer, of the Faculty of Locomotion, sent to my room and asked would I stand at the north side of the frame on the sixth and hold it steady while the light was turned. He said I was recommended for standing still, by the porter I think. I said I would.

**the 5th of February.** Pasted into the front of the room and read three times:

> **NOTICE OF DEMONSTRATION.** By order of Mr. S. Ferrer, M.L.S., for the Faculty of Locomotion, Doors & Threshold Studies. In the third-floor reading room, on the sixth of February, at the second bell.
> 1. The glass is silvered and is not water, and no other surface is to be attempted, not in this room nor in any other.
> 2. No spoon is to be brought into the room on any account whatsoever, including for the taking of posset, the posset to be taken in the passage.
> 3. The frame requires a hand at the north side. The hand is not to be paid, the demonstration being in the nature of instruction, and the hand is to be a person of steady habit and no reputation.
> 4. The two trestles are the Faculty's. The boards are the Library's and are not to be walked upon except by the glass.
> 5. The windows of the room are to be shut for the duration. Any complaint of draught is to be entered in the day-book and not remedied, the ventilation of the room being a matter for the Under-Librarian, who is not to be sent for.

**the 6th of February, 1461.** The demonstration. Mr. Ferrer's people had the mirror up on trestles in the third-floor reading room, and it is silvered glass and not water, which they were very particular about. The frame wanted a hand to keep it from walking on the boards, and I was the hand. I stood at the north side and held it while the light was turned. There was some talk of a draught, but the windows were shut. Dr. Ferrer said afterwards, in front of the room, that the hand had not moved, and I have written it here because no one else will.

*[Eight leaves cut out.]*

### The later entries, in the same hand, older and steadier

**undated, entered after the gap.** The light here is good. I have the desk by the river window, the second from the catalogue press, and there are four desks and only one other man, who does not speak and is not rude about it. There is a bell that goes eleven times in the morning and eleven in the afternoon, and I have got so that I wait for it.

**undated.** The window is twenty-two inches across the inner frame and thirteen at the glazing, the same as the other room, and I measured it with a cord on the third day. The light comes in over the water at the low sun and lies along the desks as far as the catalogue press and no further.

**undated.** I have finished the essay, on the intervals in the *Cantor*, and it is the best thing I have done, and I do not know where to put it, the *Journal* being stopped pending a clarification that has not come. I asked at the counter and the young man said the matter was not before him, which I took to be a kindness.

**undated.** The copyist's bill for the essay is four bells and six clipped for a hundred and six leaves, and I have not paid it and do not think I can. The copyist is at Bell Court and has not asked me for it, which is worse than asking.

**undated.** My card is still good. I had it out to look at the number and it is the same number. The fine is four marks six bells and two clipped, which I do not think is right, but I am comfortable and shall take it up when I am back.

### The way I steady the frame, as I was taught it and keep it

1. Take the north side before the light is brought, and stand square to the frame with the feet a hand and a half apart, the boards being the Library's.
2. Put the flat of the right hand on the frame at the shoulder and the flat of the left at the hip, and let neither hand close on the moulding.
3. Look at the lower corner of the glass and not into it, and follow the light with the eyes only, the head being the heaviest part of a man.
4. Keep the breath shallow and count the intervals of the *Cantor* under it, which occupies the mind and does not move the arms.
5. Do not look at the gallery, and do not answer any question put from it.
6. If the frame walks, let it walk against the hand and not away from it, and on no account take the hand off to set it right.

### The matter of the spoons

Dr. Ferrer looked in at my desk on the fourth and said, in his own words, that the notice was not directed at me, and that a spoon in the room is not a spoon but a small silvered glass with a handle, and that the Faculty has lost one walker to a spoon. I said that I had read the notice three times, and that it was the posset I was thinking of and not the argument. He said that a man who has stood at a frame for one afternoon does not know which is which. I answered that the notice had told me what to do in the room, and he was pleased with that and went out. I have entered the exchange because it is the only time a Doctor of the Faculty has argued with me and I have held my ground.

### The failure with the copyist

The essay is finished and cannot be submitted, the *Journal of Harmless Maleficence* being on hiatus pending a clarification to be issued by the body that issued the hiatus. What it cost me is four bells and six clipped to the copyist at Bell Court for a hundred and six leaves, and the sum is not paid. What it cost me besides is that I have the only copy, and a fair copy of an essay that has nowhere to go is a debt with a hand and no book under it.

## Apparatus

*Under-Librarian's stamp, in violet:* The reading rooms close at the fourth bell. Windows in the north range are not to be opened. Mr. Nott's fines are waived, and will be waived for as long as the present arrangement continues. The notice of demonstration, if found, is not to be pasted in.

*Conservator's note, 1504:* The stubs are eight, the knife was worked from the gutter, and the sewing is undisturbed, so that the volume was taken apart before the cut and bound after it. The earlier entries the first issue omitted are in the same ink as the rest of the first book. The two tables stand on the spare leaves at the back in the diarist's own hand.

*Pencil note, in Mr. Nott's own hand, later and unsteady:* I am not lost. The difference matters. A man who is lost has stopped, and I am only waiting for the catalogue press, which the young man said would be brought. In the room, by the window, where the light is good. As to my standing at the frame, I make it forty-one minutes, and the Registry clock agrees with me.
