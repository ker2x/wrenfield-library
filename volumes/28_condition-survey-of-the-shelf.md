## Abstract (as filed)

This report surveys the sixteen holdings of the shelf and the one standard in the dry season of 1504, treats where required and declines where not, and gives the grounds of each declining at §8. Two holdings, AN 2.3 and CR 1.1, are surveyed and not described. *Form 7B, Declaration of Non-Interference with Time*, is absent from the file, and is not a condition of the shelf.

## 1. What a Condition Survey Is

A condition survey is the examination of a collection as physical evidence. It is not repair, not cleaning, and not the making of a list of things one would like to have done. It is the reading of objects that do not speak in words: paper, sewing, water, insects, and use. It asks what a collection can still prove, and what proof any proposed alteration would remove.

Four things are looked for. The paper records how it was made, how stored, and what has been dropped on it. The sewing records who bound the volume, with what thread, and whether anyone has been inside the boards since. Water records where the roof failed; use, who read it and with what in their hands.

A survey may examine, measure, collate, count, photograph, record, and shape a box to a volume. It may not wash, paste, re-sew, re-back, re-corner, deacidify, or remove an earlier repair: not a stain, because a stain is a measurement; not a leaf pressed flat, because the curl of a leaf records the water that made it curl.

The Conservator's first act is therefore often to do nothing. A book is evidence before it is an object, and every treatment destroys evidence. The object is not duplicated anywhere: the text is duplicated, in the impression and in the catalogue, but the object alone is surveyed.

## 2. A Short Lexicon for Readers Outside the Faculty

| Re-back | A new spine over an old; the old survives beneath |

## 3. The Standard by Which the Shelf Is Judged

The shelf is judged against **JM 1.1**, Ashgrove-Vane, *Principles of Minor Maleficence*, 3rd ed. (the good one), 1440. It is the only volume here in the condition its maker intended: corners rubbed to the board, spine darkened by four decades of handling, quire vii loosened but sound, no treatment performed. Its corners have been proposed four times for re-covering and declined four times, a standard being a standard because it shows how a book behaves when nobody interferes. The refusals, dated 1461, 1473, 1488, and this year, are in the Registry.

The survey is taken in the dry season, first to the seventeenth, for the reason at **DE 22.2 / O55 1504**, §6: only then can water in the volume be told from water entering it. The 1503 survey, taken wet, is superseded in three particulars: AC 118.7, HC 1.1, and the plate stub at LT 3.7.

## 4. Procedure: How the Shelf Is Walked

1. **In daylight, lamp out**: a lamp shows what the surveyor decided to look for. No window is opened; the AC range has asked twice.
2. **A witness is required**, and is not a colleague of the Conservator. Mr Casper Nunn, Deputy Under-Librarian, holds the checklist and signs.
3. **The shelf is walked in accession order, left to right, once**, not in call-number order, which differs.
4. **Each volume is removed by the boards**, never by the spine, and laid on a felt pad at the shelf's end.
5. **Each volume is described outward first**: dimensions, boards, covering, spine angle, sewing stations, colour of the fore-edge.
6. **Only then is it opened**, and only where it opens of itself: the places it falls open are the places it has been read.
7. **Plates are counted, not turned**, and examined at the stub and the gutter.
8. **Every smell is recorded in words at once**: paste, mould, smoke, other. Where the answer is *other*, the survey stops and a paragraph is written.
9. **Every loose fragment is left where it lies**, its position noted to the eighth of an inch.
10. **Nothing is cleaned, not even the shelf**, until the survey is signed.
11. **The volume is returned as found**, spine at the same angle to the wall.
12. **The survey is closed the same day.**

**The closing rule.** Two holdings are not opened. **AN 2.3 / C76 1499** is not opened past the sewing, for the reasons at §6. **CR 1.1 / — 1483** is not opened at all, because to open it is to read it, and the reading has been ruled upon twice. The two not opened are the two that most need opening, and that is the report.

## 6. The Worked Case: AN 2.3, the Volume That Smells

The volume is **AN 2.3 / C76 1499**, Crowe, *A Modest Proposal for the Ethical Reanimation of Household Poultry* (applied necromancy, provisional): eight quires, no plates, dark calf over boards, re-backed before 1504. Examined on the fourth day of the dry season, nine to eleven, in daylight, before the Deputy Under-Librarian.

Outward and inward. Outward: boards and re-back sound, corners knocked. Inward: endpapers clean, first and second quires sound; at the third quire, in the sewing, an odour, entered as *other*. Not mould, not decay, not paste. Warm, faint, closer to struck flint, strongest at the third and fourth stations, absent from the sixth.

Four airings: in the window bay of Reading Room 2, with Agricultural Curses' consent; once on a trestle with a fan; twice in the courtyard. Each left it unaltered, full strength within a day. It is produced in the sewing, not held in the paper, since at least 1501.

**The sampling refused.** Proposed: draw thread at the fifth station with a hollow needle for assay by the Faculty of Thaumaturgical Engineering. I refused on three grounds. Drawing there would break the fifth quire's sewing, through which the sixth station's thread runs; the smell would migrate. The thread is the object; a sample is a piece of the record which does not grow back. The assay would change nothing: common linen leaves the smell *other*, otherwise the Faculty of Ethics, Appeals & Paperwork must be consulted, and it approves nothing.

**What the smell is, and what it records.** I decline what the smell *is*; the finding I can support: the smell is the record. The reanimation left no plate, no errata, no note, no mark on the paper, only the smell — the sole trace that the procedure in ch. vi, *On the Return of the Bird*, was carried out. The procedure is disputed — whether any bird comes back, whether the returning bird is the bird. To remove it is to make the volume say nothing.

**The two treatments proposed, and my reasons for declining both.** Mr Oll's, in writing on the twelfth: remove the quire, re-sew in clean linen on the original stations, re-back, at six bells, thread supplied by the Library — destroying the only known instance of the residue. Dr Crowe's own, verbal, on the seventh: seal it unopened in a lead-lined case, at two marks four bells. Sealing stops the airing, my only measurement. Four have shown it produced, not held; a fifth would test the rate. I will not trade measurement for tidiness.

**The recommendation.** The volume stays on the shelf, unopened past the third quire, aired once a year in the dry season by the same method, the result entered in this report, kept at the shelf. If the odour changes character, a sample may be drawn with a witness present, the loss entered as a loss. Until then, nothing.

## 7. CR 1.1, Which Cannot Be Described Without Being Read

The Library has asked me to describe the condition of CR 1.1 twice: by letter of the ninth of the fourth month, 1503, and by letter of the second of this month. I have answered both in writing, and both answers are in the Registry. The second is printed here in full.

> To Mr Fenwick Oll, Under-Librarian, from Judith Cray, Conservator, on the condition of the volume at CR 1.1.
>
> You ask me to describe the condition of the volume. I cannot describe its condition without describing the volume, nor the volume without reading it, the reading being the matter on which the Committee has ruled twice. I submit what may be had without it. It is an octavo of eight sheets and a half, thirty-four leaves, sewn on four stations, three visible, the fourth inferred from the swell of the first quire. The boards are millboard, blind-tooled c.1480–1490, not agreeing with the imprint. The spine faces the wall by order of the Committee. The covering is darkened at the head and the tail; the fore-edge is neither gilded nor grubby; there is no foxing and no worm. The cover bears the words DO NOT READ, in a hand I will not describe further, the description of hands having been the Committee's second ruling. If the Committee wishes more, it must authorise the opening, which is the reading, and I will not do it of my own motion.
> J. Cray, Conservator, the fifth day of the seventh month, 1504.

The Committee has not answered; the volume remains spine to the wall. A condition that cannot be established must be assumed to be improving. I make that assumption the wrong way round, deliberately: the alternative would generate a treatment, and the treatment would be the reading.

## 8. The Grounds of Declining, Stated as Grounds

1. **The damage is the evidence.** The hoof-prints at BH 12.4; the thumbprint in mortar at TE 19.5; the comforted reader's annotations at DP 12.8; the pressed catkin at AC 118.7.
2. **The fault is not in the object.** The oversize volume on a short shelf; the volume charged to a borrower; the volume at DE 31.1, one position past the range's end.
3. **The repair is load-bearing.** The waxed thread at JM 6.1; the good tape at HC 1.1.
4. **The request is prior.** The volume at HS 8.2 facing away from the window at the author's written request.
5. **The test is not available.** The staining at DD 7.2, where the patient is a party.
6. **The reading is the description.** CR 1.1, where thorough examination would be the act the Committee has twice forbidden.
7. **The trace is the whole of the corroboration.** AN 2.3, where the odour is the only physical evidence that the procedure was performed.

## 10. A Failure of 1479, and What It Cost the Library

Early in my appointment, in the ninth month of 1479, I was given the Wrenfield Press day-book for that year: a small quarto of twelve quires, paper over thin boards, from the printer's widow, covers loose and fore-edge torn in three places. The fore-edge carried a faint inscription in the printer's hand, in iron-gall ink, of which perhaps eleven words were legible; I read four as a docket and the rest as smudge. I performed the treatment then standard: lifted the boards, guarded the three torn leaves with new paper at the inner margin, re-sewed on the original stations in new linen, re-backed in calf, trimmed the fore-edge square, and re-cornered the boards. The work took eleven days and was praised; the fee was nine marks four bells, my share two.

What was lost was threefold. The inscription: trimming took eleven words to six, and the guards covered the printer's registration marks on four leaves, the only evidence of how the press was set. The sewing: re-sewing destroyed the original thread, and with it the evidence of a repair of 1481 visible in its colour. And worst, the boards: the thin boards had been damp at the lower corner and had cockled in a shape recording a leak in the north range in the winter of 1478; the new calf boards are flat, the leak now recorded nowhere.

The loss was assessed in 1480 at eleven marks four bells, the insured value. The insurance paid eight marks, the policy carrying a one-mark deductible and a clause excluding damage by the insured's own treatment. The remaining three marks four bells fell on the binding fund, which closed for two years, and four volumes went unbound in 1481 and 1482. I paid the deductible myself, in four instalments: the only fee I have ever paid the Library.

## 11. Objection, and Reply

**The Committee on Standing Matters objects, in its own voice, as follows.**

> The Committee has read the report and does not accept its method. The shelf is deteriorating. The table records water damage at three holdings, a split fold at a fourth, loose boards at a fifth, and an odour at a sixth which she declines to sample. The Committee does not question her skill; it questions her inactivity. The treatment of the shelf is urgent, and every year of delay is a year of failure. A conservator who will not act while objects fail is not exercising a discipline, she is exercising a veto. She has declined fourteen proposals in one year and performed no treatment at all. Her grounds would justify declining any treatment whatsoever; a rule forbidding all treatment is not a professional standard but an obstruction, and the Committee will place the matter before the Faculty of Ethics, Appeals & Paperwork.

**The Conservator replies.**

> The Committee mistakes the direction of the harm. The shelf is not deteriorating at a rate the Committee can compute, and it has never asked me for the rate, though I publish it annually. The water entered in 1491, in 1487, and before the accession; it has not moved. The split fold is the author's argument, the loose boards are held by a repair doing its work, and the odour at AN 2.3 is being produced, which is why I will not sample it, a sample destroying the thing sampled. The Committee counts the years of delay and ignores the years of survival: the volumes it wishes me to treat have held their condition for four, seventeen, and one hundred and six years, and the treatments proposed are not reversible. My grounds forbid treatment that removes evidence. Bring me a proposal that removes nothing and I will perform it tomorrow. The Faculty of Ethics, Appeals & Paperwork has approved nothing since its foundation. The report stands.

## 12. Recommendations, and What Is Deferred

1. That the shelf be re-housed at fourteen inches of clearance rather than twelve and a half, relieving DP 44.9 without touching it; cost, eight bells.
2. That the recall of DE 5.5 proceed on Form 7C and the fine be waived, the borrower being a party of settled opinions who has returned it once.
3. That the five volumes at HT and the four at CM be boxed, in the dry season, the boxes made to the volumes, not the reverse.
4. That the annual airing of AN 2.3 be continued and its result printed at §6.
5. That the Faculty of Divination's request for the annotations at DP 12.8 be refused in writing.
6. That nothing be done to JM 1.1, ever, and the four refusals be entered with its accession note.
7. That the two holdings not opened be entered in the shelf register as passed over.

Deferred: the assay at DD 7.2, to the patient's report; the fold at EA 2.4, to the next dry season; the case for AN 2.3, indefinitely.
