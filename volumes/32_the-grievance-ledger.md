## Abstract (as filed)

This ledger consolidates the sixty-one grievances of the control group of HC 1.1 / M89 1496 — persons enrolled as not cursed and merely unfortunate, without consolation, indemnity or notice — and reports the four occasions on which the ledger has itself been grieved. It is submitted that the ledger is not the record of the grievance but the grievance, and that it cannot be reduced, every amendment being an entry charged for. Form 7B, *Declaration of Non-Interference with Time*, is not bound with this document, notwithstanding that this is the Faculty of forms.

## 1. What a Grievance Is, and What This Ledger Is

A **grievance**, in this Faculty, is an injury entered on the roll. It is not the injury itself, and the distinction is the whole of the practice.

Four things are confused with a grievance, and are not grievances. A **complaint** leaves no entry, and becomes grievable only when a form is selected and a fee is paid. A **claim** is answered or refused, and either way it ends; a grievance is entered, and continues. A **remedy** is what might be done about an injury, and the Faculty grants none: its acts are to enter, to determine, to refuse and to renew. A **curse** is an instrument of Harmless & Decorative Curses or of Jinxcraft & Minor Maleficence, depending on the hour and the argument; a grievance may concern a curse and need not.

The **ledger** is a guard-book kept at the counter of the Hall of Bells, in which every grievance of the control group is transcribed in the words in which it was taken down, with the form selected, the fee charged, the year of entry, and such notes as the enrolling officer has made in the margin. It is not a minute-book, not a cause list and not a judgment roll, and it determines nothing: it is the instrument by which a determination may later be sought, and its entries are its whole content.

It is **for** keeping the injury in one place, keeping the parties to it, and keeping the file open. A closed file is a record, and no one is answerable in it. An open file is a holding: it may be renewed and amended, and every person named in it remains a party. The distinction costs the Faculty nothing and the complainants everything.

## 2. The Control Group, and How It Was Enrolled

In Firstmonth 1485 the Faculty of Harmless & Decorative Curses required of Perpetua Mowbray a comparison: harm sought where a curse had been cast, against harm not sought at all. One hundred and six persons were enrolled from the ordinary bad luck of Low Town and the Tenterfields, in the order in which their doors were found open, by four clerks of the Registry over eleven days, of whom no one has since been able to name more than two.

Enrolment conferred nothing: it did not impose a curse and did not remove one, it did not console, and it did not inform. Notice was considered in Fourthmonth 1485 and found to be a form of consolation, for which no fee had then been set, and a Faculty may not perform a service for which no fee is set. The omission was entered in the survey book as an omission, and not as a courtesy. The enrolled discovered their enrolment by being asked to sign for it afterwards; the sheet on which the signatures were taken is not in the file, having been filed at Counter IX, which closed in 1497.

The group was observed for the same ninety days, by the same four registers, without a casting, and fared worse than the cursed in three registers and equally badly in the fourth. Mowbray's conclusion, which this Faculty has never dissented from, is that a curse producing less harm than no curse produces no harm. The control group is at §5 of **HC 1.1 / M89 1496**, the comparison at §6; its arithmetic has never been questioned, only the informing.

A fifth class — luck — is entered under the nearest of the four, and the entry is the record.

The first is instructive. Tallis asked that the entry against him be struck out. The enrolling officer ruled that a strike is an amendment, an amendment an entry, and an entry charged for; whereupon the strike was entered, the entry made, and Tallis had two entries where he had had one. He objected to the second entry on Form 9F; that strike was also ruled an amendment. He died in 1497 with four entries against him, and his daughter renews annually on Form 55A (5 bells). The second and third occasions are one complainant's, counted separately because they were charged separately; the fourth and fifth demonstrate that there is no form on which a ledger may be complained of until one is asked for, and that the asking is what produces it.

## 6. The Sixty-First Grievance, in Full

In Secondmonth 1504 the ledger was entered in its fifth renewal and submitted to the Dean's Chamber at rung six on the fourth of Ninthmonth. On the twelfth of Ninthmonth, at forty minutes past nine, Hesper Rill of the Tenterfields attended at Counter IX and filed the sixty-first grievance. She is a weaver, in her forty-first year, and the daughter of Perpetua Rill.

The counter was closed. Counter IX was shut in 1497, its clerk having been confirmed and moved to the Registry, and the notice of closure is nailed to the shutter, weathered to four legible words. Hesper Rill took the shutter for a desk, wrote her grievance on a sheet she had brought, and left it beneath with the fee, counted correctly: 6 bells, in clipped bells, tied in a cloth. The clerk of Counter VII found it on the fourteenth, walking the Hall to close the shutters, and entered it as received at Counter IX, the closure being a closure of the counter and not of its function.

The grievance was filed after the ledger had gone to the Dean, and that is the point of it. The Chamber had returned the file unopened on the seventh of Ninthmonth. An entry into a file held by the Chamber must be filed where the file is, which is nowhere, the Chamber holding it without opening it. Hesper Rill was directed to Counter IX, did as she was directed, and was correct.

Greaves attended on the fifteenth, read the sheet, took the fee from the cloth, and entered the grievance at the head of the book. His difficulties were four. First the form: she had written on a blank sheet, and a sheet is not a form. He selected Form 47B (6 bells), the reputational form, which does not require ripeness, her harm being the reading of her name from the ledger at the counter — a reading which had not occurred when she wrote. Second the fee: 6 bells had been paid and 6 bells charged, and the payment having preceded the selection of the form, it was on the face of it an amendment, and an amendment is an entry charged for; he declined to charge, the Faculty not charging a complainant for its own selection. The third and fourth he could not resolve, and referred to the Chamber, which returned them unopened, and they remain.

Greaves's disposition of the sixty-first grievance was as follows. He transcribed it verbatim, as at §7; entered it in the Reputational class, Form 47B, fee 6 bells, year 1504, class not determined; and noted in the margin in his own hand: *The sixty-first in the nineteenth year. She walked to a closed counter and was right to.* He supplied her with Form 7B, *Declaration of Non-Interference with Time*, without charge, informing her that it is issued at the counter of the fourth reading room, filed under R and bookable. He gave her the Faculty's standing reply, in four words: *the window is closed.* And he entered her grievance as a renewal of her mother's, without charge, the renewal fee being chargeable to the party, and the party being dead.

## 7. The Grievance as Taken Down

The following is the sixty-first grievance in the complainant's own words, as the clerk took it down at Counter VII on the fourteenth of Ninthmonth 1504, and as it is transcribed at the head of the guard-book. It is entered on Form 47B, which does not require ripeness.

> My name is Hesper Rill. I am a weaver of the Tenterfields and I cannot read, and I have paid a girl two clipped bells to write this, which is the second fee in the matter. My mother was Perpetua Rill, who is in this book twice, and she died in Thirdmonth 1503 and is buried at the Tenterfields, and in Ninthmonth of this year the fee for the renewal of her grievance was called for at my house, on Form 55A, and was paid by me, 5 bells, and the receipt came back addressed to her and was stamped twice, and the second stamp is on the address. I then attended the counter to say that she is dead, and while I waited my own name was read out from this book by the clerk, at the counter, to a man who had asked after another name altogether, and my name was read as a party, and there were four persons at the counter and one of them is my customer. I have never read this book and I did not know I was in it. I do not object to the reading out, which was correct, and I do not ask that the book be corrected, my mother having held that a book once written cannot be unwritten. I ask for one entry: that on the twelfth of Ninthmonth, at forty minutes past nine, at Counter IX, this ledger was read aloud at the counter, and that the reading is the injury. I am asked in the alternative whether I succeed to my mother's grievance or my mother's grievance succeeded to me, and I cannot answer, and I do not think the counter can either, and I ask that the question be entered with the answer and not instead of it.

## 8. The Objection of the Committee, and the Reply

The Joint Standing Committee on Standing Matters considered the ledger at its sitting of the eleventh of Tenthmonth 1504, on a motion to close it, and its objection is entered here in its own voice, as taken down by its clerk.

> The Committee objects that this ledger perpetuates the grievance it records, by the ordinary operation of the instrument and not by any fault in the enrolling officer. Every entry asserts an injury; every renewal asserts it again, for 5 bells; every amendment is an entry and is charged for, so that the book grows by the means by which it is corrected. The result is a roll of sixty-one grievances in which nothing has been determined in nineteen years and nothing can be, because a determination would close the file and the file is the instrument. The Committee observes that the ledger has been grieved four times, and that a ledger which is grieved has generated the injury it exists to record; and that the complainants, enrolled without being told, are kept informed by nothing and kept parties by everything. It does not say the enrolling officer has been idle. It says that an instrument which cannot be reduced cannot be a record, and that a book which is its own complainant cannot be a book. It moves that the ledger be closed, or that it be satisfied, the Committee being indifferent which, and that no further renewal be accepted until one of the two has occurred.

Greaves's reply, filed at Counter II on the thirteenth of Tenthmonth 1504, is as follows.

The objection is not an objection to the ledger. It is a description of it, and it is accurate, and it is refused.

**The ledger is the grievance.** The injury was never the cough of 1488, which passed, nor the goat of 1486, which did not return. It is that one hundred and six persons were enrolled in a comparison of which they were not told, and the only place at which that injury is admitted is this book. To close the book is to complete the omission; to strike an entry completes it for that complainant, and leaves him with the strike.

**It cannot be reduced, because every amendment is an entry charged for.** The Committee says that an instrument which cannot be reduced cannot be a record. The Faculty's answer is that it is not a record, never was one, and would be a poorer thing if it were: an instrument that can be reduced can be made to say less than happened. This one cannot, and the reason is not obstinacy but the schedule. A strike is an amendment, an amendment an entry, an entry 6 bells at the counter and 2 bells more if the complainant enters it, and the fee is charged and the entry stands. Tallis of Low Town learned that in 1488 and paid for it four times before he died.

**The Committee's own motion supplies the third answer.** To close the ledger and to satisfy it are the same act, and the act is a determination, and no determination of a grievance of the control group has ever been made, no application having been in the correct form, the correct form being 7B, issued at the counter of the fourth reading room.

The Dean's non-approval is the fourth answer, and the best. The ledger was submitted at rung six on the fourth of Ninthmonth and returned unopened, the Chamber confirming by note that non-approval is not refusal, refusal requiring a decision, and the office not reaching decisions. An approved ledger is closed and is a record; an unapproved ledger is extant, may be produced, and its complainants remain parties to it. The Dean has kept the file open; he has also kept the complainants in it.

## 9. How a Grievance Is Entered, from the Counter to the Entry

The procedure is performed at the counter and not at a table, a table being where entries are amended, and in the order in which it must be performed.

1. **Attend at the counter of the Hall of Bells between eight and four.** Counter IX was closed in 1497 and remains the correct counter for matters of enrolment. Do not take the guard-book to a table.
2. **State the grievance aloud, in your own words, to the clerk.** The clerk takes it down as spoken and numbers the sheet in the margin. An entry afterwards corrected is an entry in the correcting hand.
3. **Select a form from the ninety-nine.** The selection is the complainant's and the clerk will advise; the advice is free and the fee is not. A complainant who cannot read is advised twice, the second advice costing 3 bells.
4. **Pay the fee in bells at the counter.** Clipped bells are accepted at par for sums under 6 bells. A sum tendered elsewhere is returned to the place of tender, and the grievance is entered only when tendered again.
5. **Have the entry read back.** Where the grievance turns on being read aloud, the reading is the entry, and the entry is the injury, and the reading is performed twice.
6. **Sign for the entry, which has already occurred.** The signature is taken on Form 34S and is not the entry. A complainant who declines to sign does not withdraw the entry, entry being a Faculty act.
7. **Take the receipt, and, if the matter is not determined within the year, renew it on Form 55A.** Renewal is 5 bells and pays for the file to stay open, which is the whole service purchased.
8. **Do not apply to strike out an entry.** The application is granted, and costs more than the entry did.

## 10. A Named Failure: the Fee Defect of Hedley Marle, and What the Rule Cost Him

Hedley Marle, a certificated practitioner of the Tenterfields, having paid 3 marks 4 bells in 1489 for the double classification of Case 173 of **HC 1.1 / M89 1496**, attended the Hall of Bells in Fourthmonth 1490 with a further grievance: that he had been charged twice for one casting. He filed it correctly on Form 23D (4 bells) and paid at the counter in clipped bells, the sum being under 6 bells. The fee was correct in amount, the form correct in number, and the payment the defect.

The rule, which stands at §8 of the Schedule of Counter Fees and at no other place, is that fees are payable at the counter and in bells; that clipped bells are received at par for sums under 6 bells; and that a fee tendered in clipped bells for the purpose of relying on the tender as a payment is received, stamped and held, and does not enter the grievance. Marle had tendered 4 bells in clipped bells and asked for a receipt, which is the act of so relying. The entry was stamped *Fee: held* and not *Fee: paid*, and a grievance is entered when the fee is paid and not when it is held.

Marle attended on eight several days to discover the rule, and was told it on the eighth, at Counter II, by a clerk instructed to tell him on the fourth and at a lecture. The cost of learning it was: the original fee, 4 bells, held and never refunded, the file having been stamped; the second fee, 4 bells, paid in bells on the eighth attendance; the reinstatement charge, 2 bells, for the thirty-one days between tender and payment; seven days of a journeyman's attendance at 8 bells the day by the Tenterfields scale, 4 marks 16 bells; and the submission on Form 11D, 3 bells, filed to complain that the rule is not written where a man may read it. His outlay was 5 marks 3 bells, of which 11 bells went to the Faculty for the privilege of complaining about a fee of 4 bells taken and not counted.

The Faculty's determination, in the enrolling officer's hand in the margin: *The rule is at §8. The Schedule is at the counter, and has been at the counter since 1461, and is bound with the Schedule of 1461 and not with the present Schedule, which is at the counter also.* Marle, being unable to read, had read neither. His grievance was entered as the thirty-fourth entry in the Financial class, and is not determined.

## 11. Related Matters, and the State of the File

Two entries in the ledger are, on their face, not of the control group, and are retained because they were filed by persons enrolled in it. The first is the grievance of the Comb, filed at the Registry in Tenthmonth 1503 by the representative of a body of reanimated poultry; it is cross-entered at **AN 3.1 / C76 1503**, where the argument that a reanimated hen is a servant and not a tool is made at length. The enrolling officer's note reads: *The birds wish the entry, not the remedy, and the entry has been made.* The second is the matter of *Tenterfield Quiet*, at **EA 5.1 / C76 1502**, in which the complainant is a bean that will not germinate within earshot of laughter, and the question is whether a bean can be defamed. Two members of the control group served as witnesses for the bean and were named in the judgment; the naming is entered here as a reputational grievance of 1502 (Form 47E, 6 bells). Neither can be struck from the ledger.

The ledger is in its nineteenth year. It holds sixty-one grievances in seventy-four classes, four of them against the ledger itself, and seventy-nine entries in all, the margin carrying the surplus. Sixteen complainants have died. One was enrolled twice, at two addresses, and the clerks have never agreed whether he is one person or two; the fee has been charged once in each view. Nine grievances have been renewed in each of the last five years on Form 55A, and nothing has been determined.

The present reissue adds the sixty-first grievance, the fee schedule at §10, and the reply at §8, and is the third impression of a work first printed in Sixthmonth 1504. It is offered to the Faculty of Ethics, Appeals & Paperwork as the register of a class, and to Dean Ambrose Quill as a file open to him to read and not to approve. The historian of the harmless jurisdiction has asked which Faculty owns the ledger, and has been referred to **HC 2.1 / M67 1504**, §3, where the findings of 1404, 1419, 1462 and 1491 are given, two of which contradict each other and all four remain in force.
