## Abstract (as filed)

This essay asks whether a Faculty whose whole subject is curses that do nothing can be said to exist. It answers that it cannot, then that it can, then reports an experiment of the author's own in which nothing happened for three weeks and was measured throughout. Form 7B, *Declaration of Non-Interference with Time*, is not attached. The author did not know there was one, has since been shown the counter, and found it shut.

## 1. What the Thing Is, Or An Approach To Saying So

*Tutor's hand, in red:* §1 is where you were to say plainly what a harmless curse is. You have said what it is not, at length, and compared it to a doorknob. Try again, and try it first.

A curse is a working of maleficence directed at a person, a household, an animal or a thing. Every curse in Jinxcraft has a harm in it: a jinx makes a man stumble, a blight makes a field fail, and the harm is the point and the proof. A **harmless curse** is a curse with the harm left out. It is not a failed curse: a failed curse has a harm in it that did not arrive, whereas a harmless curse is composed so that the harm was never in it. The syllables are correct, the intent is filed, the fee is paid, the working is complete, and nothing follows. It is not a blessing, a different degree and counter, and not a joke, though it is often mistaken for one, including by me. It is for use where a curse is required and a harm is not wanted: at a wedding, between cousins, against a colleague one must continue to sit beside. Its vocabulary is set out below, Dr Tarn holding that a reader outside the Faculty should not learn it from me mid-argument.

| Term | What it means | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Harmless | The harm was never composed into the working | HC usage, from 1402 |
| Inert | A working never once observed to act | JM usage, insulting |
| Decorative | Harmless, and intended to be seen | HC 1.1, ch. 2 |
| Deferred | Held, unspent, for a person not yet born | HC 1.1, ch. 5 |
| Aspirational | Composed to miss | student slang, not HC |
| Nothing-fee | The reduced fee for a working with no harm | HC fee schedule, 1491 |
| Evidence of nothing | A measurement of an absence, and its record | Dr Tarn, in tutorial |

What it feels like to do it: like writing a letter and not sending it while a clerk watches and stamps the envelope. One speaks thirty-one syllables, one pays, one waits, and one goes home. The waiting is the whole of the practice. That is what the thing is, and it has cost me two leaves of an argument already written.

## 2. The Question, Stated Properly

A faculty is real if its subject is real. This seems safe. HC's subject is the harmless curse. If a thing does nothing then nothing follows from it, and if nothing follows from it there is nothing to examine.

*Tutor's hand, in red:* A room is real. Begin again. You defined the subject in §1 and redefined it here as "does nothing", the definition you were told to abandon. The difference between *the harm was never composed* and *nothing happened* is the whole Faculty, and you have dropped it in a line.

## 4. The Method I Proposed

I set out to test the question by experiment, argument having failed me twice in one term. The method is given as proposed, not as performed, the difference being the finding.

1. Compose a working harmless by construction, in the thirty-one syllable form given in the lecture of the 2nd of March, and no other.
2. Have it checked by a competent person, so that failure of the working cannot be confused with failure of the composing.
3. Select a target who consents in writing and is not a member of the University, so that no jurisdiction is entangled.
4. Fix the harm to be excluded in advance, in writing, and fix the measure by which its absence will be detected.
5. Cast the working once, at a stated hour, and record the hour.
6. Measure daily for twenty-one days.
7. Record nothing as a result, and not as a failure to obtain a result.
8. Report to the tutor at the end of the third week, whatever the record shows.

*Tutor's hand, in red, at step 4:* The right method, wrongly applied, which is the story of your year. You fixed your measure after you had cast, on the 11th, in pencil, and wrote it in ink here in the position of step 4. A measure fixed afterwards is a description. I do not ask you to remove the step. I ask you to notice the date.

## 5. The Experiment, In Full

*Tutor's hand, in red, at the head of this section:* This is the section. Do not shorten it in the fair copy. You have a habit of shortening the only part that happened.

The target was my aunt's neighbour in Low Town, Osric Fen, a fuller, who had quarrelled with her over the emptying of a gutter in February and had, by his own account, "said a thing he should not have said" about her in the yard of the Weeping Compass. She asked whether something could be done. I said I was in the second year of a degree in exactly that, and that I would do the nothing properly if he consented. She thought this a lawyer's answer. Fen consented on the 3rd of March, in writing on the back of a chandler's bill, on condition that none of it reached his employer's ear; I have honoured that until this page.

On the morning of the 4th of March I composed the working at a table in Reading Room 2, thirty-one syllables, in the lecture's form, with the substitution permitted in the harmless case: the harm-clause is *not composed*, and the syllables that would carry it are replaced by the four closing syllables of the courteous form, which take the working out of the world instead of into it. Marigold Thistlewick, then in her third year and the only person there who could, checked it syllable by syllable; she corrected the fifteenth, where I had written *weight* for *wait*, and signed the leaf. At the ninth bell I spoke it in the yard behind the Compass, Fen present and my aunt watching from her step, and nothing happened. I state that plainly because it is the whole of the event. Fen went back to his vat. My aunt asked whether it had started; I said it had finished, which is true of a harmless working.

I then measured the nothing for three weeks. At the same hour each morning, between the sixth and the seventh bell, I wrote down in a day-book those things which had not happened to Fen: on the 5th of March, no fall of any kind; on the 6th, no loss of custom; on the 7th, no quarrel at the vat; on the 8th, none at the yard gate, where a quarrel is more likely. Twice I recorded a harm that did occur — on the 12th, a strained shoulder; on the 19th, a fine of 3 clipped bells for a cart in the Tenterfields — and both fell outside the excluded list, and I have argued with myself for a fortnight about whether a working that excludes a list does nothing or does something small, and I have no answer. On the 11th I fixed the measure, so that step 4 is a description and not a plan, as Dr Tarn says.

On the 25th of March I closed the day-book, twenty-one days and one hundred and eleven entries, and took it to Dr Tarn. She read it through in front of me, a quarter of an hour, without looking up. Her verdict had two parts. The first: the record was honest, the casting competent, and the experiment the best thing I had done all year, which was not a compliment. The second: I had proved nothing, because a working composed to do nothing and a working that does nothing leave the same record, and my day-book could not tell them apart; and to tell them apart I would have to test a jinx, which would produce a harm, which would settle the jurisdiction question in JM's favour in four minutes, which is why nobody in HC has done it in ninety-four years. Then she asked whether I knew my aunt had paid. I said I had paid. She said that was the difficulty with the Faculty and not with me.

*Tutor's hand, in red, at the foot of the section:* The strained shoulder. You measured it, recorded it, and put it outside the list. If the working had excluded the shoulder and the shoulder happened, you would have falsified your own case in your own hand, and you would have been right to. Sit with the twelfth of March for a week.

## 7. The Objection, and the Reply

I set out the case against myself as strongly as I could, Dr Tarn requiring it and it being also what I think.

**The objection.** HC cannot be real, because nothing it does is measurable. A subject is real to the extent that it can be observed, weighed, counted and cited, and this Faculty has not in ninety-four years produced one observation two people could stand in front of and agree about. Its practitioners cast in yards and report that nothing took place; its journal has printed no issue since 1402, on a point of definition; its examinations are marked, as this one has been, on the approach. Where a Faculty has no measurement it has a manner of speaking instead, and its fees are a polite extraction, its roll a subscription. This is the JM case as Corbin Vale puts it, and it is stronger than anything in my §2.

**The reply, in which Dr Tarn concedes the measurement and denies the conclusion.** She says: you are right that we cannot weigh the nothing; right that in ninety-four years we have produced no observation; right that the JHM is a shelf of galleys, a sealed instrument and one long silence about a word; and right that a man of Fen's trade could have told you nothing happened without paying two bells to a University. I concede the measurement and deny the conclusion, and the reason is the twelfth of March. On the twelfth of March a harm occurred inside the period, at the address, at the hour, and fell outside the excluded list, and you could not in advance say why — and *that*, Toby, is a measurement of the working. A working that is real and does nothing and a working that is not real and does nothing part company the moment you ask which harms it excludes, because the first has a list and the second has whatever happened. You have been measuring the absence. Measure the list. The list is the working, and it took me fourteen years to get it, and I do not expect you to have it this year.

*Tutor's hand, in red:* The reply is mine and I have allowed it here because it is the only paragraph in this essay that answers the question set. Note that I have conceded the point you spent §4 and §5 proving, and that you have not noticed, and ask yourself what that tells you about §4 and §5.

## 8. The Document I Quote at Length

Dr Tarn lent me the galley of the *Journal of Harmless Maleficence*, which must not be folded and was recalled in the same week. I quote the longest thing I have taken from any source, because it is the only place where the Faculty's own people say out loud what happened.

> *Journal of Harmless Maleficence*, vol. viii (1402), galley 11, second page, "Practical Notes, Communicated":* — "A working done for a household in this city, in the month of February, was composed by me without a harm, and spoken in the presence of the householder and of his man, and the fee taken was the small fee, which is four clipped bells in the country and six in the city. The working being spoken, the householder desired to know whether it had taken effect, and I answered that it had, and he asked by what sign he might know it, and I had no sign to give him, and I perceived then that I had told him a falsehood, not in the working, which was sound, but in the word *taken*, which is a word from another Faculty and should not be used of a working that has no harm in it. I recommend to the Faculty that we cease to say *taken*, and say instead that a harmless working is *finished*, and that we cease to offer signs, and that we state upon the fee note that no sign will be offered, so that the householder cannot ask. This is the eighth time I have made this recommendation, and the fourth time upon the recommendation of Mr Sallow's classification, which puts us under HC and not under JM, where the householder's question does not arise."

*Tutor's hand, in red, in the margin of the passage as copied:* The word is *taken*. Three Faculties have died of a borrowed verb, and this one is not going to: the recommendation above is why the fee note now carries the sentence. Also, my colleague in AC charges six for a working the pollen does by itself. Do not quote that to him.

## 12. Conclusion, and the Form

The Faculty of Harmless Curses is real in the way that a form is real: it exists once it is filed. It is not real in the way that a harm is real, because there is no harm. Both statements are true. I do not think they can both be true, and I have run out of leaves, and the second class is for people who run out at the right place.

I did not attach Form 7B, *Declaration of Non-Interference with Time*, because I did not know there was one. I now know it is missing from every thesis in this library, and that Dr Tarn traces the general absence to the filing of the fourth reading room under R in 1487. §1 of Greaves, *Ninety-Nine Forms for Small Grievances*, says the same in a form and does not resolve it. I went to the counter on the 3rd of April to ask for the form. The counter is there. It was shut. The Under-Librarian opened a window and shut it again, and I have entered that in my day-book as the last measurement: the only one I have ever taken that two people could stand in front of and agree about. I am grateful to Dr Tarn for the loan of the journal, which I did not fold, and for the sentence about the list, which I shall not understand until the third year.

*Tutor's hand, in red:* Come to the tutorial. You did not come to the tutorial. See me on the 9th with one page and the receipt.
