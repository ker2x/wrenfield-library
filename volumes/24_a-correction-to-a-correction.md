## Abstract (as filed)

This document corrects *A Correction*, no. 41, filed by the present author touching the fourth column of the latency table of the *Advanced Guide to Jinxes*, fourth edition, and corrects the manner of that correction also. A correction, in this Faculty, is the instrument by which a published error is struck; it is not a review. The file lacks a completed Form 7B, *Declaration of Non-Interference with Time*, as does every thesis in this Library, and several documents that are not theses.

## 1. What a Correction Is

A **correction** is the instrument by which a published error is struck. It is a document of one or two paragraphs, filed with the Registry, entered in the Register, stamped, charged for, and printed in the next number of the Faculty's *Proceedings*. It does not alter the work it corrects — the first thing to be understood about it and the last thing anyone believes: the *Advanced Guide* still reads, in its fourth edition, *days*, and will read so in its fifth. What the correction alters is the Register. A reader who consults the Register learns that the heading is wrong; a reader who consults the book does not.

| Term | What it means in this Faculty |
|---|---|
| Correction | The instrument that strikes a published error; filed with the Registry, not the author |
| Standing | The right to be heard on a passage; the Faculty accepts the filing and defers it |
| The Register | The single list of corrections, kept at the Registry, one line each, struck or standing |
| Strike | To mark an entry struck; the earlier correction to a passage prevails over the later |
| State | A printing run. A correction corrects one state only; move the page and it is filed again |
| Correction of a correction | A second filing against a passage already corrected; charged at 3 bells |
| Form EA/11 | Application to correct a correction. Obtainable at the desk. Present with no filing |

Anyone may file; the Registry takes paper and fee, enters the line and defers the question of standing. What a correction cannot do is reach the work. It cannot expunge a page or restore a fee, has no retrospective effect and no remedial one, and costs a bell, or two, or three, or six. What it costs the person corrected the Faculty does not count, having no column for it. To correct is to do, at some expense, a thing that has almost no consequence except the one nobody records.

## 2. What a Correction Is Not

It is not a refutation. A refutation engages the argument and may run to a book; a correction engages a line. It is not a review: a review is the Faculty's opinion of a work, published under the reviewer's name, for which the reviewer is paid; a correction is a claim of fact about a page, for which the claimant pays. It is not a quarrel. A quarrel is conducted in letters and at the ordinary; filed with the Registry, it becomes a correction, which is a demotion.

Nor is it a defence. A person corrected may not answer in the Register, which holds one column for the work and none for its author, so that the *Advanced Guide* occupies four lines and Mr. Vale none. Nor is it a remedy: twice it has restored a fee, and the restorations were made by the author corrected and entered by the Faculty as gifts.

## 4. The Correction That Went Wrong: the Case of Mr. Tull

The author has been wrong twice at length: the first was the *Table of Onsets*. Mr. Ambrose Tull, D.Th., of the Faculty of Harmless Curses, published *A Table of Onsets; Being an Account of the Hour at Which Nine Decorated Curses Take Effect, with a Table of Recedings* in 1477, in an edition of three hundred copies, of which the Faculty bought sixty. The table filled pp. 60–61 as a double-page sheet, the onsets in the left column of the left page and the recedings in the right column of the right, so that an open book shows four columns and the middle two read as one. The author, working from a copy bound with the sheets reversed, compared the wrong columns and filed correction no. 3 on the 2nd of June 1478.

It was accepted within the fortnight and printed in the September number in the form the Faculty uses: *the table at pp. 60–61 of the work cited is inverted; the fourth column should be read against the fifth*. The work cannot answer that sentence and its author is not permitted to. Within the year the apothecaries of Low Town had ceased to order the book: a table that inverts its own onset is to be checked twice, and a checked table is to be replaced. The ordinary of Hilary 1479 read the correction aloud; the reading took eleven seconds. Mr. Tull's students fell from thirty-one to twelve; his lecture share fell nine marks and then seven.

He wrote nine letters between the June of 1478 and the Michaelmas of 1481 and the author replied three times. She filed correction no. 8, a correction of her own correction, on the 4th of October 1481, stating that she had misread the sheet and that Mr. Tull was right. The Register records it four lines below the first, where a reader may find it. It availed nothing: the entry of 1478 was not struck, no. 8 having come after a correction of the same passage. She paid, beyond the Registry's fee, nine marks and four bells to Mr. Tull himself before his death in 1490, which the Faculty entered as a gift. Her whole cost, with postage, was 18 marks, 14 bells and 3 clipped bells. She was thirty years old. The reading of the Register has never taken her longer than the eleven seconds it took that afternoon.

## 5. Why the Author Files Against Herself

Standing Order 12 provides that the earliest correction to a passage prevails, and that a correction filed after a correction of the same passage is struck from the Register. It is not that the first corrector wins; it is that the second loses twice, having paid the fee and lost the line. Of the author's forty, eleven correct passages already corrected by other hands, and three of those were entered before she had notice of the others. Had she waited upon p. 88, Mr. Vale's letter, written in the same month and posted in the same week, would have reached the Register first, her correction would have been struck, and she would then have been obliged to correct his correction, which the Register would have numbered 42.

Self-correction is therefore not vanity but the practice of a person who has read the Standing Order and is the only party in a position to file before herself. She corrects herself while the sheet is warm: three bells and no reputation. In thirty-one of the forty instances she has been right to; in the nine others the correction stood anyway, the Register not distinguishing between a correction that is right and one that is earlier. The Faculty has four times been asked for a rule, and has made a rate instead.

## 6. The Correction of 1496, Quoted in Full, with Its Headnote

*A Correction*, no. 41. As printed in the *Proceedings of the Faculty of Ethics, Appeals & Paperwork*, Michaelmas number, 1496, p. 214, under the standing headnote of the Register:

> *Corrections are printed as filed. The Faculty does not endorse, verify, or undertake to read. A correction is printed at the cost of the party filing, and the fee is not refundable upon acceptance, rejection, or error, the error of a correction being a matter for a further correction.*
>
> *A Correction*, no. 41. To the Faculty of Jinxcraft & Minor Maleficence, and to Mr. C. Vale, D.P.M. The latency table at p. 88 of the *Advanced Guide to Jinxes* (4th ed., 1494) is wrong in the fourth column, which is headed *days*. It should be headed *hours*. No other change is required; the figures in the column are correct as they stand, and the correction is one of heading only, and may be made in the next impression by the alteration of a single word, which the author of the *Guide* is at liberty to make or to decline to make, the Faculty having no power to compel an impression. The author of the present correction has checked the column three times against the plate and twice against the running text, and files with confidence. Filed the 9th of March 1496. — O. Marchetti-Bell, D.Th.

That is the whole of it, apart from the signature and the Registry's stamp. It corrects a heading of four letters.

## 8. Objection and Reply

Mr. Vale's first letter, received the 17th of March 1496, in his own hand and preserved in the file:

> *Dr. Marchetti-Bell. The table has five columns; the fourth is the days and the fifth is the hours, and the fifth is headed correctly and always has been. You have counted from the wrong side, which is a habit of yours, and done it in print under the Faculty's headnote, which is not a habit of yours but is now one. I have written to the Registry to say so and they have written back to say that the Register is a list. I am told I may not answer in the Register. Very well. I answer here, at the length the matter deserves, which is four lines, and I enclose the plate. — C. Vale.*

The author's answer, sent the 20th of March and filed with no. 41(b):

> *Mr. Vale. You are right about the columns and I am wrong. My copy of the Reserve is a made-up one, the sheets having been reversed by a previous owner; yours is the book, and I compared mine. I do not offer this as an excuse, the excuse being mine and the error being in print; I offer it as the account, the Faculty's practice being to prefer an account to an excuse. I have filed correction no. 41(b), which corrects my correction of three days ago and is four times its length, and paid the fee. I do not ask you to accept it. It is not the kind of thing you accept; it is the kind of thing you are sent. — O. M.-B.*

Mr. Vale's second reply, received the 2nd of April 1496, printed here entire, and without comment:

> *Dr. Marchetti-Bell. I have read the enclosure twice, which is once more than it will get from anyone else, and once more than I can afford this term. It is very long, it is very thorough, and it is addressed at the bottom to the wrong Faculty, which you will find is the third time. I have nothing to add to it and nothing to take from it. My fourth column is fine. I am keeping it. — C. Vale.*

## 9. The One Point on Which the Author Is Still Wrong: the Sunday of 1487

One item in the table at §7 the author has neither corrected nor explained. Correction no. 41 is dated the 9th of March 1496, a Sunday, and bears a Registry reception stamp of that date in violet ink. The Registry does not sit upon Sundays. The author has examined the register in three repositories and compared the stamp against seven others of that year, and can offer no account of it. She enters it as an error and does not correct it: there is no second version of the day.

She is, on this one point, wrong, and the cost of the error is 4 marks 3 bells, entered in her private account under *sundries, Registry* and never presented:

The Sunday reception of 6 March 1487 occurred. The Registry did sit that day to receive the fourth reading room, filed under R with its counter, its lamp, its clock and its contents; and the clock stopped at forty-one minutes past two and has not been wound since. The clerk was Mr. Anselm Pike, then Junior Clerk (Third Class, Unconfirmed); being unconfirmed, no charge could lie against him; and the Registry's remedy where no charge lies is promotion. The author's no. 9 — that the stamp is an error — is itself the error, and stands unstruck in the Register, thirteen years old, with her name against it.

What she has lost by it is small. What the Faculty has lost is the only instrument in its keeping that could tell it where a room is. She has not been told: the Clerk of Appeals knows and has asked that the telling cease; the Under-Librarian knows and does not discuss the Registry. And Form 7B, *Declaration of Non-Interference with Time*, which would have required the date and hour of the filing, is missing from every thesis in this Library and from the whole of the file of 1487, the only completed copy having gone into the room; so the one document that could have answered the question is filed with the object of it.

## 10. The Standing Offer, and the Fees

The author stands ready to correct this correction, upon Form EA/11, at the request of any party with standing, or without it. Such a document would be titled *A Correction to A Correction to A Correction*, no. 41(b), and is drafted; it has been drafted since 1499, in two states, the second being the one she proposes to file. She notes that no. 41(b) exists, corrects the fourth and fifth columns of a table on p. 89, was filed on the 20th of March 1496, and that no. 41(c) is drafted to correct its numbering, which the Register has entered under 41, there being no column for a letter.

The fees are six: 1 bell upon filing for the first correction and 2 bells thereafter; 3 bells upon a correction of a correction, the Faculty having made a rate instead of a rule; 6 bells where the work is out of print, which the Faculty has four times been asked to establish; a rebate of 4 bells where the correction corrects one issued by the Faculty itself, claimed twice and allowed once; no charge for Form 7B, required with every filing and present with none; and 3 marks 8 bells paid to date, recovered 4 bells and one apology. The fee is a charge for the Registry's labour in entering a line, collected whether the correction is accepted or struck.

## 11. Procedure, for the Reader Who Intends to File

Set out in the order the Faculty requires, and not in the order that works.

1. Establish the error. Read the passage twice, from two copies, and take the copy that is not made-up.
2. Establish the state. The correction will correct one state only, and the Faculty will not say which.
3. Establish the Faculty of the work, file with its desk, and pay at the Registry; jurisdiction has exceeded the fee since 1402.
4. Apply upon Form EA/11, claiming or deferring standing, the second being faster.
5. Complete Form 7B, *Declaration of Non-Interference with Time*. It will not be present. File without it, as every filing since 1487 has done.
6. Pay before filing, the standing being deferred; the money precedes the question.
7. File on the first six days. Sunday receptions have been known once, in 1487, and are not to be relied upon.
8. Require the second stamp where the correction corrects a correction; both stamps will be dated, the second dated the first.
9. Wait upon the Register. The entry prevails if it is the earliest to the passage, and is struck if it is not.
10. Where the passage is the author's own, file at once, before the sheet is dry; the author has done so thirty-nine times and thanked herself at §7.

## 12. The Correction of Shelf 44.9, and the Cost of Not Being Read

A volume is not corrected by being entered; it is corrected by being read. The Library's classifications and registers are read by almost nobody, which is the argument for keeping them short and the reason they are not. Shelf 44.9 holds a work of 1301 proving that the second volume of a certain history was never written, in a Library that lists it; the shelf is shorter than it should be by the width of that volume. A correction touching that work was filed as no. 27 in 1495, was accepted, and did not resolve. A correction filed and unread costs a bell and buys nothing, and the Faculty has never struck one for being useless.
